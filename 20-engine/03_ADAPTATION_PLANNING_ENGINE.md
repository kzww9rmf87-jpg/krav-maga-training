# ADAPTATION PLANNING ENGINE

Version 0.1

---

# Purpose

The Adaptation Planning Engine organizes training over time.

Its objective is not to schedule workouts.

Its objective is to schedule biological adaptations.

Every decision made by the engine answers one question.

"What adaptation should be prioritized now?"

---

# Core Principle

The athlete cannot maximize every adaptation simultaneously.

Training therefore consists of prioritizing specific adaptations while maintaining previously acquired capacities.

Adaptation priority changes over time.

Training continuity never does.

---

# Time Scales

The CAS organizes planning across five time scales.

## Session

One training session.

Focuses on one primary adaptation.

---

## Microcycle

Usually one week.

Defines the distribution of sessions.

Balances fatigue and recovery.

---

## Mesocycle

Usually four to eight weeks.

Develops one or two priority adaptations.

Maintains all others.

---

## Macrocycle

Several months.

Organizes major adaptation priorities.

Examples:

- Off-season
- In-season
- Return from injury
- General development
- Performance peak

---

## Lifetime

The largest planning horizon.

The purpose of CAS is lifelong progression.

Every macrocycle should improve long-term capability.

Never sacrifice future capacity for short-term results.

---

# Adaptation Priorities

Every adaptation belongs to one of three states.

## Priority

Primary development target.

Receives the largest training stimulus.

---

## Maintenance

Previously developed adaptation.

Receives the minimum effective dose.

---

## Recovery

Temporarily reduced.

Allows regeneration before future progression.

Adaptations continuously move between these three states.

---

# Progressive Overload

Adaptation requires progressive overload.

The overload may occur through:

- Load
- Volume
- Density
- Complexity
- Velocity
- Range of Motion
- Frequency

The engine selects the smallest overload capable of producing further adaptation.

More is never automatically better.

---

# Deload

Fatigue accumulates.

Adaptation requires recovery.

Every planning model must include periods of reduced stress.

A deload may reduce:

- Volume
- Intensity
- Frequency

The objective is not detraining.

The objective is renewed adaptation.

---

# Adaptation Rotation

The CAS avoids developing every capability at maximum simultaneously.

Instead, priorities rotate.

Example:

Mesocycle A

Priority:
Maximum Strength

Maintenance:
Power
Conditioning
Movement

Mesocycle B

Priority:
Power

Maintenance:
Strength
Movement
Robustness

Mesocycle C

Priority:
Functional Hypertrophy

Maintenance:
Strength
Power

The engine therefore preserves previous gains while developing new ones.

---

# Seasonal Planning

Planning depends on the athlete's context.

Examples

Combat sport season

↓

Maintain strength

Increase specific skill

Manage fatigue

---

Off-season

↓

Increase strength

Increase hypertrophy

Develop aerobic base

---

Return from injury

↓

Restore movement

Restore robustness

Rebuild strength

Delay high-intensity work

The planning engine adapts automatically.

---

# Constraints

Planning must always respect the following principles.

Recovery precedes overload.

Power requires freshness.

Strength tolerates moderate fatigue.

Movement is trained year-round.

Recovery is programmed.

Not assumed.

No adaptation is allowed to disappear completely.

---

# Decision Hierarchy

When conflicts appear, the engine follows this order.

1. Health

2. Recovery

3. Movement Quality

4. Primary Adaptation

5. Secondary Adaptations

6. Performance Metrics

Performance never overrides health.

---

# Success Criteria

A planning model succeeds when:

Adaptations improve.

Fatigue remains manageable.

Training remains sustainable.

The athlete arrives healthy at the next cycle.

Long-term capability continuously increases.

---

# Final Principle

Training is not a sequence of workouts.

Training is the intelligent organization of biological adaptations across time.

Time is therefore one of the primary training variables.

The Adaptation Planning Engine exists to manage it.
