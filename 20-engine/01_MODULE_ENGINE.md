# MODULE ENGINE

Version 0.1

---

# Purpose

The Module Engine is the core of the Combat Athlete System.

Its purpose is to transform physiological objectives into structured training sessions.

The Module Engine never starts from exercises.

It always starts from the desired adaptation.

Exercises are implementation details.

Adaptations are the objective.

Every training session generated by CAS is the result of assembling Capability Modules according to physiological rules, recovery constraints and long-term priorities.

---

# Core Philosophy

The Module Engine follows one immutable principle:

> Build adaptations, not workouts.

A workout is only a temporary arrangement of modules.

A capability is a long-term biological adaptation.

The engine therefore optimizes adaptation rather than fatigue.

---

# Core Concepts

## Adaptation Domain

An Adaptation Domain represents a fundamental physical quality that can be developed through training.

Current domains are:

- Maximum Strength
- Power
- Functional Hypertrophy
- Conditioning
- Robustness
- Movement
- Recovery
- Specific Skill

Every module belongs primarily to one Adaptation Domain.

An Adaptation Domain is not a Capability Module. A domain describes a
physiological finality — what the body is being asked to become. It is
not something the engine assembles into a session directly.

---

## Capability Module

A Capability Module is the smallest functional training unit of CAS.

A Capability Module is not an Adaptation Domain. Where a domain describes
a finality, a module describes an operational, reusable building block —
the thing the Module Engine actually assembles into sessions. A module
pursues exactly one Adaptation Domain as its primary adaptation, but it
is deliberately more granular than the domain itself: several distinct
modules may serve the same domain (Grip and Robustness both primarily
serve Robustness; Preparation and Movement both primarily serve
Movement), because they represent different operational moments or
mechanisms, not different finalities.

A Capability Module is also not a Capability, despite the shared word. A
Capability is a real action a human performs, expressed as a verb — carry,
push, pull, run, get up, maintain a grip, resist a perturbation, transmit
force. A Capability Module is the training block that develops the
physical quality (or qualities) that action requires. Capabilities are
the "why" the Manifesto describes ("we don't train the pectorals, we
develop a pushing capacity"); Capability Modules are the "how" the engine
assembles. This distinction is acknowledged doctrine but not yet a
modeled layer — no Capability type or field exists in the engine today.
It is recorded here only so the two terms are never read as synonyms.

A module exists to improve exactly one primary adaptation — required,
never more than one.

A module may also list zero to several secondary adaptations, but these
are never its purpose. The Decision Engine reasons primarily on the
primary adaptation; secondary adaptations exist for documentation and
future analysis, to better represent physiological reality without
weakening the primary/secondary distinction that keeps module selection
simple.

A module is independent from any particular exercise.

Different exercises may implement the same module.

Likewise, one exercise may serve different modules depending on execution parameters.

---

## Canonical Capability Module Catalog

The Module Engine assembles sessions from a fixed catalog of Capability
Modules, not from Adaptation Domains directly and not from ad hoc groupings
invented per session. This catalog follows the default assembly order
defined below (see Session Assembly).

- Preparation — primary: Movement — secondary: none
- Movement — primary: Movement — secondary: none
- Power — primary: Power — secondary: none
- Strength — primary: Maximum Strength — secondary: Power, Robustness
- Functional Hypertrophy — primary: Functional Hypertrophy — secondary: none
- Robustness — primary: Robustness — secondary: none
- Grip — primary: Robustness — secondary: none
- Core — primary: Movement — secondary: none
- Conditioning — primary: Conditioning — secondary: none
- Recovery — primary: Recovery — secondary: none

Strength is the only module with secondary adaptations listed, because
`10-science/01_THE_PHYSICAL_MODEL.md` explicitly states them: strength
development improves power potential and joint stability. Every other
secondary is left empty rather than inferred — Scientific Integrity
forbids asserting a physiological relationship the foundation documents
do not already state. Secondaries should be added only when a specific
document or analysis justifies them, not by default.

Specific Skill is deliberately absent from this catalog. CAS is a
physical preparation engine; specific skill is developed by practicing
the target discipline itself, not by a Capability Module. See
`10-science/01_THE_PHYSICAL_MODEL.md`: "La préparation physique ne
remplace jamais la pratique."

This catalog is stable business data, not a per-session invention.
Sessions are temporary assemblies of these modules; the modules
themselves are not derived from sessions.

The catalog may grow as new operational needs are identified, but any
addition must satisfy the same rules as the modules above: one primary
adaptation, a distinct justification for existing separately from the
domain it serves, and reusability across sessions and cycles.

## Training Session

A training session is an ordered collection of Capability Modules.

Its objective is not to maximize fatigue.

Its objective is to maximize useful adaptation while respecting recovery capacity.

---

## Training Cycle

A cycle is an organized sequence of sessions designed to prioritize specific adaptations over time.

Cycles define priorities.

Modules execute them.

# Module Rules

The following rules are absolute.

Rule 1

Every module has one primary adaptation.

Never two.

---

Rule 2

Every module must justify its existence.

If a module does not create a distinct adaptation, it should not exist.

---

Rule 3

A module must remain simple.

One physiological objective.

One clear progression.

One measurable outcome.

---

Rule 4

Modules never compete with their own objective.

If fatigue prevents the desired adaptation, the module must be modified or postponed.

---

Rule 5

Modules are reusable.

They are independent from sessions.

The same module may appear in different sessions and different cycles.

---

# Session Assembly

Training sessions are assembled according to physiological priorities.

The default order is:

Preparation

↓

Movement

↓

Power

↓

Strength

↓

Functional Hypertrophy

↓

Robustness

↓

Grip

↓

Core

↓

Conditioning

↓

Recovery

This order may change only when a scientific or practical justification exists.

---

# Fatigue Management

Fatigue is a resource.

Not an objective.

Each module contributes to four independent fatigue dimensions.

- Neural Fatigue
- Muscular Fatigue
- Connective Tissue Stress
- Metabolic Fatigue

The engine evaluates cumulative fatigue before assembling a session.

If the recovery cost exceeds the athlete's available capacity, the session must be modified.

Adaptation always takes priority over completion.

---

# Progression Logic

Each Adaptation Domain follows its own progression model.

Maximum Strength

Increase force production while maintaining technical quality.

Power

Increase movement velocity while preserving maximal intent.

Functional Hypertrophy

Increase useful muscle mass while preserving movement quality.

Conditioning

Increase work capacity without compromising strength development.

Movement

Increase usable range of motion and motor efficiency.

Robustness

Increase tolerance to repeated mechanical stress.

Recovery

Improve the ability to tolerate and recover from future workloads.

Specific Skill

Increase transfer toward the target discipline.

---

# Constraints

The Module Engine must always respect the following constraints.

Power is never trained under significant neural fatigue.

Technique always has priority over load.

Recovery capacity limits training volume.

No session exists to create fatigue.

Every session exists to create adaptation.

---

# Evolution

The Module Engine is designed to evolve.

New exercises may be added.

New sports may be supported.

New scientific evidence may modify implementation.

However, the architecture remains unchanged.

Adaptation Domains generate Capability Modules.

Capability Modules generate Sessions.

Sessions generate Cycles.

Cycles generate long-term adaptation.

---

# Definition of Success

The Module Engine succeeds when it consistently produces training sessions that maximize useful adaptation while minimizing unnecessary fatigue.

The quality of the engine is measured by the athlete's long-term progression.

Never by the complexity of the system.
