# ENDURANCE

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Endurance model defines how athletes sustain performance over time.

For combat athletes,

endurance is not the ability to move continuously for long durations.

It is the ability to repeatedly produce high-quality actions,

recover rapidly,

and maintain technical performance throughout an entire contest.

---

# Definition

Endurance is the ability to resist fatigue while maintaining performance.

Performance includes:

Force

Power

Speed

Technique

Decision Making

Movement Quality

Endurance preserves these qualities under fatigue.

---

# Philosophy

Combat endurance is not passive.

It is the ability to repeatedly express explosive actions while minimizing performance decline.

The objective is not simply to last longer.

The objective is to remain dangerous until the final second.

# Aerobic Endurance

Definition

Ability to sustain low to moderate intensity work over long durations.

Primary Adaptations

Cardiovascular efficiency.

Capillary density.

Mitochondrial adaptations.

Combat Transfer

Recovery between exchanges.

Recovery between rounds.

Recovery between training sessions.

Aerobic capacity supports every other quality.

---

# Anaerobic Endurance

Definition

Ability to repeatedly sustain high-intensity efforts.

Examples

Repeated combinations.

Explosive takedowns.

Scrambles.

Heavy Bag Intervals.

Combat Transfer

High-intensity exchanges.

Repeated attacks.

Late-round explosiveness.

---

# Strength Endurance

Definition

Ability to repeatedly produce force.

Examples

Repeated clinch exchanges.

Repeated pulling.

Repeated pushing.

Ground control.

Strength endurance preserves force production.

---

# Power Endurance

Definition

Ability to repeatedly express high levels of power.

Examples

Punch combinations.

Repeated explosive kicks.

Medicine Ball Throws.

Reactive drills.

Power should remain explosive despite fatigue.

---

# Speed Endurance

Definition

Ability to maintain movement speed throughout repeated efforts.

Examples

Repeated footwork.

Repeated striking.

Counter-attacks.

# Movement Economy

Efficient movement reduces energy expenditure.

Examples

Efficient footwork.

Relaxed striking.

Technical precision.

Economy improves endurance without changing physiology.

---

# Fatigue Resistance

Endurance delays performance decline.

It does not eliminate fatigue.

Programming should aim to preserve quality,

not simply tolerate exhaustion.

---
