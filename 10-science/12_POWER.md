# POWER

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Power model defines how force and velocity combine to produce explosive athletic performance.

Power is one of the most decisive physical qualities in combat sports.

It determines how rapidly force can be expressed during striking, throwing, jumping, accelerating and changing direction.

The objective of CAS is not simply to increase power,

but to develop power that transfers effectively to combat performance.

---

# Definition

Power is the ability to produce force rapidly.

Power is the product of:

Force

×

Velocity

High force without speed is not power.

High speed without force is not power.

Optimal power combines both.

---

# Philosophy

Combat is explosive.

The athlete rarely has several seconds to produce force.

Power determines who reaches maximal force first.

CAS therefore develops explosive force production,

not merely maximal force.

# Rate of Force Development

Rate of Force Development (RFD) describes how quickly force is produced.

For combat athletes,

RFD is often more important than maximal force.

Fast force wins exchanges.

---

# Force-Velocity Relationship

Power exists across the entire force-velocity spectrum.

Heavy Loads

↓

High Force

↓

Low Velocity

Moderate Loads

↓

Optimal Power

↓

Moderate Velocity

Light Loads

↓

High Velocity

↓

Lower Force

Training should target multiple regions of the spectrum.

# Fatigue

Power declines rapidly with fatigue.

Power training should therefore occur:

Early in the session.

After warm-up.

Before high-volume work.

Quality takes priority over quantity.

Movement quality.
