# STABILITY

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Stability model defines how the body controls posture, transfers force and maintains movement quality under changing conditions.

Stability is essential for efficient force production, injury prevention and technical performance.

The Combat Athlete System develops stability to improve movement efficiency rather than simply balance.

---

# Definition

Stability is the ability to maintain or regain an optimal body position while producing or resisting force.

Stability depends on:

Motor Control

↓

Strength

↓

Mobility

↓

Sensory Information

↓

Coordination

↓

Movement Strategy

It is an active process.

---

# Philosophy

Stability is not immobility.

True stability allows efficient movement.

The objective is not to eliminate motion,

but to control it.

A stable athlete can express force without unnecessary energy loss.

---

# Biological Foundations

Stability depends upon:

Neural Control

Muscular Coordination

Joint Integrity

Proprioception

Vestibular Function

Visual Information

Movement Experience

No single system is responsible for stability.

---

# Stability vs Balance

CAS distinguishes:

Balance

↓

Maintaining the body's center of mass over its base of support.

Stability

↓

Maintaining efficient control while moving or resisting external forces.

Combat performance depends primarily on stability.

---

# Static Stability

Definition

Maintaining position without movement.

Examples

Single-leg stance.

Plank.

Isometric holds.

Static stability provides a foundation,

not the final objective.

---

# Dynamic Stability

Definition

Maintaining control during movement.

Examples

Landing.

Cutting.

Kicking.

Throwing.

Punching.

Dynamic stability is more relevant to combat.

---

# Core Stability

The trunk transfers force between the upper and lower body.

Effective core stability improves:

Punching power.

Kicking power.

Throwing.

Change of direction.

Ground control.

The objective is force transmission,

not isolated abdominal strength.

---

# Shoulder Stability

Critical for:

Punching.

Frames.

Clinch.

Posting.

Ground support.

Shoulder stability combines mobility and strength.

---

# Hip Stability

Supports:

Footwork.

Level changes.

Kicking.

Running.

Explosive transitions.

Hip stability influences lower-body force production.

---

# Knee Stability

Important for:

Landing.

Acceleration.

Deceleration.

Direction changes.

Knee stability depends on the entire kinetic chain.

---

# Ankle Stability

Supports:

Balance.

Footwork.

Explosive movement.

Landing.

Direction changes.

Ankle stability begins with foot control.

---

# Rotational Stability

The ability to resist unwanted rotation while producing intentional rotation.

Examples

Punching.

Throws.

Clinch.

Anti-rotation drills.

Rotational stability improves energy transfer.

---

# Reactive Stability

Definition

The ability to rapidly recover control following an unexpected perturbation.

Examples

Opponent contact.

Off-balanced positions.

Landing after impact.

Scrambling.

Reactive stability is essential in combat.
