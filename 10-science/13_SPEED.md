# SPEED

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Speed model defines how athletes produce rapid movement in combat situations.

Speed is more than movement velocity.

It is the ability to perceive, decide and execute actions in the shortest possible time.

The Combat Athlete System develops speed that transfers directly to combat performance.

---

# Definition

Speed is the ability to perform a movement or sequence of movements in minimal time.

Speed depends on:

Reaction

Decision Making

Neural Activation

Movement Coordination

Force Production

Technique

No single component defines speed.

---

# Philosophy

Speed is context-dependent.

The fastest athlete is not necessarily the athlete who moves the fastest in isolation.

The fastest athlete is the one who produces the correct action first.

Combat speed is intelligent speed.

---

# Biological Foundations

Speed depends upon:

Neural Efficiency

Motor Unit Recruitment

Rate of Force Development

Intermuscular Coordination

Muscle Fiber Characteristics

Reaction Processing

# Types of Speed

CAS distinguishes several forms of speed.

Reaction Speed

Movement Speed

Execution Speed

Acceleration

Change of Direction Speed

Footwork Speed

Strike Speed

Kick Speed

Decision Speed

Combat Speed

Each should be trained differently.

---

# Reaction Speed

Definition

Time between stimulus and movement initiation.

Examples

Visual reaction.

Auditory reaction.

Tactile reaction.

Combat Transfer

Counter-attacks.

Defense.

Shot defense.

Distance management.

---

# Movement Speed

Definition

Maximum velocity of body movement.

Examples

Sprint.

Hand movement.

Leg movement.

Body displacement.

Movement speed depends on technique as much as physiology.

---

# Execution Speed

Definition

Time required to complete an entire action.

Examples

Punch.

Kick.

Technical stand-up.

Sprawl.

Execution speed combines reaction and movement.

---

# Acceleration

Definition

Ability to rapidly increase velocity.

Combat athletes rarely sprint over long distances.

Acceleration is therefore more valuable than maximum running speed.

---

# Change of Direction Speed

Definition

Ability to rapidly decelerate,

change direction,

and accelerate again.

Examples

Lateral movement.

Ring movement.

Defensive footwork.

Reactive repositioning.

---

# Footwork Speed

Efficient movement of the lower body.

Includes:

Step initiation.

Weight transfer.

Balance.

Recovery.

Footwork speed determines positioning.

Position determines opportunity.

---

# Strike Speed

Includes:

Punch initiation.

Punch acceleration.

Recovery after impact.

Combination speed.

Guard recovery.

The complete striking cycle matters.

Not only hand velocity.

---

# Kick Speed

Includes:

Hip rotation.

Leg acceleration.

Balance.

Recovery.

Return to fighting stance.

Fast recovery is as important as fast execution.

---

# Decision Speed

Speed is cognitive as well as physical.

The athlete must:

Perceive.

Interpret.

Decide.

Execute.

Decision speed often separates elite competitors.

---

# Combat Speed

Combat speed integrates:

Reaction.

Decision.

Acceleration.

Execution.

Recovery.

Positioning.

Technique.

Combat speed is an emergent quality.

---

# Limiting Factors

Speed may be limited by:

Insufficient strength.

Poor coordination.

Excessive fatigue.

Poor mobility.

Technical inefficiency.

Fear of movement.

Slow perception.

The limiting factor should be identified before intervention.

---

# Programming Variables

Speed development depends on:

Freshness.

Maximum intent.

Low fatigue.

High movement quality.

Appropriate rest.

Specificity.

Quality always precedes volume.

---

# Fatigue

Speed declines rapidly with fatigue.

Speed training should therefore occur:

Early in the session.

After the warm-up.

Before conditioning.

The objective is maximal execution quality.

---

# Recovery

High-quality speed work requires:

Neuromuscular freshness.

Adequate sleep.

Minimal accumulated fatigue.

Optimal concentration.
