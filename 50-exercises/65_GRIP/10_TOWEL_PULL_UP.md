# Purpose

The Towel Pull-Up develops vertical pulling strength while imposing a high demand on support grip, open-hand grip and forearm endurance.

Within CAS, it is used when the athlete must improve the ability to maintain a strong hand connection while producing upper-body pulling force.

The exercise is particularly relevant for combat athletes because it combines:

- sustained gripping,
- elbow flexion,
- scapular control,
- shoulder stability,
- and whole-body tension.

It must not be selected merely as a harder pull-up variation.

Its value depends on whether the athlete needs grip-integrated pulling capacity and possesses sufficient shoulder, elbow and hand readiness.

# Movement Description

A towel is draped securely over a stable pull-up bar.

The athlete grips one end of the towel with each hand and begins from a controlled hanging position.

From this position, the athlete pulls the body upward by driving the elbows down and back while maintaining a strong grip and stable shoulder position.

The repetition is completed when the upper chest or chin reaches the intended height without loss of body control.

The athlete then lowers under control to the prescribed bottom position.

---

# Starting Position

The athlete should begin with:

- one towel securely positioned over a stable pull-up bar,
- one towel end held in each hand,
- hands at approximately shoulder width or slightly narrower,
- thumbs wrapped when possible,
- wrists controlled,
- elbows extended or slightly unlocked,
- shoulders actively stabilized,
- ribs controlled,
- pelvis neutral,
- legs quiet,
- and the body aligned beneath the grip.

The towel must be long enough to permit a secure hold without excessive bunching.

---

# Execution

1. Secure the towel over the pull-up bar.
2. Grip one end firmly with each hand.
3. Establish an active hang by lightly depressing and stabilizing the shoulder blades.
4. Brace the trunk and prevent excessive swinging.
5. Initiate the pull by driving the elbows downward.
6. Continue pulling while maintaining towel tension and wrist control.
7. Reach the prescribed top position without craning the neck.
8. Pause briefly if required.
9. Lower under control.
10. Return to the prescribed bottom position while maintaining shoulder integrity.
11. Repeat only while grip quality and pulling mechanics remain acceptable.

---

# Key Technical Cues

- Crush the towel.
- Keep the wrists strong.
- Pull the elbows toward the ribs.
- Keep the chest tall.
- Do not shrug into the ears.
- Keep the ribs down.
- Minimize swinging.
- Control the descent.
- Stop before the hands begin to slip uncontrollably.

# Grip Demand

The Towel Pull-Up creates a substantially greater grip demand than a conventional bar pull-up.

The towel increases difficulty by:

- reducing the firmness of the gripping surface,
- increasing deformation under load,
- requiring continuous finger flexor activity,
- placing the hand in a more open position,
- and increasing reliance on friction.

The exercise primarily trains:

- support grip,
- open-hand grip,
- and grip endurance.

It is not a pure crushing-grip exercise.

---

# Technical Complexity

Moderate

The pulling pattern is familiar to athletes who can already perform pull-ups.

However, the towel introduces:

- greater grip instability,
- reduced mechanical security,
- higher forearm demand,
- and increased risk of technical breakdown through swinging or slipping.

The exercise should therefore be introduced only after the athlete demonstrates competence in conventional hanging and pulling variations.

---

# Prerequisites

The athlete should ideally demonstrate:

- pain-free dead hanging,
- pain-free conventional pull-ups or assisted pull-ups,
- adequate shoulder-flexion tolerance,
- stable scapular control,
- sufficient elbow-tendon tolerance,
- secure towel gripping,
- and the ability to control the trunk during vertical pulling.

The athlete should not begin with maximal-effort repetitions.

---

# Eligibility Criteria

The Towel Pull-Up is eligible when:

- grip-integrated pulling strength is a target,
- the athlete can perform the required vertical-pull pattern safely,
- the towel and bar are secure,
- hand and elbow tissues are healthy,
- the athlete can maintain shoulder control,
- and local grip fatigue will not compromise higher-priority training.

---

# Ineligibility Criteria

The exercise is not eligible when the athlete presents:

- acute finger pain,
- thumb pain,
- wrist pain,
- medial or lateral elbow pain,
- shoulder pain during hanging,
- inability to maintain an active shoulder position,
- insufficient conventional pull-up strength,
- recent hand injury,
- unsafe equipment setup,
- or severe residual grip fatigue.

---

# Relative Contraindications

Use caution when the athlete has:

- previous pulley injury,
- chronic elbow tendinopathy,
- shoulder instability,
- reduced overhead tolerance,
- skin tears,
- callus damage,
- or high weekly grappling volume.

In these cases, the engine should reduce volume, use assistance or select a lower-stress alternative.

---

# Absolute Contraindications

Do not prescribe the exercise when there is:

- acute hand or finger injury,
- acute wrist injury,
- acute elbow injury,
- acute shoulder injury,
- neurological symptoms in the upper limb,
- inability to grip the towel securely,
- or an unstable pull-up station.

---

# Common Errors

## Passive Shoulder Position

The athlete hangs entirely on passive structures without scapular control.

Risk:

- shoulder irritation,
- reduced pulling efficiency,
- loss of positional stability.

Correction:

- establish an active hang before initiating the pull.

## Excessive Swinging

The athlete uses momentum to complete repetitions.

Risk:

- reduced target adaptation,
- uncontrolled towel loading,
- increased slipping risk.

Correction:

- brace the trunk,
- reduce repetition target,
- use assistance if necessary.

## Wrist Collapse

The wrists flex or deviate excessively under load.

Risk:

- reduced force transfer,
- increased forearm and wrist stress.

Correction:

- maintain a strong neutral wrist,
- reduce difficulty,
- use a thicker or more manageable towel setup.

## Elbows Flare Excessively

The elbows move away from the body without purpose.

Risk:

- reduced pulling efficiency,
- increased shoulder stress.

Correction:

- drive the elbows down toward the ribs.

## Neck Reaching

The athlete extends the neck to bring the chin over the hands.

Risk:

- false range completion,
- cervical discomfort.

Correction:

- keep the head neutral and pull the torso upward.

## Uncontrolled Descent

The athlete drops rapidly into the bottom position.

Risk:

- abrupt shoulder loading,
- loss of scapular control,
- excessive tendon stress.

Correction:

- lower under control.

## Grip Failure Before Technical Failure

The hands begin to slide while the athlete continues attempting repetitions.

Risk:

- sudden fall,
- skin injury,
- tendon overload.

Correction:

- terminate the set at the first clear loss of secure grip.

---

# Coaching Priorities

1. Verify equipment security.
2. Establish an active hang.
3. Maintain a strong towel grip.
4. Keep the wrists controlled.
5. Initiate with the back rather than the hands alone.
6. Minimize swing.
7. Maintain full-body tension.
8. Stop before uncontrolled slipping.
9. Protect the shoulders during the bottom position.
10. Preserve repetition quality over repetition count.

---

# Prescription Variables

## Repetitions

Typical range:

- 1 to 6 repetitions for strength,
- 4 to 8 repetitions for strength endurance,
- higher repetitions only for well-adapted athletes.

## Sets

Typical range:

- 3 to 5 sets.

## Tempo

Typical options:

- controlled ascent,
- brief top pause,
- 2 to 4 second eccentric,
- no uncontrolled drop.

## Rest

Typical range:

- 2 to 4 minutes for strength,
- 90 to 180 seconds for strength endurance.

## Intensity

Intensity is determined by:

- bodyweight,
- assistance level,
- towel thickness,
- towel material,
- grip height,
- range of motion,
- repetition count,
- and proximity to grip failure.

---

# Strength Prescription

Typical prescription:

- 3 to 5 sets,
- 2 to 5 repetitions,
- 2 to 4 minutes rest,
- 1 to 3 repetitions in reserve,
- complete grip control.

Weighted towel pull-ups are reserved for advanced athletes with established tissue tolerance.

---

# Strength-Endurance Prescription

Typical prescription:

- 3 to 4 sets,
- 4 to 8 repetitions,
- 90 to 180 seconds rest,
- submaximal grip fatigue,
- no uncontrolled slipping.

---

# Isometric Variation

The athlete may hold:

- the top position,
- the mid-range position,
- or a flexed-arm position.

Typical prescription:

- 5 to 20 seconds,
- 2 to 4 sets,
- full grip security,
- stable shoulder position.

Isometric variations increase local grip demand and should be progressed conservatively.

---

# Assisted Variation

Assistance may be provided through:

- resistance bands,
- foot support,
- partner support,
- or an assisted pull-up machine when the towel setup permits.

The assistance level should allow:

- secure gripping,
- controlled pulling,
- stable shoulders,
- and technically consistent repetitions.

---

# Progressions

Progressions include:

1. longer towel exposure,
2. reduced assistance,
3. greater repetition count,
4. slower eccentric,
5. longer isometric pauses,
6. thicker towel,
7. reduced towel friction,
8. uneven grip height,
9. unilateral emphasis,
10. external loading.

External load should be the final progression, not the first.

---

# Regressions

Regressions include:

- towel-assisted dead hang,
- foot-supported towel hang,
- band-assisted towel pull-up,
- partial-range towel pull-up,
- eccentric-only towel pull-up,
- conventional pull-up,
- neutral-grip pull-up,
- lat pulldown with towel attachment.

---

# Recommended Progression Sequence

1. Conventional active hang.
2. Conventional pull-up.
3. Supported towel hang.
4. Full towel hang.
5. Assisted towel pull-up.
6. Eccentric towel pull-up.
7. Full towel pull-up.
8. Paused towel pull-up.
9. Thick-towel pull-up.
10. Weighted or asymmetric towel pull-up.

# Fatigue Profile

## Systemic Fatigue

Moderate

The exercise loads a large portion of the upper body and may create meaningful whole-body effort when performed near failure.

## Local Fatigue

High

Primary local fatigue occurs in:

- finger flexors,
- forearms,
- biceps,
- brachialis,
- brachioradialis,
- and upper-back musculature.

## Neurological Fatigue

Moderate

Low-repetition high-effort sets can create substantial neural demand.

## Connective-Tissue Stress

Moderate to High

Stress may accumulate in:

- finger flexor tendons,
- elbow flexor tendons,
- wrist structures,
- and shoulder tissues.

---

# Recovery Cost

Recovery cost is typically moderate.

It increases with:

- high repetition volume,
- slow eccentrics,
- long isometric holds,
- thick towels,
- external load,
- repeated sets near failure,
- and concurrent grappling volume.

Grip soreness may persist even when general fatigue is low.

---

# Session Placement

The exercise is usually placed:

- after explosive upper-body work,
- after primary barbell strength work when grip preservation matters,
- as a secondary upper-body strength exercise,
- or as a dedicated grip-strength movement.

It should not normally precede:

- Olympic-lifting derivatives,
- heavy deadlifts,
- maximal pulling work,
- intensive grappling,
- or technical sessions requiring fresh hands.

When towel pulling is the primary objective, it may be placed earlier after warm-up.

---

# Weekly Placement

Typical frequency:

- 1 to 2 times per week.

A third exposure may be appropriate only when:

- volume is low,
- the athlete has strong tissue tolerance,
- grappling volume is limited,
- and no hand or elbow symptoms are present.

---

# Interaction With Other Training

## Deadlifts

Towel pull-ups can reduce grip readiness for heavy deadlifts.

Separate high-demand sessions when grip is a limiting factor.

## Rows and Pull-Ups

The exercise overlaps strongly with vertical pulling and elbow-flexor work.

Avoid redundant high-volume pulling.

## Loaded Carries

Carries and towel pull-ups both stress support grip.

Combined volume must be controlled.

## Grappling

Grappling may already provide substantial towel-like gripping stress, especially in gi-based disciplines.

The engine must include sport practice in total grip volume.

## Striking

Severe forearm fatigue may impair wrist comfort, hand relaxation and striking quality.

Avoid high-fatigue towel work before priority striking sessions.

---

# Combat Transfer

The Towel Pull-Up supports combat performance by improving:

- sustained hand connection,
- upper-body pulling force,
- forearm endurance,
- wrist stability,
- scapular control,
- and resistance to grip opening.

Its transfer is strongest for athletes who must pull while maintaining a deformable or unstable grip.

Technical grip-fighting skill remains sport-specific.

---

# Sport-Specific Relevance

## Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

High relevance for:

- gi gripping,
- sleeve and collar retention,
- sustained pulling,
- finger-flexor endurance.

Volume must be reduced when gi training is already high.

## Judo

High relevance for:

- lapel and sleeve gripping,
- pulling while maintaining open-hand tension,
- grip retention.

## Wrestling

Moderate relevance for:

- wrist control,
- tie retention,
- pulling under hand fatigue.

## MMA

Moderate relevance for:

- clinch control,
- wrist control,
- pulling strength under grip fatigue.

## Krav Maga

Moderate relevance for:

- limb control,
- clothing control,
- weapon-retention mechanics,
- upper-body pulling strength.

## Boxing and Kickboxing

Low to Moderate relevance.

The exercise can support general pulling and wrist robustness but should not compromise hand freshness for striking.

# Substitution Logic

## Substitute Conventional Pull-Up When

- grip demand is not the target,
- the athlete cannot hold the towel safely,
- pulling strength must be trained with less local hand fatigue,
- or elbow and finger tissues need reduced stress.

## Substitute Towel Hang When

- grip capacity is the target but full pulling strength is insufficient,
- the athlete needs a lower-complexity entry point,
- or vertical pulling volume is already high.

## Substitute Lat Pulldown With Towel When

- bodyweight pulling is too difficult,
- load must be adjusted precisely,
- or the athlete needs controlled grip-integrated pulling.

## Substitute Farmer Carry When

- support grip is required without overhead shoulder loading,
- vertical pulling is contraindicated,
- or whole-body carrying strength is also desired.

## Substitute Plate Pinch When

- pinch grip is the actual target,
- thumb opposition is underdeveloped,
- or vertical pulling volume must be minimized.

# Progression Criteria

The athlete may progress when:

- all repetitions are completed without slipping,
- the wrists remain controlled,
- the shoulders remain stable,
- the trunk remains quiet,
- no pain occurs,
- the athlete retains at least 1 to 2 repetitions in reserve,
- and recovery is complete before the next grip-intensive session.

---

# Regression Criteria

The exercise should be regressed when:

- grip fails before the intended muscular target,
- the athlete swings excessively,
- shoulder position becomes passive,
- wrist alignment deteriorates,
- the athlete cannot control the eccentric,
- pain appears,
- or local fatigue disrupts subsequent sport practice.

---

# Readiness Screen

Before prescription, assess:

- finger pain,
- thumb pain,
- wrist pain,
- elbow pain,
- shoulder pain,
- skin integrity,
- previous grip-intensive training,
- current grappling volume,
- and ability to hang actively.

The athlete should not begin if the towel cannot be held securely.

---

# Safety Rules

- Use a strong towel without visible damage.
- Place the towel over a stable pull-up bar.
- Confirm that the bar is secure.
- Keep the landing area clear.
- Do not continue after grip security is lost.
- Avoid maximal effort with irritated finger or elbow tendons.
- Do not use an unstable door-mounted setup.
- Progress towel thickness gradually.
- Protect the shoulder at the bottom position.
- Terminate immediately if numbness, sharp pain or sudden weakness occurs.

---

# CAS Engine Rules

The CAS Engine may select the Towel Pull-Up when:

- grip-integrated pulling is the intended adaptation,
- support or open-hand grip is a limiting factor,
- vertical pulling is eligible,
- the athlete meets readiness requirements,
- and total grip volume remains recoverable.

The engine must avoid selecting it when:

- the athlete has active hand, wrist, elbow or shoulder pain,
- grip fatigue would compromise a higher-priority session,
- the athlete lacks basic pull-up competence,
- or a simpler grip exercise better matches the objective.

---

# Decision Trace Requirements

When the Towel Pull-Up is selected, the Decision Trace should identify:

- the targeted grip function,
- the reason a towel variation is preferred over a standard pull-up,
- the athlete’s current pull-up capacity,
- the assistance or loading method,
- the intended repetition range,
- the interaction with grappling and pulling volume,
- relevant hand, elbow or shoulder constraints,
- and the progression criterion.

Example:

> Towel Pull-Up selected to develop support-grip endurance integrated with vertical pulling. The athlete demonstrates pain-free conventional pull-ups and adequate shoulder control. Three submaximal sets are prescribed after primary strength work to avoid compromising technical grappling practice.

---

# Summary

The Towel Pull-Up is a grip-intensive vertical pulling exercise.

Its primary value lies in combining:

- support grip,
- open-hand strength,
- forearm endurance,
- vertical pulling strength,
- scapular control,
- and full-body tension.

Within CAS, it is appropriate when the athlete requires stronger grip-integrated pulling capacity and possesses adequate hand, elbow and shoulder readiness.

It must be programmed conservatively because local grip fatigue and connective-tissue stress can accumulate rapidly.
