# GRIP EXERCISE COMPARISON

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document compares the exercises currently included in the CAS Grip category.

Its purpose is to help the CAS Engine distinguish between exercises that may appear similar but produce different adaptations, fatigue profiles, technical demands and transfer effects.

The exercises compared are:

- Towel Pull-Up
- Plate Pinch
- Pinch Carry
- Rope Climb
- Rope Pull

The comparison must not be used to identify one universally superior exercise.

Each exercise is appropriate only when it matches:

- the intended grip function,
- the athlete’s current capability,
- the required movement pattern,
- the available equipment,
- the session objective,
- the athlete’s tissue readiness,
- and the total grip and pulling load.

---

# Core Principle

> Select the exercise that trains the required grip function with the lowest unnecessary complexity, fatigue and risk.

Grip exercises are not interchangeable.

An exercise may emphasize:

- pinch grip,
- open-hand grip,
- support grip,
- grip endurance,
- vertical pulling,
- horizontal pulling,
- locomotion,
- or whole-body coordination.

The CAS Engine must identify the primary adaptation before selecting the exercise.

# Primary Adaptation Comparison

## Towel Pull-Up

Primary adaptation:

- grip-integrated vertical pulling strength.

Best suited for:

- athletes who can already perform pull-ups,
- development of open-hand support grip,
- combining grip and relative upper-body strength,
- lower-complexity preparation for rope climbing.

Not ideal when:

- isolated grip work is required,
- vertical pulling volume is already high,
- the athlete cannot control bodyweight pulling,
- or shoulder tolerance is limited.

---

## Plate Pinch

Primary adaptation:

- static pinch-grip strength.

Best suited for:

- thumb opposition development,
- finger-pressure strength,
- low-systemic-fatigue grip training,
- precise local loading,
- asymmetry assessment.

Not ideal when:

- support grip is the actual limitation,
- movement under load is required,
- whole-body force transfer is a priority,
- or the athlete has active thumb irritation.

---

## Pinch Carry

Primary adaptation:

- pinch-grip endurance under locomotion.

Best suited for:

- maintaining pinch strength while moving,
- combining hand strength with posture,
- unilateral grip assessment,
- anti-lateral-flexion integration.

Not ideal when:

- static pinch strength is the primary objective,
- the athlete cannot perform a secure plate pinch,
- balance is impaired,
- or local hand fatigue must be minimized.

---

## Rope Climb

Primary adaptation:

- repeated open-hand gripping and vertical pulling against bodyweight.

Best suited for:

- advanced grip-integrated pulling,
- repeated bodyweight force production,
- whole-body climbing coordination,
- high transfer to deformable gripping tasks.

Not ideal when:

- the athlete lacks descent skill,
- vertical pulling strength is insufficient,
- the equipment or supervision is inadequate,
- local tissue tolerance is limited,
- or a simpler exercise can achieve the objective.

---

## Rope Pull

Primary adaptation:

- repeated hand-over-hand pulling endurance.

Best suited for:

- grip-integrated pulling without leaving the ground,
- horizontal or vertical external-load pulling,
- repeated regripping,
- scalable work-capacity development.

Not ideal when:

- maximal bodyweight pulling strength is the objective,
- isolated pinch strength is required,
- the athlete cannot maintain stable posture,
- or pulling volume is already excessive.

---

# Grip Function Comparison

## Pinch Grip

Best exercises:

1. Plate Pinch
2. Pinch Carry

Plate Pinch is preferred when:

- static pinch strength is the primary goal,
- total systemic fatigue must remain low,
- precise side-to-side comparison is needed,
- or the athlete is learning the pinch pattern.

Pinch Carry is preferred when:

- pinch strength must be maintained during movement,
- postural control is also desirable,
- or the athlete has already established secure static pinch capacity.

---

## Open-Hand Support Grip

Best exercises:

1. Towel Pull-Up
2. Rope Climb
3. Rope Pull

Towel Pull-Up is preferred when:

- vertical pulling strength is required,
- technical complexity should remain moderate,
- bodyweight pulling is appropriate,
- and climbing skill is not necessary.

Rope Climb is preferred when:

- repeated bodyweight pulling is required,
- the athlete has advanced technical competence,
- whole-body coordination is part of the objective,
- and the environment is safe.

Rope Pull is preferred when:

- external resistance must be adjustable,
- the athlete should remain grounded,
- repeated regripping is the main demand,
- or horizontal pulling is more relevant.

---

## Grip Endurance

Most suitable exercises:

- Rope Pull
- Rope Climb
- Pinch Carry
- Towel Pull-Up

The best choice depends on the grip function required.

Use:

- Pinch Carry for pinch endurance,
- Rope Pull for repeated open-hand pulling endurance,
- Rope Climb for high-level grip endurance under bodyweight,
- Towel Pull-Up for lower-volume strength-endurance.

---

# Movement Pattern Comparison

## Static Hold

Primary exercise:

- Plate Pinch.

Advantages:

- simple execution,
- precise loading,
- low systemic fatigue,
- direct assessment of pinch capacity.

Limitations:

- limited whole-body transfer,
- no locomotion,
- no repeated pulling,
- low coordination demand.

---

## Loaded Carry

Primary exercise:

- Pinch Carry.

Advantages:

- combines grip with movement,
- adds trunk and shoulder stabilization,
- improves grip retention under locomotor disturbance.

Limitations:

- less precise than a static hold,
- introduces balance and gait variables,
- may be limited by posture before grip.

---

## Vertical Pull

Primary exercises:

- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Climb.

Towel Pull-Up provides:

- controlled repetition structure,
- moderate complexity,
- easier volume standardization,
- simpler progression.

Rope Climb provides:

- repeated regripping,
- greater coordination demand,
- higher grip endurance,
- greater technical and safety cost.

---

## Horizontal or Adjustable-Direction Pull

Primary exercise:

- Rope Pull.

Advantages:

- load can be scaled,
- direction can be modified,
- repeated hand-over-hand pulling is trained,
- the athlete remains grounded.

Limitations:

- less direct bodyweight transfer,
- setup quality strongly affects execution,
- can become conditioning-dominant if poorly prescribed.

# Complexity Comparison

## Low Complexity

Plate Pinch

Appropriate for:

- beginners,
- isolated grip development,
- precise loading,
- low-skill accessory work.

## Low to Moderate Complexity

Pinch Carry

Appropriate for:

- athletes with static pinch competency,
- posture-integrated grip work,
- controlled locomotion.

## Moderate Complexity

Towel Pull-Up

Rope Pull

Towel Pull-Up requires:

- pull-up competence,
- shoulder control,
- trunk stability.

Rope Pull requires:

- secure regripping,
- stable stance,
- pulling rhythm,
- trunk control.

## High Complexity

Rope Climb

Requires:

- climbing technique,
- descent control,
- advanced grip tolerance,
- full-body coordination,
- safe equipment and supervision.

---

# Fatigue Comparison

## Lowest Systemic Fatigue

Plate Pinch

This is the preferred option when:

- local pinch strength is required,
- whole-body fatigue must remain minimal,
- or the exercise is placed after demanding primary work.

## Low to Moderate Systemic Fatigue

Pinch Carry

Systemic fatigue increases with:

- longer distances,
- bilateral loading,
- heavier implements,
- and repeated sets.

## Moderate Systemic Fatigue

Towel Pull-Up

Rope Pull

Towel Pull-Up fatigue is driven by:

- bodyweight,
- pulling volume,
- proximity to failure.

Rope Pull fatigue is driven by:

- external resistance,
- distance,
- pace,
- stance,
- and work duration.

## Highest Systemic Fatigue

Rope Climb

Rope Climb combines:

- repeated pulling,
- grip demand,
- trunk stabilization,
- leg coordination,
- ascent,
- and descent.

---

# Local Grip Fatigue Comparison

## High

- Plate Pinch
- Pinch Carry
- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Pull

## Very High

- Rope Climb

Rope Climb creates the greatest local fatigue because the athlete must:

- support bodyweight,
- repeatedly regrip,
- pull,
- stabilize,
- and control the descent.

---

# Connective-Tissue Stress Comparison

## Thumb and Finger Emphasis

Highest:

- Plate Pinch
- Pinch Carry

Primary tissues:

- thumb adductors,
- thumb tendons,
- finger flexors,
- hand intrinsics.

## Finger Flexor and Elbow Emphasis

Highest:

- Rope Climb
- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Pull

Primary tissues:

- finger flexor tendons,
- wrist flexors,
- medial elbow structures,
- elbow flexor tendons,
- shoulder tissues.

## Highest Overall Tissue Demand

Rope Climb

This is due to:

- bodyweight loading,
- repeated grip transitions,
- high pulling demand,
- descent exposure,
- and potential fatigue accumulation.

# Progression Comparison

## Plate Pinch

Primary progression variables:

- load,
- plate thickness,
- surface smoothness,
- hold duration,
- unilateral execution.

## Pinch Carry

Primary progression variables:

- distance,
- duration,
- load,
- surface difficulty,
- asymmetrical loading.

## Towel Pull-Up

Primary progression variables:

- reduced assistance,
- repetitions,
- slower eccentric,
- thicker towel,
- isometric pauses,
- external load.

## Rope Climb

Primary progression variables:

- climbing height,
- number of pulls,
- foot-lock efficiency,
- reduced leg assistance,
- repeated climbs,
- legless execution.

## Rope Pull

Primary progression variables:

- external load,
- pulling distance,
- work duration,
- rope thickness,
- stance difficulty,
- reduced rest.

- lighter plates,
- textured surfaces,
- shorter holds,
- bilateral execution,
- pinch block with adjustable loading.

## Pinch Carry Regressions

- static plate pinch,
- shorter distance,
- lighter load,
- bilateral execution,
- supported carry.

- towel hang,
- assisted towel pull-up,
- eccentric-only towel pull-up,
- conventional pull-up,

- static rope hold,
- lighter load,
- shorter pulling distance,
- seated rope pull,
- cable rope row.

# Combat-Sport Transfer Comparison

## Gi-Based Grappling

Highest relevance:

- Rope Climb
- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Pull
- Plate Pinch
- Pinch Carry

Primary reasons:

- deformable gripping,
- repeated pulling,
- finger and thumb endurance,
- grip retention.

## No-Gi Grappling and Wrestling

Highest relevance:

- Rope Pull
- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Climb

Primary reasons:

- wrist and limb control,
- pulling strength,
- hand-over-hand force,
- forearm endurance.

## MMA

Most relevant:

- Rope Pull
- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Climb

Secondary relevance:

- Pinch Carry
- Plate Pinch.

## Krav Maga

Most relevant:

- Rope Pull
- Towel Pull-Up
- Pinch Carry

Applications include:

- limb control,
- clothing control,
- object manipulation,
- weapon retention,
- pulling strength.

## Boxing and Kickboxing

Most appropriate options:

- Plate Pinch in low volume,
- Pinch Carry in low volume,
- Towel Pull-Up when pulling strength is also required.

High-fatigue grip work should not compromise:

- hand relaxation,
- wrist comfort,
- glove use,
- or striking speed.

# Selection by Primary Objective

## Objective: Increase Pinch Strength

Select:

1. Plate Pinch
2. Pinch Carry

Default choice:

- Plate Pinch.

Select Pinch Carry when movement under load is also required.

---

## Objective: Increase Pinch Endurance

Select:

1. Pinch Carry
2. Plate Pinch with longer holds

Default choice:

- Pinch Carry for locomotor integration.
- Plate Pinch for more isolated control.

---

## Objective: Increase Open-Hand Pulling Strength

Select:

1. Towel Pull-Up
2. Rope Climb

Default choice:

- Towel Pull-Up for most athletes.
- Rope Climb for advanced athletes with safe equipment and technical competence.

---

## Objective: Increase Repeated Pulling Endurance

Select:

1. Rope Pull
2. Rope Climb
3. Towel Pull-Up

Default choice:

- Rope Pull when external load and work duration must be controlled.
- Rope Climb when bodyweight climbing is specifically relevant.

---

## Objective: Minimize Systemic Fatigue

Select:

1. Plate Pinch
2. Pinch Carry with conservative loading

Avoid:

- high-volume Rope Climb,
- near-failure Towel Pull-Up,
- long-duration Rope Pull.

---

## Objective: Maximize Whole-Body Integration

Select:

1. Rope Climb
2. Rope Pull
3. Pinch Carry

---

## Objective: Lowest Technical Complexity

Select:

1. Plate Pinch
2. Pinch Carry

---

## Objective: Highest Grip-Specific Challenge

Select according to grip type:

- Plate Pinch for pinch grip,
- Rope Climb for open-hand support grip,
- Rope Pull for repeated grip endurance.

---

# Selection by Athlete Level

## Beginner

Preferred exercises:

- Plate Pinch
- Static pinch preparation
- Light Pinch Carry
- Supported rope hold

Towel Pull-Up may be used only if conventional pull-up capacity is established.

Rope Climb is generally not appropriate.

---

## Intermediate

Preferred exercises:

- Plate Pinch
- Pinch Carry
- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Pull
- partial Rope Climb.

---

## Advanced

Eligible exercises:

- all listed exercises,
- including full Rope Climb,
- legless Rope Climb,
- weighted Towel Pull-Up,
- heavy Rope Pull,
- advanced Pinch Carry variations.

Advanced status does not remove tissue-readiness requirements.

---

# Selection by Limiting Factor

## Weak Thumb Contribution

Select:

- Plate Pinch
- Pinch Carry.

## Premature Hand Opening During Pull-Ups

Select:

- Towel Pull-Up,
- supported towel hangs if regression is needed.

## Poor Grip During Repeated Pulling

Select:

- Rope Pull.

## Poor Grip Under Bodyweight and Regripping

Select:

- Rope Climb,
- only if technical prerequisites are met.

## Poor Grip While Moving

Select:

- Pinch Carry for pinch demand,
- Rope Pull for repeated open-hand pulling.

## Excessive Pulling Volume Already Present

Select:

- Plate Pinch at low volume,
- or omit direct grip work if recovery is limited.

## Usually Near the End of the Session

- Plate Pinch

## May Be Used as a Secondary Strength Exercise

- Towel Pull-Up

## Highest Interference With Deadlifts and Heavy Pulling

- Rope Climb
- Towel Pull-Up
- Rope Pull

- Pinch Carry

- Plate Pinch at conservative volume

- dropped plates,

- grip slipping,

# Default CAS Selection Hierarchy

When multiple exercises are eligible, CAS should generally use the following hierarchy:

1. identify the required grip function,
2. identify whether pulling or locomotion is required,
3. select the lowest necessary complexity,
4. assess hand, wrist, elbow and shoulder readiness,
5. account for grappling and pulling volume,
6. protect higher-priority technical sessions,
7. select a recoverable prescription,
8. define a progression and regression rule.

---

# Default Selection Rules

## Choose Plate Pinch When

- pinch strength is the clear target,
- low systemic fatigue is preferred,
- no movement integration is required.

## Choose Pinch Carry When

- pinch endurance under movement is required,
- static pinch competency already exists,
- posture integration adds value.

## Choose Towel Pull-Up When

- vertical pulling strength and open-hand grip are both targets,
- the athlete has conventional pull-up competency,
- Rope Climb complexity is unnecessary.

## Choose Rope Climb When

- climbing-specific grip and pulling integration is required,
- the athlete meets all technical prerequisites,
- the environment is safe,
- and high fatigue cost is justified.

## Choose Rope Pull When

- repeated hand-over-hand pulling is the primary target,
- external loading must be adjustable,
- the athlete should remain grounded,
- or conditioning integration is desired.

---

# CAS Engine Rules

The CAS Engine must not select a Grip exercise only because it appears more advanced or difficult.

The engine must:

- identify the exact grip function,
- distinguish static from dynamic demand,
- distinguish pinch grip from open-hand support grip,
- distinguish isolated grip work from grip-integrated pulling,
- select the lowest sufficient technical complexity,
- account for local tissue fatigue,
- account for all pulling and grappling exposure,
- protect hand health,
- and justify the choice in the Decision Trace.

The engine should prefer simpler exercises when two options provide the same required adaptation.

---

# Decision Trace Requirements

For every Grip exercise selection, the Decision Trace should identify:

- the grip function targeted,
- the movement pattern required,
- the reason the selected exercise is preferred over alternatives,
- the athlete’s current skill level,
- the expected fatigue cost,
- the interaction with pulling and combat practice,
- relevant hand, wrist, elbow or shoulder constraints,
- and the progression or regression rule.

Example:

> Towel Pull-Up selected instead of Rope Climb because the athlete requires open-hand vertical pulling strength but does not yet demonstrate safe rope-climbing descent. The exercise provides the required grip and pulling stimulus with lower technical and safety cost.

---

# Summary

The Grip chapter contains exercises with distinct functions.

Plate Pinch is the most direct option for isolated pinch strength.

Pinch Carry develops pinch endurance while integrating posture and locomotion.

Towel Pull-Up combines open-hand grip with vertical pulling strength.

Rope Climb provides the highest whole-body and technical demand through repeated bodyweight climbing.

Rope Pull develops repeated hand-over-hand grip and pulling endurance with adjustable external resistance.

The correct CAS selection depends on:

- the required grip function,
- the movement pattern,
- the athlete’s competence,
- the desired transfer,
- the acceptable fatigue cost,
- and the athlete’s current tissue readiness.

No exercise is universally superior.

The best exercise is the one that produces the intended adaptation safely, efficiently and without unnecessary interference.
