# Purpose

The Ab Wheel Rollout is a dynamic anti-extension exercise used to develop the athlete's ability to maintain trunk and pelvic organization while the arms move away from the body.

Within the Combat Athlete System, its purpose is to improve:

* anti-extension strength;
* anterior trunk control;
* rib cage and pelvis organization;
* force transmission through the trunk;
* shoulder-to-trunk coordination;
* and the ability to resist lumbar extension under increasing leverage.

The Ab Wheel is not selected primarily to produce abdominal fatigue.

It is selected when the athlete must resist extension while maintaining an integrated relationship between:

* the shoulders;
* the rib cage;
* the abdominal wall;
* the pelvis;
* and the hips.

---

# Core Principle

> The rollout distance is valid only while the athlete can maintain trunk position.

A longer rollout is not automatically a better rollout.

The exercise loses its intended function when the athlete:

* extends excessively through the lumbar spine;
* allows the rib cage to flare;
* loses pelvic control;
* shifts the load away from the trunk;
* or uses momentum to return.

Range of motion must be earned through control.

# Exercise Definition

The Ab Wheel Rollout begins from a stable kneeling or standing position with the athlete holding a wheel or equivalent rollout device.

The athlete moves the wheel forward while allowing the shoulders to flex and the body to lengthen.

Throughout the movement, the athlete must resist excessive lumbar extension and maintain a controlled relationship between the rib cage and pelvis.

The athlete then returns to the starting position using the abdominal wall, shoulders and trunk as an integrated system.

The exercise is considered successful only when the athlete preserves the intended trunk position through the selected range of motion.

# Entry Requirements

The standard kneeling Ab Wheel Rollout should be prescribed only when the athlete can demonstrate:

* a controlled front plank;
* the ability to maintain rib cage and pelvis alignment;
* pain-free shoulder flexion through the required range;
* pain-free wrist and elbow loading;
* the ability to brace without excessive breath holding;
* and the ability to distinguish lumbar extension from shoulder movement.

A suitable entry benchmark may include:

* a technically stable front plank for 20 to 30 seconds;
* a controlled Dead Bug;
* and a short-range rollout without lumbar position loss.

These benchmarks are guidance rather than universal performance tests.

# Standard Setup

1. Place the Ab Wheel on a stable, non-slip surface.
2. Kneel on a pad if required for comfort.
3. Hold the handles firmly with the wrists in a controlled position.
4. Position the wheel beneath or slightly in front of the shoulders.
5. Keep the elbows extended without forcing them into hyperextension.
6. Organize the rib cage over the pelvis.
7. Create light posterior pelvic control without excessive spinal rounding.
8. Contract the gluteals sufficiently to support pelvic position.
9. Brace the trunk before initiating the rollout.
10. Keep the neck aligned with the trunk.

---

# Standard Execution

1. Begin from the organized kneeling position.
2. Move the wheel forward by allowing the shoulders and body to travel together.
3. Maintain trunk tension as the lever length increases.
4. Prevent the lower back from arching.
5. Prevent the rib cage from flaring upward.
6. Continue only as far as the athlete can maintain the intended position.
7. Pause briefly at the end of the controlled range.
8. Pull the wheel back toward the knees using the shoulders and trunk together.
9. Return without driving the hips backward first.
10. Reorganize the starting position before the next repetition.

---

# Technical Intent

The athlete should attempt to:

* lengthen the body without losing spinal control;
* keep the rib cage connected to the pelvis;
* move through the shoulders rather than the lower back;
* maintain continuous abdominal tension;
* and return as one integrated unit.

The exercise should feel like controlled whole-body lengthening rather than repeated spinal flexion and extension.

---

# Breathing Strategy

## Default Strategy

* inhale before or during the early part of the rollout;
* maintain controlled trunk pressure during the most difficult range;
* exhale during the return or after passing the most demanding point;
* reset breathing between repetitions when necessary.

## Acceptable Alternative

A controlled exhalation during the rollout may help some athletes:

* reduce rib flare;
* improve abdominal recruitment;
* and maintain pelvic control.

## Invalid Strategy

Uncontrolled prolonged breath holding that causes:

* dizziness;
* excessive tension;
* loss of rhythm;
* or deterioration of technique.

Breathing strategy must support trunk control rather than become the primary challenge.

---

# Coaching Cues

Useful cues include:

* Keep your ribs connected to your pelvis.
* Squeeze the glutes lightly.
* Move from the shoulders, not the lower back.
* Make your body long.
* Keep the wheel under control.
* Stop before your back arches.
* Pull the floor back toward you.
* Return as one piece.
* Own the range.
* Do not chase distance.

The Engine should not prescribe an excessive number of cues simultaneously.

One or two cues should be selected according to the athlete's primary technical error.

---

# Valid Repetition Standard

A repetition is valid when:

* the wheel moves through the prescribed range;
* lumbar position remains within the accepted technical threshold;
* the rib cage does not flare excessively;
* the pelvis remains controlled;
* the athlete does not collapse through the shoulders;
* the wheel remains stable;
* the return is controlled;
* momentum does not dominate;
* and the athlete finishes in the planned starting position.

A technically shorter repetition is preferable to a longer invalid repetition.

---

# Stopping Rules

The set must stop when:

* lumbar extension becomes visible and cannot be corrected immediately;
* the athlete loses rib cage and pelvis alignment;
* the hips sag toward the floor;
* the athlete cannot return without excessive momentum;
* the shoulders collapse or become painful;
* the wheel deviates uncontrollably;
* the athlete shortens range involuntarily because of fatigue;
* breathing becomes uncontrolled;
* or the athlete reports pain.

The athlete must not continue merely to complete the planned repetition target.

# Common Errors

## Excessive Lumbar Extension

The lower back arches as the athlete rolls forward.

### Likely Causes

* excessive range;
* insufficient anti-extension strength;
* poor rib cage control;
* poor pelvic control;
* fatigue;
* or misunderstanding of the movement.

### Correction

* reduce range;
* use a wall target;
* regress to Stability Ball Rollout or Dead Bug;
* reinforce rib cage and pelvis organization;
* or reduce the number of repetitions.

---

## Rib Flare

The lower ribs rise away from the pelvis.

### Likely Causes

* excessive shoulder range;
* poor breathing strategy;
* insufficient abdominal control;
* or a rollout distance beyond current capacity.

### Correction

* exhale gently during the rollout;
* reduce range;
* improve shoulder mobility separately;
* or use a simpler anti-extension exercise.

---

## Hip Hinge Dominance

The athlete moves the arms slightly but sends the hips backward and forward.

### Consequence

The movement becomes less demanding for the trunk and may no longer represent a true rollout.

### Correction

* keep the hips slightly forward;
* shorten the range;
* use a fixed wall target;
* or perform a Stability Ball Rollout before returning to the wheel.

A limited hip hinge may be acceptable in early regressions if explicitly prescribed.

---

## Returning With the Hips First

The athlete pushes the hips backward before the wheel moves.

### Consequence

The athlete reduces trunk demand and changes the intended movement.

### Correction

* cue the athlete to pull the wheel back;
* maintain gluteal tension;
* use fewer repetitions;
* or reduce the rollout distance.

---

## Excessive Spinal Flexion

The athlete rounds aggressively through the upper or lower back.

### Consequence

The exercise may become a dynamic spinal-flexion movement rather than controlled anti-extension.

### Correction

* return to the defined neutral range;
* reduce excessive posterior pelvic tilt;
* and maintain a long trunk position.

Slight thoracic flexion may be acceptable when it supports control and remains consistent.

---

## Shoulder Collapse

The athlete sinks between the shoulders or loses scapular control.

### Likely Causes

* insufficient serratus function;
* shoulder fatigue;
* excessive range;
* or poor setup.

### Correction

* push actively through the handles;
* shorten the range;
* use a plank or Stability Ball Rollout regression;
* or address shoulder control separately.

---

## Bent-Elbow Pulling

The athlete bends the elbows significantly during the return.

### Consequence

The exercise becomes more dependent on the arms and reduces the intended long-lever trunk demand.

### Correction

* reduce range;
* maintain soft but stable elbows;
* or regress the exercise.

Minor elbow movement may be acceptable, but it must not dominate the return.

---

## Using Momentum

The athlete rolls rapidly forward or rebounds out of the bottom position.

### Consequence

* reduced positional control;
* increased technical risk;
* inconsistent loading;
* and reduced anti-extension quality.

### Correction

* slow the eccentric phase;
* add a brief pause;
* use lower repetition targets;
* or reduce range.

---

## Excessive Neck Extension

The athlete looks forward and lifts the head during the rollout.

### Correction

Maintain the neck in line with the trunk and direct the gaze toward the floor.

Anti-extension strength and dynamic trunk control

2 to 5 sets

## Valid Repetition Range

3 to 12 repetitions

## Standard V0.1 Range

3 to 4 sets of 5 to 10 controlled repetitions

## Advanced Strength Range

3 to 5 sets of 3 to 6 repetitions using a more difficult variation

## Technical Learning Range

2 to 4 sets of 3 to 6 short-range repetitions

## Rest Range

45 to 120 seconds

## Default Rest

60 to 90 seconds

## Tempo Options

### Controlled Standard

`3-1-2`

* 3 seconds rollout;
* 1 second controlled pause;
* 2 seconds return.

### Continuous Controlled

`2-0-2`

### Eccentric Emphasis

`4-1-2`

Tempo must not cause the athlete to exceed available technical capacity.

## Frequency

1 to 3 exposures per week

## Recommended Weekly Exposure

2 exposures per week when anti-extension development is a meaningful objective

Frequency must account for:

* shoulder fatigue;
* concurrent pressing volume;
* other long-lever core exercises;
* and combat training load.

---

# Load Determination

External load is not normally required in the initial kneeling variation.

Difficulty is primarily determined by:

* rollout distance;
* lever length;
* body position;
* control;
* tempo;
* and variation.

The correct difficulty allows the athlete to:

* complete the prescribed range;
* preserve technical quality;
* maintain one to three technically valid repetitions in reserve;
* and avoid excessive secondary fatigue.

The set should not normally be taken to muscular failure.

Technical failure defines the practical endpoint.

---

# Intensity Regulation

The Ab Wheel does not use percentage of one-repetition maximum.

Intensity should be regulated through:

* repetition quality;
* range of motion;
* variation difficulty;
* tempo;
* and technical repetitions in reserve.

## Recommended Effort

Approximately RPE 6 to 8.

## Technical Repetitions in Reserve

1 to 3 technically valid repetitions.

An athlete may still possess sufficient general strength to continue after trunk position has failed.

Those repetitions are not considered available.

---

# Volume Progression

Volume may progress through:

1. additional valid repetitions;
2. additional sets;
3. increased rollout distance;
4. longer pauses;
5. slower eccentric tempo;
6. reduced assistance;
7. or a more difficult variation.

Only one primary variable should normally progress at a time.

Range progression takes priority over adding repetitions when the athlete is performing deliberately shortened repetitions.

Technical quality takes priority over both.

---

# Progression Model

## Entry Variation

Dead Bug or Stability Ball Rollout

## Stage 1

Short-Range Kneeling Ab Wheel Rollout

The athlete rolls toward a wall or fixed target.

## Stage 2

Standard Kneeling Ab Wheel Rollout

Range increases progressively while trunk control remains stable.

## Stage 3

Long-Range Kneeling Ab Wheel Rollout

The shoulders move farther forward and the body approaches a more extended position.

## Stage 4

Paused Kneeling Ab Wheel Rollout

The athlete pauses at the longest controlled position.

## Stage 5

Slow-Eccentric Kneeling Ab Wheel Rollout

The eccentric phase is deliberately extended.

## Stage 6

Elevated-Knee or Deficit Variation

The setup increases available range or lever demand.

## Stage 7

Assisted Standing Ab Wheel Rollout

A band, incline or wall target limits difficulty.

## Stage 8

Standing Ab Wheel Rollout

The athlete performs the movement from the feet with full-body control.

The standing rollout is an advanced exercise and is not required for general Core development.

---

# Progression Criteria

The athlete may progress when all prescribed sets are completed with:

* stable lumbar position;
* controlled rib cage and pelvis alignment;
* consistent repetition range;
* no pain;
* no uncontrolled momentum;
* no significant shoulder collapse;
* appropriate breathing;
* and at least one technically valid repetition in reserve.

The progression should be repeated across at least two exposures when the increase in difficulty is substantial.

---

# Maintenance Rule

Maintain the current variation when:

* the athlete completes the prescription;
* technique is acceptable but not yet consistent;
* the final repetitions approach the quality threshold;
* or the next progression would create unnecessary complexity.

Maintenance is an intentional training decision.

---

# Regression Criteria

Regress the exercise when:

* technical errors occur during the majority of repetitions;
* the athlete cannot maintain the starting position;
* the athlete repeatedly loses lumbar control;
* shoulder or wrist discomfort appears;
* the athlete cannot return the wheel under control;
* fatigue from previous training prevents correct execution;
* or the selected variation exceeds the athlete's technical level.

# Equivalent Substitutions

## Strong Equivalence

### Barbell Rollout

Preserves:

* anti-extension;
* dynamic shoulder movement;
* long-lever trunk control;
* and similar progression potential.

### Stability Ball Rollout

Preserves the primary function with lower peak difficulty and greater instability.

### Suspension Trainer Fallout

Preserves dynamic anti-extension but changes the direction and loading characteristics.

## Acceptable Equivalence

### Body Saw

Preserves dynamic anti-extension with forearm support and reduced shoulder flexion.

### Long-Lever Plank

Preserves anti-extension but removes most of the dynamic rollout component.

### Hollow Body Hold

Preserves anti-extension but changes the body orientation and shoulder loading.

## Partial Equivalence

### Dead Bug

Preserves trunk and pelvic control with lower loading and greater limb coordination.

It is a strong regression but not a mechanically equivalent replacement.

# Substitution Rules

The Ab Wheel should be replaced when:

* the athlete lacks an Ab Wheel or acceptable alternative;
* wrist loading is not tolerated;
* shoulder flexion is painful;
* the athlete cannot maintain lumbar position;
* current fatigue prevents safe execution;
* the exercise conflicts with high-priority shoulder work;
* or another exercise provides the same adaptation at a lower relevant cost.

Preferred substitution order:

1. Barbell Rollout;
2. Stability Ball Rollout;
3. Suspension Trainer Fallout;
4. Body Saw;
5. Long-Lever Plank;
6. Hollow Body Hold;
7. Dead Bug.

The exact order must change according to the reason for substitution.

A shoulder-related substitution should not automatically select another high-shoulder-demand rollout.

# Session Placement

## Default Placement

Core section after primary Strength, Assistance and Robustness work.

## Acceptable Alternative Placement

### Preparation

A low-volume and low-difficulty rollout variation may be used to reinforce trunk organization.

Example:

* 2 sets of 4 short-range repetitions.

This must not create fatigue before primary work.

### Primary Core Module

The exercise may appear earlier when anti-extension is the primary session objective.

### Strength Superset

May be paired with a low-interference exercise when:

* trunk fatigue does not compromise the paired movement;
* sufficient rest is preserved;
* and the athlete maintains technique.

## Avoid Placement

Immediately before:

* heavy squats;
* heavy deadlifts;
* maximal overhead pressing;
* high-priority explosive striking;
* or technical work requiring a fresh trunk,

when the rollout prescription is expected to create meaningful fatigue.

# Contraindications

## Hard Exclusions

Do not prescribe when the athlete reports:

* acute unexplained low-back pain;
* radiating pain;
* neurological symptoms;
* acute abdominal or inguinal pain;
* recent abdominal surgery without appropriate clearance;
* acute shoulder injury preventing supported flexion;
* acute wrist or elbow injury preventing safe loading;
* or dizziness during exertion.

## Temporary Exclusions

Temporarily exclude when:

* trunk fatigue prevents correct position;
* severe shoulder fatigue is present;
* the athlete cannot perform a pain-free regression;
* technique repeatedly fails despite reduced range;
* or the athlete is in a recovery state incompatible with the selected difficulty.

## Precautions

Use caution with:

* previous low-back pain;
* shoulder mobility restrictions;
* previous abdominal-wall injury;
* hernia history;
* significant anterior pelvic control limitations;
* wrist sensitivity;
* and unfamiliar high-eccentric loading.

The CAS must not diagnose these conditions.

A relevant health professional should determine whether training is appropriate when uncertainty exceeds the Engine's safe operating rules.

# Combat-Sport Relevance

## General Transfer

The Ab Wheel contributes to combat performance by improving the athlete's ability to resist unwanted trunk extension while transmitting force between the limbs and torso.

Potential applications include:

* preserving posture during punching combinations;
* preventing force leakage during powerful strikes;
* maintaining trunk organization during kicks;
* controlling the torso during sprawls;
* maintaining position during clinch pressure;
* resisting extension during grappling exchanges;
* supporting ground transitions;
* and maintaining posture while the arms act away from the body.

## Transfer Mechanism

Transfer occurs through shared requirements for:

* trunk stiffness;
* rib cage and pelvis control;
* force transmission;
* shoulder-to-trunk coordination;
* and position maintenance under long leverage.

The exercise does not teach striking or grappling technique.

It supports physical qualities that may allow technical skills to be expressed more effectively.

# Limitations of Transfer

The Ab Wheel does not directly develop:

* striking technique;
* rotational velocity;
* reactive stability against unpredictable contact;
* aerobic capacity;
* anaerobic capacity;
* maximal lower-body strength;
* neck strength;
* or sport-specific timing.

It occurs in a controlled environment and does not reproduce the variability of combat.

It should therefore remain one component within a broader training system.

---

# Performance Indicators

Useful indicators include:

* maximum controlled rollout distance;
* number of technically valid repetitions;
* consistency of rollout distance;
* ability to maintain rib cage and pelvis organization;
* eccentric control;
* ability to pause at end range;
* technical repetitions in reserve;
* progression from assisted to unassisted variations;
* and reduced secondary limitation from the shoulders or wrists.

The CAS should prioritize technical performance indicators over total repetition count.

---

# Success Criteria

The exercise is producing the intended result when:

* rollout distance increases without lumbar compensation;
* repetition quality remains stable;
* the athlete can control longer lever positions;
* force transmission improves during related movements;
* the athlete reports appropriate trunk loading rather than low-back discomfort;
* the exercise no longer causes disproportionate soreness;
* and progression occurs without interference with technical combat training.

---

# Failure Criteria

The exercise is not producing an acceptable result when:

* low-back discomfort repeatedly occurs;
* lumbar extension remains uncontrolled;
* shoulders consistently fail before the trunk;
* the athlete cannot progress beyond a very short range despite appropriate regressions;
* technical quality decreases over successive sessions;
* the exercise creates excessive interference;
* or a lower-cost alternative produces the same adaptation more reliably.

The Engine should then:

* regress;
* substitute;
* reduce volume;
* alter placement;
* or remove the exercise temporarily.

# Example Prescriptions

## Technical Introduction

```yaml
exerciseId: CORE-001
exerciseName: Ab Wheel Rollout
variation: WALL_LIMITED_KNEELING
primaryFunction: ANTI_EXTENSION
sets: 3
repetitions: 5
tempo: 3-1-2
restSeconds: 75
intensityTarget: RPE_6
technicalIntent: Maintain rib cage and pelvis alignment through a short controlled range.
stoppingRule: Stop if lumbar extension appears or the return requires momentum.
progressionCriterion: Complete all repetitions at the fixed range for two exposures without visible compensation.
```

## Standard Development

```yaml
exerciseId: CORE-001
exerciseName: Ab Wheel Rollout
variation: KNEELING
primaryFunction: ANTI_EXTENSION
sets: 3
repetitions: 8
tempo: 3-1-2
restSeconds: 90
intensityTarget: RPE_7
technicalRepetitionsInReserve: 2
technicalIntent: Increase rollout distance while preserving trunk and pelvic position.
stoppingRule: End the set when the athlete cannot maintain the same controlled range.
progressionCriterion: Complete 3 sets of 10 with consistent range and two technical repetitions in reserve.
```

## Advanced Strength

```yaml
exerciseId: CORE-001
exerciseName: Ab Wheel Rollout
variation: LONG_RANGE_KNEELING_PAUSED
primaryFunction: ANTI_EXTENSION
sets: 4
repetitions: 5
tempo: 4-2-2
restSeconds: 120
intensityTarget: RPE_8
technicalRepetitionsInReserve: 1
technicalIntent: Control a long lever and maintain a two-second pause without lumbar extension.
stoppingRule: Stop if the pause cannot be maintained or trunk position changes.
progressionCriterion: Complete all sets with identical range before progressing toward an assisted standing variation.
```

# Explainability

## Standard Selection Explanation

The Ab Wheel was selected because the current objective requires dynamic anti-extension strength and trunk control.

It trains you to maintain rib cage and pelvis alignment while the arms move away from the body, which supports force transmission and postural control.

## Regression Explanation

The Ab Wheel was regressed because the longer rollout caused loss of trunk position.

The shorter variation preserves the same anti-extension objective while allowing technically valid repetitions.

## Substitution Explanation

The Ab Wheel was replaced because the current shoulder or wrist constraint made the rollout unsuitable.

The selected alternative preserves anti-extension training with a lower relevant joint demand.

## Non-Selection Explanation

The Ab Wheel was not selected because the current session already contains sufficient anti-extension and shoulder-loading work.

Adding it would increase fatigue without providing a necessary additional adaptation.

# Decision Engine Outputs

When selected, the Engine must define:

* variation;
* rollout range;
* sets;
* repetitions;
* tempo;
* rest;
* technical intent;
* stopping rule;
* progression criterion;
* and reason for selection.

The Engine must not output only:

```text
Ab Wheel — 3 × 10
```

That prescription is incomplete because it does not define:

* range;
* technical threshold;
* tempo;
* or progression conditions.

---

# Validation Rules

The Ab Wheel prescription is valid only when:

* the selected module requires anti-extension or dynamic trunk control;
* no Hard Constraint excludes the exercise;
* the athlete meets the variation's entry requirements;
* equipment is available;
* a technically appropriate range is defined;
* volume is compatible with recovery;
* the prescription includes a stopping rule;
* and the Decision Trace explains the selection.

The prescription is invalid when:

* the exercise is justified only by abdominal fatigue;
* a standing variation is assigned without sufficient competency;
* the athlete reports relevant acute pain;
* the range is undefined;
* technical failure is treated as an acceptable repetition;
* or the exercise duplicates an already sufficient stimulus without justification.

# Scientific Evidence Position

## Evidence Classification

Level 2 — Supported Exercise Application

The Ab Wheel is supported by:

* established biomechanical principles of anti-extension training;
* observed high anterior trunk demand;
* widespread strength and conditioning practice;
* and consistent progression through leverage manipulation.

Direct evidence proving superior transfer from the Ab Wheel to combat-sport performance is limited.

The CAS therefore does not claim that the Ab Wheel directly improves striking or grappling performance.

Its inclusion is justified by its ability to train physical qualities relevant to:

* trunk control;
* force transmission;
* and posture maintenance.

---

# Current Knowledge Limitations

The exact muscular demand of the Ab Wheel varies according to:

* athlete anatomy;
* rollout distance;
* pelvic position;
* shoulder range;
* equipment;
* tempo;
* and technical execution.

There is no universal rollout distance appropriate for all athletes.

The relationship between Ab Wheel performance and combat performance is indirect and context-dependent.

The Engine must therefore use the exercise as one possible anti-extension tool rather than a mandatory standard.

---

# Final Principle

> The athlete progresses in the Ab Wheel by controlling more leverage, not by surviving more spinal extension.

The Ab Wheel is valuable when it develops:

* anti-extension strength;
* dynamic trunk control;
* force transmission;
* and shoulder-to-trunk coordination,

while preserving:

* technical quality;
* acceptable fatigue;
* pain-free execution;
* and compatibility with higher-priority combat training.

When these conditions are not met, the Engine must reduce the range, regress the variation or select another anti-extension exercise.
