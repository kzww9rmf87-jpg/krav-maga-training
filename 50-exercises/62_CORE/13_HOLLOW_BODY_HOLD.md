# HOLLOW BODY HOLD

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Hollow Body Hold is a supine isometric trunk exercise used to develop anti-extension strength, anterior-chain endurance and whole-body tension.

Within CAS, it is primarily selected as a bodyweight core-strength exercise positioned between basic motor-control drills and more demanding dynamic anti-extension work.

It teaches the athlete to maintain ribcage–pelvis alignment while the arms and legs create long lever arms away from the centre of mass.

---

# Philosophy

Combat athletes must be able to create and preserve whole-body stiffness while the limbs generate force, absorb force or change position.

The Hollow Body Hold develops this capacity through a simple but demanding isometric position.

CAS does not treat the exercise as a test of how low the hands and feet can be held.

The objective is to maintain a stable trunk, controlled breathing and continuous tension without compensating through lumbar extension, rib flare or excessive neck strain.

# Execution Standard

Lie supine with the hips and knees flexed and the arms positioned according to the selected regression.

Set the ribcage over the pelvis and create circumferential abdominal tension.

Lift the head and shoulder blades slightly from the floor without pulling aggressively through the neck.

Extend the legs and arms only to the range that allows the lower trunk position to remain stable.

Maintain continuous whole-body tension while breathing in a controlled manner.

End the set before the lumbar spine arches, the ribs flare, the legs rise involuntarily or the neck becomes the dominant source of effort.

---

# Key Coaching Cues

Ribs down, pelvis quiet.

Press the lower trunk into a stable position without excessive flattening.

Reach long through the fingers and toes.

Keep the shoulder blades just off the floor.

Make the shape smaller before the back arches.

Breathe behind the brace.

Hold tension, not discomfort.

# Progression Model

Entry Level

The athlete can maintain a bent-knee hollow position for 15–20 seconds while breathing and preserving lumbar control.

Progression Strategy

Lengthen one lever at a time.

Progress the legs before lowering them closer to the floor.

Move the arms overhead only when rib position remains stable.

Increase duration after technical position is reliable.

Add external load or rocking only after the full bodyweight hold is controlled.

Regression Strategy

Bend the knees.

Raise the legs.

Bring the arms alongside the torso.

Reduce hold duration.

Use repeated short holds with complete resets.

Performance Indicators

Stable lumbar position

No rib flare

Controlled breathing

Consistent shoulder-blade elevation

Symmetrical arm and leg position

No neck-dominant strategy

Consistent hold duration

Exit Criteria

The athlete performs 3 sets of 30 seconds in a full hollow position with controlled breathing, stable lumbar alignment and no symptom increase.

At that point, CAS may progress the exercise or select a higher-load anti-extension option.

---

# Programming Applications

Movement Preparation

Use 2–3 short sets of 10–20 seconds to reinforce whole-body tension before strength or gymnastic work.

Robustness Module

Use 3–5 sets of 15–30 seconds with strict technical control.

Core Strength Accessory

Use 3–4 sets close to technical failure without allowing lumbar compensation.

Bodyweight Strength Module

Use longer-lever or loaded variations for 3–5 sets.

Return-to-Training Progression

Use tucked or bent-knee variations after basic supine trunk control has been restored.

---

# Substitution Logic

Substitute the Hollow Body Hold when:

The athlete needs greater anti-extension demand than a basic Dead Bug provides.

Equipment is unavailable.

The session requires a low-systemic-cost trunk exercise.

The athlete benefits from whole-body tension practice.

Dynamic spinal movement is not desired.

Do not substitute the Hollow Body Hold for:

Maximal loaded trunk strength

High-force loaded carries

Rotational power training

Ballistic trunk work

Combat-specific striking practice

Dynamic anti-extension work when velocity or movement tolerance is the primary objective

---

# CAS Selection Logic

CAS favors the Hollow Body Hold when:

The target adaptation is anti-extension strength endurance.

The athlete has mastered basic supine trunk control.

The session has limited equipment or space.

A bodyweight core-strength exercise is required.

The desired stimulus must create low systemic fatigue.

Whole-body tension is relevant to the session objective.

CAS deprioritizes the Hollow Body Hold when:

The athlete cannot maintain lumbar control in a short-lever variation.

Supine trunk flexion or leg extension provokes symptoms.

The target adaptation is maximal strength or power.

The athlete requires rotational, lateral or loaded-carry capacity.

Local trunk fatigue would interfere with the primary session objective.

# Evidence Classification

Evidence Level

Level 2 — Expert Practice

The Hollow Body Hold is widely used in gymnastics, bodyweight strength and physical preparation to develop whole-body tension and anti-extension endurance.

CAS treats it as a practical trunk-strength progression rather than a direct measure of combat performance or maximal core strength.

---

# Decision Summary

The Hollow Body Hold is a low-equipment anti-extension exercise that develops anterior-chain endurance, whole-body tension and ribcage–pelvis control.

Its principal value lies in bridging the gap between basic trunk motor-control exercises and higher-load anti-extension work.

CAS selects it primarily for robustness, bodyweight strength and general physical preparation.

It should be regressed whenever lumbar position, breathing or neck control deteriorates, and progressed only when the athlete can maintain a technically stable hollow shape.
