# CORE EXERCISE COMPARISON

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document compares the Core exercises currently defined in CAS and clarifies the conditions under which each exercise should be selected.

Its purpose is not to rank exercises from best to worst.

Its purpose is to help the CAS Engine distinguish between exercises that may appear similar but create different mechanical, neurological, technical and recovery demands.

The comparison covers:

- Ab Wheel
- Pallof Press
- Dead Bug
- Hollow Body Hold
- Hanging Leg Raise
- Dragon Flag
- Farmer Carry
- Suitcase Carry
- Overhead Carry

---

# Core Principle

> CAS selects the Core exercise that best matches the required trunk function, athlete capacity, movement context and fatigue budget.

Core training is not treated as a single quality.

The exercise must be chosen according to the dominant requirement:

- anti-extension,
- anti-rotation,
- anti-lateral flexion,
- dynamic trunk flexion control,
- integrated loaded locomotion,
- shoulder–trunk force transmission,
- motor learning,
- strength,
- endurance,
- or robustness.

# Functional Comparison

## Anti-Extension

Primary options:

1. Dead Bug
2. Hollow Body Hold
3. Ab Wheel
4. Dragon Flag
5. Overhead Carry

Progression logic:

Dead Bug

↓

Hollow Body Hold

↓

Ab Wheel

↓

Dragon Flag

The Overhead Carry belongs to a separate integrated branch because the anti-extension demand occurs while standing, walking and stabilizing an overhead load.

### Selection Distinction

Dead Bug is selected when the objective is control.

Hollow Body Hold is selected when the objective is static endurance.

Ab Wheel is selected when the objective is dynamic anti-extension strength.

Dragon Flag is selected when the objective is maximal long-lever anterior-chain strength.

Overhead Carry is selected when anti-extension must be integrated with shoulder stability and gait.

---

## Anti-Rotation

Primary options:

1. Pallof Press
2. Suitcase Carry

Secondary options:

- Farmer Carry
- Overhead Carry
- Dead Bug through contralateral variations

### Selection Distinction

The Pallof Press applies a clear transverse-plane rotational challenge while the athlete maintains a relatively fixed base.

The Suitcase Carry creates anti-rotation demand during locomotion and under unilateral vertical loading.

CAS selects the Pallof Press when isolated anti-rotation control, lower orthopaedic cost or precise positional coaching is required.

CAS selects the Suitcase Carry when anti-rotation must be expressed during gait with meaningful grip, pelvic and postural demands.

---

## Anti-Lateral Flexion

Primary option:

Suitcase Carry

Secondary options:

- Unilateral Overhead Carry
- Offset Farmer Carry variations
- Pallof Press in selected stances

The Suitcase Carry is the default CAS choice when resisting lateral trunk collapse is the dominant objective.

The unilateral Overhead Carry increases shoulder mobility and stability requirements and should not be treated as a direct regression of the Suitcase Carry.

---

## Integrated Trunk Stiffness

Primary options:

1. Farmer Carry
2. Suitcase Carry
3. Overhead Carry

These exercises train the trunk as part of a whole-body force-transmission system rather than as an isolated body segment.

### Selection Distinction

Farmer Carry emphasizes symmetrical axial loading, grip and global stiffness.

Suitcase Carry emphasizes asymmetrical force management, frontal-plane control and pelvic stability.

Overhead Carry emphasizes overhead shoulder stability, rib-cage control and anti-extension.

---

## Dynamic Anterior-Core Strength

Primary options:

1. Hanging Leg Raise
2. Ab Wheel
3. Dragon Flag

These exercises are not mechanically interchangeable.

The Hanging Leg Raise involves active hip flexion, posterior pelvic control, hanging grip and scapular stabilization.

The Ab Wheel primarily challenges the athlete to resist spinal extension while the shoulders move through flexion and the body lengthens.

The Dragon Flag exposes the anterior chain to an extreme long-lever anti-extension demand while the upper body remains anchored.

# Exercise-by-Exercise Selection Profile

## Ab Wheel

### Select When

- high-level anti-extension strength is required,
- the athlete can maintain rib-cage and pelvic alignment under a long lever,
- dynamic trunk stiffness is desired,
- shoulder flexion is pain-free,
- a compact high-stimulus exercise is needed.

### Avoid or Regress When

- lumbar extension appears early,
- the athlete cannot control the eccentric phase,
- shoulder discomfort is present,
- foundational bracing is insufficient,
- fatigue from heavy pressing or overhead work is excessive.

### Closest Alternatives

Dead Bug for motor control.

Hollow Body Hold for static endurance.

Dragon Flag for a more advanced long-lever demand.

---

## Pallof Press

### Select When

- anti-rotation capacity is the main objective,
- precise trunk control is more important than maximal loading,
- a low-impact core exercise is needed,
- the athlete is returning from pain or reduced readiness,
- stance-specific force transmission is desired.

### Avoid or Modify When

- the available resistance setup is unstable or unsafe,
- the athlete rotates through the pelvis or shoulders,
- the exercise becomes primarily an arm press,
- external load cannot be positioned correctly.

### Closest Alternatives

Suitcase Carry for locomotor anti-rotation.

Dead Bug for lower-cost contralateral control.

---

## Dead Bug

### Select When

- foundational lumbopelvic control is required,
- the athlete must learn rib-cage–pelvis alignment,
- readiness is low,
- joint stress must remain minimal,
- warm-up, rehabilitation or movement preparation is the priority.

### Avoid or Progress When

- the athlete performs the movement with complete control and insufficient challenge,
- the exercise no longer creates a meaningful coordination demand,
- the required adaptation is maximal core strength rather than motor control.

### Closest Alternatives

Hollow Body Hold for greater continuous tension.

Pallof Press for standing anti-rotation.

Ab Wheel for advanced anti-extension strength.

---

## Hollow Body Hold

### Select When

- static anterior-chain stiffness is required,
- bodyweight trunk endurance is a priority,
- the athlete needs a bridge between Dead Bug and advanced anti-extension work,
- gymnastics-style body tension has useful transfer,
- minimal equipment is available.

### Avoid or Regress When

- lumbar contact cannot be maintained,
- breathing becomes impossible,
- the athlete compensates through excessive cervical flexion,
- hip-flexor fatigue replaces trunk tension.

### Closest Alternatives

Dead Bug for easier motor-control work.

Ab Wheel for dynamic anti-extension strength.

Dragon Flag for maximal long-lever strength.

---

## Hanging Leg Raise

### Select When

- dynamic anterior-core strength is required,
- hanging grip and scapular control are useful secondary adaptations,
- posterior pelvic tilt can be maintained,
- the athlete needs strength through controlled hip flexion,
- sufficient shoulder tolerance and hanging capacity exist.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the athlete swings excessively,
- the movement is produced only by hip flexion without pelvic control,
- grip fails before the trunk receives adequate stimulus,
- shoulder or elbow symptoms are present,
- the athlete lacks basic hanging control.

### Closest Alternatives

Bent-Knee Hanging Raise as a regression.

Hollow Body Hold for static body-line control.

Dragon Flag for advanced long-lever anterior-chain work without hanging.

---

## Dragon Flag

### Select When

- elite anti-extension strength is required,
- the athlete has mastered lower-level anterior-core exercises,
- maximal bodyweight trunk strength is a meaningful objective,
- the athlete can control long-lever eccentric loading,
- an appropriate stable anchor is available.

### Avoid or Regress When

- lumbar extension or hip collapse occurs,
- the athlete lacks Ab Wheel or Hollow Body mastery,
- cervical discomfort appears,
- the upper-body anchor is unsafe,
- fatigue prevents controlled eccentrics.

### Closest Alternatives

Ab Wheel for lower technical and mechanical demand.

Hollow Body Hold for static regression.

Hanging Leg Raise for dynamic anterior-core work with a different force profile.

---

## Farmer Carry

### Select When

- global trunk stiffness is required,
- grip strength and postural endurance are secondary objectives,
- heavy bilateral loading is appropriate,
- loaded gait and whole-body force transmission are priorities,
- a simple high-transfer GPP exercise is needed.

### Avoid or Modify When

- gait deteriorates significantly,
- the athlete cannot maintain shoulder position,
- grip failure prevents the intended trunk stimulus,
- heavy axial loading conflicts with current recovery needs,
- equipment or walking space is inadequate.

### Closest Alternatives

Suitcase Carry for unilateral trunk demand.

Overhead Carry for shoulder-stability emphasis.

---

## Suitcase Carry

### Select When

- anti-lateral-flexion capacity is required,
- asymmetrical loading has useful transfer,
- pelvic control during gait is a priority,
- unilateral grip strength is desired,
- rotational compensation must be trained under locomotion.

### Avoid or Modify When

- the athlete leans away from or toward the load,
- pelvic shift cannot be controlled,
- the load repeatedly contacts the leg,
- grip is the sole limiting factor,
- low-back symptoms increase under asymmetrical loading.

### Closest Alternatives

Pallof Press for lower-cost anti-rotation.

Farmer Carry for symmetrical loading.

Unilateral Overhead Carry for greater shoulder and anti-extension demand.

---

## Overhead Carry

### Select When

- overhead shoulder stability is required,
- anti-extension control must be integrated with gait,
- scapular upward rotation and shoulder centration are priorities,
- combat-specific arm-position robustness is desired,
- the athlete possesses pain-free overhead mobility.

### Avoid or Modify When

- rib flare or lumbar extension cannot be controlled,
- the elbow bends or the implement drifts,
- shoulder symptoms are present,
- overhead mobility is insufficient,
- heavy pressing has already created substantial shoulder fatigue.

### Closest Alternatives

Farmer Carry for lower shoulder demand.

Suitcase Carry for unilateral trunk control without overhead loading.

Pallof Press for lower-cost trunk stabilization.

# Difficulty Continuums

## Foundational Control Continuum

Dead Bug

↓

Pallof Press

↓

Hollow Body Hold

This continuum progresses from supported motor control toward greater standing integration and continuous anterior-chain tension.

---

## Anti-Extension Strength Continuum

Dead Bug

↓

Hollow Body Hold

↓

Ab Wheel from Reduced Range

↓

Full Kneeling Ab Wheel

↓

Advanced Ab Wheel Variation

↓

Dragon Flag Progression

↓

Full Dragon Flag

Progression is conditional on technical mastery, not on elapsed training time.

---

## Loaded Carry Continuum

Farmer Carry

↓

Suitcase Carry

↓

Overhead Carry

This is not a strict universal difficulty ranking.

The continuum reflects increasing asymmetry, mobility and stabilization complexity.

A heavily loaded Farmer Carry may create greater absolute mechanical demand than a light Overhead Carry.

---

## Dynamic Anterior-Core Continuum

Bent-Knee Hanging Raise

↓

Controlled Hanging Knee Raise

↓

Straight-Leg Hanging Raise

↓

Toes-to-Bar or Equivalent Advanced Variation

The Dragon Flag belongs to a parallel long-lever anti-extension branch rather than being the automatic next step after the Hanging Leg Raise.

# Recovery-Cost Comparison

## Very Low Recovery Cost

- Dead Bug
- Pallof Press with moderate resistance

## Low Recovery Cost

- Hollow Body Hold
- Light technical Suitcase Carry
- Light technical Overhead Carry

## Low to Moderate Recovery Cost

- Ab Wheel
- Hanging Leg Raise
- Moderate Farmer Carry
- Moderate Suitcase Carry
- Moderate Overhead Carry

## Moderate Recovery Cost

- Dragon Flag
- Heavy Farmer Carry
- Heavy Suitcase Carry
- High-volume Hanging Leg Raise

CAS must calculate recovery cost from the actual prescription rather than from exercise identity alone.

Load, volume, duration, range of motion, variation and proximity to technical failure can substantially change the cost category.

# Readiness-Based Selection

## Low Readiness

Preferred options:

- Dead Bug
- Pallof Press
- Short low-load carries when technically stable

Avoid by default:

- Dragon Flag
- maximal Ab Wheel variations
- high-volume Hanging Leg Raise
- heavy carries

---

## Moderate Readiness

Preferred options:

- Hollow Body Hold
- Ab Wheel with controlled range
- Pallof Press
- moderate Farmer Carry
- moderate Suitcase Carry
- technically conservative Overhead Carry

---

## High Readiness

Eligible options:

- full Ab Wheel
- Hanging Leg Raise
- Dragon Flag progressions
- heavy Farmer Carry
- heavy Suitcase Carry
- demanding Overhead Carry variations

High readiness does not override exercise-specific eligibility or pain constraints.

# Combat-Sport Transfer Comparison

## Highest General Transfer

Farmer Carry and Suitcase Carry provide strong transfer to whole-body bracing, grip endurance, posture under load and asymmetrical force management.

## Highest Rotational-Control Transfer

Pallof Press and Suitcase Carry provide the clearest anti-rotation stimulus.

## Highest Overhead and Guard-Position Robustness Transfer

Overhead Carry provides the strongest shoulder–trunk integration when the athlete has adequate mobility and stability.

## Highest Foundational Transfer

Dead Bug provides foundational motor control that supports later force production but has limited direct intensity or sport specificity.

## Highest Anterior-Chain Strength Transfer

Ab Wheel, Hanging Leg Raise and Dragon Flag provide high anterior-core strength demands, but their transfer depends on integration into broader strength and combat training.

No isolated core exercise directly reproduces punching, kicking, grappling or defensive actions.

CAS treats these exercises as contributors to force transmission and robustness, not as technical combat-skill substitutes.

# Substitution Logic

## Dead Bug Substitutions

Substitute with Hollow Body Hold when greater continuous tension is appropriate.

Substitute with Pallof Press when standing anti-rotation is more relevant.

Do not automatically substitute with Ab Wheel if foundational control is absent.

---

## Hollow Body Hold Substitutions

Substitute with Dead Bug when technical control or readiness is insufficient.

Substitute with Ab Wheel when dynamic anti-extension strength is required.

Substitute with Dragon Flag progression only when advanced eligibility criteria are met.

---

## Ab Wheel Substitutions

Substitute with Hollow Body Hold when shoulder or dynamic rollout tolerance is limited.

Substitute with Dead Bug when lumbar control is insufficient.

Substitute with Dragon Flag only when a higher long-lever demand is intentionally required.

---

## Pallof Press Substitutions

Substitute with Suitcase Carry when locomotor anti-rotation and grip are desired.

Substitute with Dead Bug contralateral variations when equipment is unavailable or recovery cost must be minimal.

---

## Hanging Leg Raise Substitutions

Substitute with a bent-knee variation when straight-leg control is insufficient.

Substitute with Hollow Body Hold when hanging capacity limits the exercise.

Substitute with Ab Wheel when anti-extension strength is desired without hanging or active hip-flexion emphasis.

---

## Dragon Flag Substitutions

Substitute with Ab Wheel when the athlete requires a lower-risk advanced anti-extension option.

Substitute with Hollow Body Hold when long-lever control is insufficient.

Do not replace it with high-repetition spinal flexion merely to preserve difficulty.

---

## Farmer Carry Substitutions

Substitute with Suitcase Carry when unilateral trunk control is the priority.

Substitute with sled work only when locomotor loading is desired but grip must be reduced; the trunk stimulus will not be identical.

Substitute with static heavy holds when walking space is unavailable, while recognizing the loss of gait demand.

---

## Suitcase Carry Substitutions

Substitute with Pallof Press when asymmetrical axial loading is contraindicated.

Substitute with Farmer Carry when bilateral loading is more appropriate.

Substitute with static suitcase holds when locomotion is unavailable, while recognizing reduced gait and pelvic-control transfer.

---

## Overhead Carry Substitutions

Substitute with Farmer Carry when overhead mobility or shoulder tolerance is insufficient.

Substitute with Suitcase Carry when unilateral trunk control is desired without overhead loading.

Substitute with controlled overhead holds when walking space is unavailable, while recognizing reduced locomotor demand.

# Conflict Rules

CAS should not select multiple exercises that duplicate the same high-cost core function without a clear programming reason.

Examples of unnecessary duplication include:

- heavy Ab Wheel plus maximal Dragon Flag volume,
- high-volume Hanging Leg Raise plus maximal Dragon Flag work,
- heavy Farmer Carry plus heavy Suitcase Carry plus heavy Overhead Carry in the same short session,
- high-fatigue anterior-core work immediately before technically demanding heavy compound lifts,
- demanding Overhead Carry after substantial overhead pressing fatigue.

Complementary combinations may be valid when total cost remains controlled.

Examples include:

- Dead Bug plus Farmer Carry,
- Pallof Press plus Ab Wheel,
- Hollow Body Hold plus Suitcase Carry,
- Hanging Leg Raise plus light Overhead Carry,
- Dead Bug in preparation followed by a heavier anti-extension exercise.

---

# Engine Selection Hierarchy

When selecting among Core exercises, CAS should apply the following hierarchy:

1. Confirm pain-free eligibility.
2. Identify the required trunk function.
3. Identify whether the task should be isolated or integrated.
4. Match the exercise to the athlete’s technical level.
5. Check equipment and environmental constraints.
6. Check interaction with the rest of the session.
7. Estimate local and systemic fatigue.
8. Select the lowest-complexity exercise that fully satisfies the objective.
9. Define a technically valid stopping rule.
10. Record the reason for selection in the Decision Trace.

---

# Minimum Decision Trace Requirements

For any Core exercise selection, the Decision Trace should identify:

- the primary trunk function targeted,
- the reason this exercise was selected over the nearest alternative,
- athlete eligibility,
- relevant pain or mobility constraints,
- expected fatigue cost,
- interaction with compound lifting, combat practice or conditioning,
- progression or regression criteria,
- technical stopping conditions.

Example:

> Suitcase Carry selected instead of Pallof Press because the session objective requires anti-lateral-flexion and anti-rotation control during loaded gait, with secondary grip development. Athlete demonstrates adequate unilateral loading tolerance and no current low-back symptoms. Prescription remains submaximal to avoid interference with the following deadlift session.

# Decision Summary

The CAS Core library contains distinct exercise families rather than interchangeable abdominal exercises.

Dead Bug develops foundational control.

Hollow Body Hold develops static anterior-chain stiffness.

Ab Wheel develops dynamic anti-extension strength.

Pallof Press develops controlled anti-rotation capacity.

Hanging Leg Raise develops dynamic anterior-core strength in a hanging context.

Dragon Flag develops elite long-lever anti-extension strength.

Farmer Carry develops bilateral whole-body stiffness, grip and loaded gait.

Suitcase Carry develops asymmetrical trunk control, anti-lateral flexion and anti-rotation during locomotion.

Overhead Carry develops integrated shoulder stability, anti-extension control and loaded gait beneath an overhead implement.

CAS must select according to function, eligibility and total training cost.

The most advanced or most fatiguing exercise is not automatically the best choice.

The correct exercise is the one that produces the desired adaptation with the lowest unnecessary risk, redundancy and recovery cost.
