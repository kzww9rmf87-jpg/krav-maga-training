# SUITCASE CARRY

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Suitcase Carry is a unilateral loaded locomotion exercise used to develop anti-lateral-flexion strength, anti-rotation capacity, grip strength, pelvic control, gait integrity and whole-body force transmission under asymmetrical load.

Within CAS, it is primarily selected as a unilateral robustness and core-strength movement.

It requires the athlete to walk while holding a single implement at one side and resisting trunk lean, pelvic shift and rotational compensation.

---

# Philosophy

Combat athletes rarely receive or produce force in perfectly symmetrical positions.

They must maintain structure while one side of the body is loaded, pulled, compressed or rotated more than the other.

The Suitcase Carry develops this asymmetrical force-management capacity through a simple and measurable locomotor task.

CAS does not treat the exercise as a challenge to tolerate maximal trunk distortion.

The objective is to preserve vertical posture, level pelvic mechanics, controlled gait and stable breathing while the external load attempts to pull the athlete toward the loaded side.

# Execution Standard

1. Place one implement beside the loaded-side foot.

2. Stand with the feet approximately hip-width apart.

3. Hinge or squat to the handle while maintaining a neutral trunk.

4. Grip the implement firmly and create full-body tension before lifting.

5. Stand tall without leaning away from or toward the load.

6. Keep the ribcage stacked over the pelvis.

7. Maintain the pelvis level and the head neutral.

8. Keep the loaded shoulder stable without excessive elevation or depression.

9. Walk with controlled, natural steps.

10. Prevent the implement from swinging or contacting the leg repeatedly.

11. Resist lateral trunk flexion toward the load.

12. Resist rotation of the chest or pelvis.

13. Maintain even step length and foot pressure.

14. Continue breathing without losing abdominal tension.

15. Stop before grip failure, pelvic shift or gait deterioration.

16. Set the implement down under control using a hinge or squat pattern.

17. Repeat the prescribed work on the opposite side unless asymmetrical programming is intentionally required.

# Technical Failure Criteria

The set is terminated when one or more of the following occurs:

The trunk leans visibly toward or away from the load.

The pelvis drops or shifts repeatedly.

The shoulders rotate or become markedly uneven.

The lumbar spine moves into excessive extension.

The athlete loses secure grip.

The loaded shoulder elevates or depresses uncontrollably.

Step length becomes asymmetrical.

The feet cross the midline.

The implement swings excessively.

The implement repeatedly strikes the leg.

Walking speed becomes uncontrolled.

The athlete cannot maintain the prescribed path.

The athlete drops the implement unexpectedly.

Pain occurs in the spine, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hand, hip, knee or ankle.

# Safety Profile

Overall Safety Rating

High when load and walking environment are appropriate

Primary Risks

Grip failure and dropped implement

Foot injury from poor set-down

Excessive spinal asymmetry under inappropriate loading

Shoulder discomfort from poor scapular position

Trip or collision during loaded walking

Compensatory trunk lean under fatigue

Risk Control Measures

Use a clear walking lane.

Begin with conservative unilateral loading.

Avoid walking to complete grip failure.

Match volume between sides unless a specific asymmetry protocol is prescribed.

Use controlled turns.

Wear appropriate footwear.

Set the implement down under control.

Do not use damaged or unstable handles.

# Contraindications and Restrictions

Absolute Contraindications

Acute hand or finger injury preventing secure grip

Acute foot or ankle injury preventing stable walking

Acute spinal pain aggravated by asymmetrical loading

Inability to safely pick up or lower the implement

Relative Contraindications

Active shoulder irritation

Elbow or wrist pain

Recent abdominal or spinal injury

Severe balance impairment

Recent lower-limb injury

Marked uncontrolled scoliosis-related symptoms

High residual grip or trunk fatigue

Restriction Logic

The exercise is restricted, regressed or substituted when the athlete cannot maintain a vertical trunk, level pelvis, stable gait and secure grip throughout the prescription.

# Progression Model

Stage 1

Learn pickup, posture and set-down with a light unilateral load.

Stage 2

Develop static anti-lateral-flexion control through suitcase holds or marching in place.

Stage 3

Develop stable walking over short distances.

Stage 4

Increase load while preserving vertical trunk and level pelvic mechanics.

Stage 5

Increase total distance or work duration.

Stage 6

Develop heavy short-distance unilateral carry capacity.

Stage 7

Introduce density, thicker handles, offset loading or more advanced asymmetrical carry variations according to the target adaptation.

Progression Rule

Only one primary variable should be progressed aggressively at a time.

The athlete must preserve trunk alignment, pelvic control and gait symmetry before load or volume increases.

# Combat Transfer

Primary Transfer

Resistance to lateral trunk collapse during clinching and grappling

Trunk stiffness under asymmetrical external force

Pelvic control while stepping, striking or changing direction

Grip strength under unilateral load

Force transmission between one upper limb and the lower body

Secondary Transfer

Postural stability during off-balance positions

Ability to maintain structure while carrying or controlling an opponent asymmetrically

Shoulder stability under unilateral traction

Resilience during drags, holds and uneven contact

Transfer Limitation

The Suitcase Carry does not reproduce the speed, direction changes, gripping angles or technical complexity of combat actions.

It supports those qualities through general physical preparation and asymmetrical trunk-strength development.

# Substitution Logic

Substitute the Suitcase Carry when:

The athlete cannot grip the available implement safely.

The walking environment is unsafe or too restricted.

Asymmetrical spinal loading aggravates symptoms.

Lower-limb injury prevents normal gait.

The athlete cannot prevent lateral trunk lean.

Grip or trunk fatigue would interfere with a higher-priority session objective.

Preferred Substitution Hierarchy

1. Lighter Suitcase Carry

2. Suitcase Hold

3. Offset Trap-Bar Carry

4. Pallof Press

5. Side Plank

6. Farmer Carry when symmetrical loading is better tolerated

---

# CAS Selection Logic

CAS selects the Suitcase Carry when the session requires one or more of the following:

Anti-lateral-flexion strength

Anti-rotation capacity

Unilateral loaded carry capacity

Grip strength

Pelvic stability

Asymmetrical force transmission

Simple integrated trunk training

CAS deprioritizes the exercise when:

The athlete presents acute spinal, shoulder, hand or lower-limb symptoms.

The athlete cannot maintain vertical posture under unilateral load.

Grip must remain fresh for higher-priority technical work.

The environment does not allow safe loaded locomotion.

A symmetrical carry is more appropriate for the current objective.

A more specific anti-rotation or sport-specific task is required.

# Evidence Classification

Evidence Level

Moderate

Evidence Basis

Unilateral loaded carries are widely used in strength and conditioning to develop anti-lateral-flexion strength, grip capacity, trunk stiffness, pelvic control and general work capacity.

Direct evidence for combat-sport performance is limited compared with evidence for general trunk and strength qualities.

CAS therefore classifies the Suitcase Carry as a high-value general physical preparation exercise with strong mechanistic transfer to asymmetrical force management.

---

# Decision Summary

The Suitcase Carry is a robust and scalable unilateral exercise for developing anti-lateral-flexion strength, anti-rotation capacity, grip strength, pelvic control and loaded gait integrity.

CAS selects it when the athlete requires asymmetrical trunk robustness without excessive technical complexity.

Its value depends on strict control of trunk position, pelvic alignment, gait symmetry and grip.

The exercise should terminate before compensatory leaning, rotation or grip failure compromises safety and movement quality.
