# FARMER CARRY

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Farmer Carry is a loaded locomotion exercise used to develop grip strength, trunk stiffness, shoulder-girdle stability, gait integrity and whole-body force transmission under load.

Within CAS, it is primarily selected as a robustness and loaded-carry movement.

It requires the athlete to walk with heavy implements held at the sides while maintaining posture, breathing control and symmetrical movement.

---

# Philosophy

Combat athletes must repeatedly produce and absorb force while moving, carrying tension through the hands, shoulders, trunk, pelvis and lower limbs.

The Farmer Carry develops this integrated capacity without requiring complex technical execution.

CAS does not treat the exercise as a simple test of how much weight can be moved from one point to another.

The objective is to preserve gait quality, trunk organization, grip integrity and shoulder position throughout the prescribed distance or time.

# Execution Standard

1. Place two matched implements beside the feet.

2. Stand between the implements with the feet approximately hip-width apart.

3. Hinge or squat to the handles while maintaining a neutral trunk.

4. Grip both implements firmly and create full-body tension before lifting.

5. Stand tall without excessive lumbar extension or shoulder elevation.

6. Keep the ribcage stacked over the pelvis.

7. Maintain the head in a neutral position and look forward.

8. Walk with controlled, natural steps.

9. Prevent the implements from swinging excessively.

10. Keep the trunk vertical without leaning to either side.

11. Maintain even step length and foot pressure.

12. Continue breathing without losing abdominal tension.

13. Stop the set before grip failure, trunk collapse or gait deterioration.

14. Set the implements down under control using a hinge or squat pattern.

---

# Key Coaching Cues

Stand tall.

# Technical Failure Criteria

The set is terminated when one or more of the following occurs:

The athlete loses secure grip.

The shoulders elevate uncontrollably.

The trunk leans repeatedly to one side.

The lumbar spine moves into excessive extension.

The athlete shortens one step markedly relative to the other.

The feet cross the midline.

The implements collide repeatedly with the legs.

The implements swing excessively.

Walking speed becomes uncontrolled.

The athlete cannot maintain the prescribed path.

The athlete drops an implement unexpectedly.

Pain occurs in the spine, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hand, hip, knee or ankle.

# Safety Profile

Overall Safety Rating

High when load and walking environment are appropriate

Primary Risks

Grip failure and dropped implements

Foot injury from poor set-down

Excessive spinal compression under inappropriate loading

Shoulder discomfort from poor scapular position

Trip or collision during loaded walking

Risk Control Measures

Use clear walking lanes.

Keep the load symmetrical unless a unilateral variation is intentionally prescribed.

Avoid walking to complete grip failure.

Use controlled turns.

Wear appropriate footwear.

Set implements down under control.

Do not use damaged or unstable handles.

---

# Contraindications and Restrictions

Absolute Contraindications

Acute hand or finger injury preventing secure grip

Acute foot or ankle injury preventing stable walking

Acute spinal pain aggravated by axial loading

Inability to safely pick up or lower the implements

Relative Contraindications

Active shoulder irritation

Elbow or wrist pain

Uncontrolled hypertension or cardiovascular limitation under heavy loaded carries

Severe balance impairment

Recent lower-limb injury

High residual grip fatigue

Restriction Logic

The exercise is restricted, regressed or substituted when the athlete cannot maintain a secure grip, stable posture and safe gait throughout the prescription.

# Progression Model

Stage 1

Learn pickup, posture and set-down with light symmetrical loads.

Stage 2

Develop stable walking over short distances.

Stage 3

Increase load while preserving gait quality.

Stage 4

Increase total distance or work duration.

Stage 5

Develop heavy short-distance carry capacity.

Stage 6

Introduce density, thicker handles or advanced unilateral variations according to the target adaptation.

Progression Rule

Only one primary variable should be progressed aggressively at a time.

The athlete must preserve secure grip, vertical trunk position and controlled foot placement before load or volume increases.

# Combat Transfer

Primary Transfer

Grip strength for clinching, grappling and equipment control

Trunk stiffness during contact and locomotion

Shoulder stability under traction

Postural control while moving under external resistance

Secondary Transfer

General work capacity

Ability to maintain structure while fatigued

Force transmission between the upper and lower body

Resilience during loaded carries, drags and opponent-control tasks

Transfer Limitation

The Farmer Carry does not reproduce sport-specific gripping angles, rotational actions or technical combat skills.

It supports those qualities through general physical preparation.

# Substitution Logic

Substitute the Farmer Carry when:

The athlete cannot grip the available implements safely.

The walking environment is unsafe or too restricted.

Axial loading aggravates symptoms.

Lower-limb injury prevents normal gait.

Grip fatigue would interfere with a higher-priority session objective.

Preferred Substitution Hierarchy

1. Trap-Bar Carry

2. Heavy Dumbbell or Kettlebell Carry

3. Static Farmer Hold

4. Sled March

5. Anti-Lateral-Flexion Core Exercise plus Separate Grip Work

---

# CAS Selection Logic

CAS selects the Farmer Carry when the session requires one or more of the following:

Loaded carry capacity

Grip strength

Whole-body robustness

Anti-lateral-flexion strength

Shoulder-girdle stability

Postural endurance

Simple integrated strength work

CAS deprioritizes the exercise when:

Grip must remain fresh for higher-priority technical work.

The athlete presents acute hand, shoulder, spinal or lower-limb symptoms.

The environment does not allow safe loaded locomotion.

The athlete cannot safely lift or lower the available implements.

A more specific adaptation requires a different exercise.

# Evidence Classification

Evidence Level

Moderate

Evidence Basis

Loaded carries are widely used in strength and conditioning to develop grip strength, trunk stiffness, postural endurance and general work capacity.

Direct evidence for combat-sport performance is more limited than evidence for general strength qualities.

CAS therefore classifies the Farmer Carry as a high-value general physical preparation exercise with strong mechanistic transfer rather than as a sport-specific exercise.

---

# Decision Summary

The Farmer Carry is a robust, scalable and technically accessible exercise for developing grip strength, trunk stiffness, shoulder stability and loaded locomotion.

CAS selects it when the athlete requires integrated whole-body robustness without excessive movement complexity.

Its value depends on strict control of posture, gait, grip and load selection.

The exercise should terminate before grip failure or movement degradation compromises safety.
