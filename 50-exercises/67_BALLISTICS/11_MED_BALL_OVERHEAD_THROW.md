# Purpose

The Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw develops whole-body ballistic power through rapid extension of the hips, knees, ankles, trunk and shoulders.

Within CAS, it is used when the athlete must improve the ability to project force upward or forward through a coordinated extension pattern.

The exercise is particularly useful for developing:

- total-body extension power,
- lower-to-upper-body sequencing,
- overhead force projection,
- rate of force development,
- and explosive hip-to-hand transfer.

It must not be selected merely because it involves a medicine ball.

Its value depends on whether extension-dominant ballistic power and overhead projection match the intended adaptation.

# Movement Description

The athlete holds a medicine ball with both hands and brings it behind or above the head according to the selected variation.

From a stable stance, the athlete rapidly extends through the lower body and trunk while projecting the ball overhead.

The ball may be thrown:

- vertically,
- forward,
- backward,
- or at an upward angle.

The athlete accelerates through release and finishes in a balanced position.

---

# Starting Position

The athlete should begin with:

- the medicine ball held securely with both hands,
- feet in a stable stance,
- knees and hips slightly flexed,
- ribs stacked over the pelvis,
- shoulders positioned pain-free overhead,
- trunk braced,
- and eyes directed toward the intended target area.

The throwing lane must be clear.

---

# Execution

1. Hold the medicine ball securely.
2. Establish a stable stance.
3. Bring the ball overhead or slightly behind the head.
4. Load the hips and knees if the variation requires lower-body contribution.
5. Brace the trunk.
6. Drive forcefully through the floor.
7. Extend the hips, knees and ankles.
8. Transfer force through the trunk and shoulders.
9. Accelerate the ball through the full movement.
10. Release the ball overhead in the intended direction.
11. Finish in balance.
12. Reset fully before the next repetition.

---

# Key Technical Cues

- Drive through the floor.
- Extend from the hips.
- Throw through the fingertips.
- Accelerate through release.
- Keep the ribs controlled.
- Do not arch excessively.
- Finish tall and balanced.
- Reset before repeating.

# Lower-Body Contribution

The Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw generally uses high lower-body contribution.

The athlete should generate force through:

- ankle extension,
- knee extension,
- hip extension,
- trunk transfer,
- shoulder flexion,
- and final release.

The movement should not become an isolated shoulder throw unless the selected variation intentionally reduces lower-body involvement.

---

# Technical Complexity

Low to Moderate

The basic movement is simple, but quality depends on:

- overhead mobility,
- correct sequencing,
- safe release angle,
- appropriate medicine-ball mass,
- and controlled finish.

Backward throws require greater spatial awareness and should be considered more complex.

---

# Prerequisites

The athlete should demonstrate:

- pain-free overhead shoulder motion,
- adequate thoracic extension,
- safe medicine-ball handling,
- stable trunk control,
- competent hip and knee extension,
- and ability to finish in balance.

---

# Eligibility Criteria

The Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw is eligible when:

- whole-body extension power is a target,
- overhead projection is appropriate,
- shoulder and thoracic readiness are adequate,
- the throwing area is safe,
- the athlete can sequence the movement correctly,
- and high-speed intent can be maintained.

---

# Ineligibility Criteria

The exercise is not eligible when the athlete presents:

- acute shoulder pain,
- elbow pain,
- wrist pain,
- cervical pain,
- thoracic pain,
- low-back pain aggravated by extension,
- inability to reach overhead safely,
- unsafe throwing space,
- or severe fatigue that suppresses velocity.

---

# Relative Contraindications

Use caution when the athlete has:

- limited shoulder flexion,
- thoracic stiffness,
- anterior shoulder irritation,
- lumbar extension sensitivity,
- or high overhead-pressing volume.

The engine should reduce ball mass, range or lower-body contribution when necessary.

---

# Absolute Contraindications

Do not prescribe the exercise when there is:

- acute upper-limb injury,
- acute spinal injury,
- uncontrolled neurological weakness,
- inability to throw overhead safely,
- or an unsuitable throwing environment.

---

# Common Errors

## Using a Ball That Is Too Heavy

The athlete moves slowly and cannot accelerate through release.

Risk:

- reduced ballistic effect,
- poor sequencing,
- excessive shoulder strain.

Correction:

- reduce medicine-ball mass.

## Excessive Lumbar Extension

The athlete arches the lower back instead of transferring force through the whole body.

Risk:

- increased spinal stress,
- poor force transfer.

Correction:

- maintain rib control and use the hips.

## Arm-Dominant Throw

The athlete relies mainly on the shoulders and elbows.

Risk:

- reduced total output,
- poor lower-to-upper-body sequencing.

Correction:

- cue force from the ground upward.

## Early Arm Action

The arms begin throwing before the hips and knees extend.

Risk:

- poor kinetic-chain sequencing,
- reduced ball velocity.

Correction:

- delay arm acceleration until lower-body force is transferring upward.

## Inconsistent Release Angle

The ball travels unpredictably.

Risk:

- unsafe projection,
- poor standardization.

Correction:

- define a clear target and consistent release path.

## Loss of Balance

The athlete steps uncontrollably or falls backward after release.

Risk:

- unsafe landing,
- poor force direction.

Correction:

- reduce load or range and finish under control.

## Excessive Countermovement

The athlete descends too deeply before throwing.

Risk:

- slower movement,
- altered exercise objective.

Correction:

- use a short, rapid loading phase.

# Coaching Priorities

1. Select the correct ball mass.
2. Establish pain-free overhead range.
3. Use a short loading phase.
4. Drive from the legs and hips.
5. Sequence force from the ground upward.
6. Maintain rib control.
7. Accelerate through release.
8. Preserve the intended force vector.
9. Finish in balance.
10. Stop before velocity declines.

# Prescription Variables

## Repetitions

Typical range:

- 2 to 5 repetitions for maximal power,
- 4 to 8 repetitions for repeated ballistic effort.

## Sets

Typical range:

- 3 to 6 sets.

## Rest

Typical range:

- 90 seconds to 4 minutes.

## Ball Mass

Typical range:

- light to moderate medicine ball,
- generally 2 to 6 kilograms depending on athlete and variation.

The selected mass must preserve acceleration and clean release.

## Throwing Direction

Options include:

- vertical,
- forward,
- backward,
- upward diagonal.

---

# Maximum-Power Prescription

Typical prescription:

- 3 to 6 sets,
- 2 to 5 repetitions,
- maximal intent,
- 2 to 4 minutes rest,
- full reset between repetitions.

---

# Strength-Speed Prescription

Typical prescription:

- 3 to 5 sets,
- 3 to 6 repetitions,
- moderate ball mass,
- 90 seconds to 3 minutes rest,
- complete technical control.

---

# Repeated-Effort Prescription

Typical prescription:

- 2 to 4 sets,
- 5 to 8 repetitions,
- lighter ball,
- controlled rhythm,
- no visible velocity loss.

Primary role:

- vertical ballistic power.

# Backward Overhead Throw

Primary role:

- maximal posterior-chain and extension power.

Primary characteristics:

- high total-body output,
- backward projection,
- greater spatial-awareness demand.

This variation requires a fully clear area behind the athlete.

---

Primary role:

- reduce lower-body contribution.

Primary characteristics:

- greater trunk and shoulder emphasis,
- lower total output,
- useful technical regression.

# Progressions

Progressions include:

1. improved release speed,
2. improved projection distance,
3. slightly greater ball mass,
4. greater lower-body contribution,
5. faster countermovement,
6. forward projection,
7. backward projection,
8. step-in variation,
9. reactive sequencing,
10. repeated high-quality throws.

---

# Regressions

Regressions include:

- lighter ball,
- reduced range,
- kneeling variation,
- shorter projection distance,
- reduced countermovement,
- submaximal technical throws,
- forward rather than backward projection.

---

# Recommended Progression Sequence

1. Light kneeling Overhead Throw.
2. Standing vertical throw.
3. Standing forward Overhead Throw.
4. Greater projection distance.
5. Slightly heavier ball.
6. Faster loading phase.
7. Step-in Overhead Throw.
8. Backward Overhead Throw.

Low to Moderate

## Local Fatigue

Low to Moderate

- shoulders,
- triceps,
- trunk,
- hips,
- and legs.

## Neurological Fatigue

Moderate

## Connective-Tissue Stress

Low to Moderate

Stress may accumulate in:

- shoulders,
- elbows,
- spine,
- hips,

---

# Recovery Cost

Recovery cost is generally low to moderate.

It increases with:

- heavier balls,
- repeated high-output sets,
- backward throws,
- excessive volume,
- and concurrent overhead pressing or striking work.

---

# Session Placement

The Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw is usually placed:

- after the warm-up,
- after movement preparation,
- before heavy strength work,
- before hypertrophy work,
- and before high-fatigue conditioning.

It may be paired with:

- deadlifts,
- squats,
- or push presses in contrast training.

It should not be placed after severe fatigue when power quality is the objective.

---

# Weekly Placement

Typical frequency:

- 1 to 3 times per week.

Higher frequency may be appropriate when:

- volume is low,
- ball mass is light,
- shoulder readiness is good,
- and technical quality remains high.

---

# Interaction With Other Training

## Squats and Deadlifts

The exercise may complement lower-body strength work by expressing extension power at high velocity.

## Push Press

Both exercises develop whole-body force transfer.

The Overhead Throw allows release, while the Push Press requires control of the implement.

## Overhead Pressing

High pressing fatigue may reduce shoulder speed and release quality.

## Striking

The exercise supports whole-body explosiveness but should not create excessive shoulder fatigue before technical striking.

## Grappling

The movement may support explosive extension and projection with relatively low interference when volume is controlled.

The Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw supports combat performance by improving:

# Substitution Logic

## Substitute Reverse Throw When

- posterior-chain power is the primary objective,
- backward projection is safe,
- or maximal extension output is desired.

## Substitute Medicine-Ball Slam When

- downward force projection is required,
- overhead release into open space is unavailable,
- or a simpler target is preferred.

## Substitute Push Press When

- the athlete should retain control of the load,
- throwing space is unavailable,
- or strength-speed is preferred.

## Substitute Scoop Toss When

- rotational or diagonal projection is the main target,
- greater hip rotation is desired.

## Substitute Jump Shrug When

- vertical extension power is required without overhead throwing,
- or medicine-ball equipment is unavailable.

# Progression Criteria

The athlete may progress when:

- the movement remains fast,
- lower-to-upper-body sequencing is consistent,
- the release direction is accurate,
- the trunk remains controlled,
- the athlete finishes in balance,
- no pain occurs,
- and there is no meaningful velocity loss.

---

# Regression Criteria

The exercise should be regressed when:

- the throw becomes slow,
- the athlete arches excessively,
- arm action dominates,
- release direction becomes inconsistent,
- balance deteriorates,
- shoulder or spinal discomfort appears,
- or fatigue reduces output.

# Safety Rules

- Use an appropriate medicine ball.
- Keep the throwing area clear.
- Verify the wall or open-space setup.
- Do not use a damaged ball.
- Maintain safe distance from other athletes.
- Do not perform backward throws without checking the area.
- Stop before technical failure.
- Retrieve the ball under control.
- Terminate immediately if sharp pain, dizziness, numbness or sudden weakness occurs.

---

# CAS Engine Rules

The CAS Engine may select the Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw when:

- whole-body extension power is the intended adaptation,
- overhead ballistic projection is appropriate,
- shoulder and thoracic readiness are adequate,
- the ball mass preserves speed,
- the athlete can finish in balance,
- and total overhead and striking volume remains recoverable.

The engine must avoid selecting it when:

- horizontal or rotational power is the actual target,
- overhead mobility is insufficient,
- the athlete cannot control lumbar extension,
- or the selected ball mass suppresses velocity.

---

# Decision Trace Requirements

When the Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw is selected, the Decision Trace should identify:

- the targeted force vector,
- the intended whole-body power adaptation,
- the selected throwing direction,
- the ball mass,
- the prescribed repetitions and sets,
- the expected limiting factor,
- the interaction with lower-body strength, overhead work and combat practice,
- relevant shoulder, trunk or spinal constraints,
- and the progression criterion.

Example:

> Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw selected to develop whole-body extension power and lower-to-upper-body sequencing. A moderate medicine ball is used to preserve acceleration and clean overhead release. Low-repetition sets are placed before strength work, with the set terminated at the first clear decline in projection speed.

---

# Summary

The Medicine-Ball Overhead Throw is a low-to-moderate-complexity ballistic exercise that develops whole-body extension power.

Its primary value lies in improving:

- lower-to-upper-body sequencing,
- hip extension power,
- overhead projection,
- rate of force development,
- and explosive intent.

Within CAS, it is appropriate when the athlete needs extension-dominant ballistic power and possesses adequate overhead mobility and trunk control.

It must be programmed with a ball light enough to preserve speed and with a throwing direction that remains safe, consistent and relevant to the objective.
