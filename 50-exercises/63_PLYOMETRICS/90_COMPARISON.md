# PLYOMETRIC EXERCISE COMPARISON

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document compares the Plyometric exercises currently defined in CAS and clarifies the conditions under which each exercise should be selected.

Its purpose is not to rank exercises from best to worst.

Its purpose is to help the CAS Engine distinguish between exercises that may appear similar but create different force vectors, landing demands, unilateral requirements, elastic demands, technical constraints and recovery costs.

The comparison covers:

- Box Jump
- Depth Jump
- Broad Jump
- Knee Jump
- Lateral Bound
- Single-Leg Hop
- Split Squat Jump

---

# Core Principle

> CAS selects the plyometric exercise that best matches the required force vector, contraction profile, landing strategy, unilateral demand, athlete readiness and fatigue budget.

Plyometric training is not treated as one single quality.

The exercise must be chosen according to the dominant requirement:

- vertical projection,
- horizontal projection,
- lateral projection,
- reactive strength,
- concentric explosiveness,
- unilateral propulsion,
- alternating-limb power,
- deceleration,
- landing control,
- elastic stiffness,
- or combat-specific stance transfer.

# Force-Vector Comparison

## Vertical Projection

Primary options:

1. Box Jump
2. Depth Jump
3. Knee Jump
4. Split Squat Jump
5. Single-Leg Hop when performed vertically

### Selection Distinction

The Box Jump is selected when the objective is maximal vertical intent with a comparatively reduced landing impact because the athlete lands on an elevated surface.

The Depth Jump is selected when the objective is rapid force reversal after a drop and the athlete has sufficient eccentric strength, landing skill and reactive capacity.

The Knee Jump is selected when the objective is explosive hip extension from a kneeling start with minimal contribution from an initial countermovement.

The Split Squat Jump is selected when vertical force must be produced from a staggered stance while the limbs alternate.

The Single-Leg Hop is selected when vertical projection must be expressed and absorbed by one limb.

---

## Horizontal Projection

Primary options:

1. Broad Jump
2. Single-Leg Hop when performed horizontally

Secondary options:

- Lateral Bound for diagonal projection
- Split Squat Jump when performed with a forward emphasis

### Selection Distinction

The Broad Jump is the default bilateral horizontal-power exercise.

The horizontal Single-Leg Hop increases limb-specific propulsion, pelvic stability and landing difficulty.

CAS selects the Broad Jump when bilateral force production and whole-body horizontal projection are the priority.

CAS selects the Single-Leg Hop when asymmetry assessment, unilateral force production or single-leg deceleration is required.

---

## Lateral Projection

Primary option:

Lateral Bound

Secondary options:

- Lateral Single-Leg Hop
- Diagonal Broad Jump variations

The Lateral Bound is the default CAS choice when frontal-plane power, side-to-side deceleration and lateral hip control are dominant objectives.

---

# Stretch-Shortening-Cycle Comparison

## Predominantly Concentric Emphasis

Primary option:

Knee Jump

The Knee Jump minimizes the contribution of a normal standing countermovement and emphasizes rapid force production from a relatively static start.

It is selected when the objective is starting strength rather than reactive strength.

---

## Slow Stretch-Shortening Cycle

Primary options:

- Box Jump
- Broad Jump
- Lateral Bound
- Split Squat Jump when performed with a visible countermovement

These exercises generally allow a longer transition between eccentric loading and propulsion.

They are appropriate for developing ballistic power before introducing the highest reactive demands.

---

## Fast Stretch-Shortening Cycle

Primary options:

1. Depth Jump
2. Repeated Single-Leg Hop
3. Low-amplitude Split Squat Jump variations

The Depth Jump produces the greatest requirement for rapid eccentric-to-concentric transition.

Repeated Single-Leg Hops create high ankle, calf and Achilles stiffness demands with one-limb force acceptance.

Low-amplitude Split Squat Jumps may be used for rhythmic reactivity, but they should not be treated as equivalent to Depth Jumps.

---

# Landing-Demand Comparison

## Lowest Relative Landing Impact

Box Jump

The elevated landing surface reduces the distance the athlete falls after take-off.

This does not make the exercise risk-free.

Landing on a box still requires accurate foot placement, trunk control and sufficient box height selection.

---

## Moderate Landing Demand

- Knee Jump when performed to a stable standing landing
- Split Squat Jump with controlled amplitude
- Lateral Bound with submaximal distance

---

## High Landing Demand

- Broad Jump
- Lateral Bound at maximal distance
- Single-Leg Hop

These exercises require substantial deceleration because the athlete must arrest forward, lateral or unilateral momentum.

---

## Very High Landing and Reactive Demand

Depth Jump

The athlete must first absorb the force created by the drop and then immediately redirect force into a jump.

CAS does not select the Depth Jump merely because the athlete is strong or experienced.

It requires specific readiness for rapid force acceptance and reversal.

---

# Unilateral-Demand Comparison

## Bilateral Dominant

- Box Jump
- Depth Jump
- Broad Jump
- Knee Jump

These exercises may still reveal asymmetries, but they do not isolate one limb as clearly as unilateral options.

---

## Alternating Unilateral

Split Squat Jump

The exercise requires repeated transfer from one staggered stance to the opposite stance.

It emphasizes alternating-limb propulsion, interlimb timing and the ability to receive force in a combat-relevant base.

---

## Single-Leg Dominant

- Single-Leg Hop
- Lateral Bound

The Single-Leg Hop may occur in the sagittal, frontal or multidirectional plane.

The Lateral Bound is usually performed from one leg to the opposite leg and places a stronger frontal-plane emphasis on hip and pelvic control.

---

# Exercise-by-Exercise Selection Profile

## Box Jump

### Select When

- vertical power is required,
- maximal intent is desired without a full ground-level landing from jump height,
- the athlete can take off and land quietly,
- confidence in ballistic jumping must be developed,
- a lower-volume high-quality power exercise is needed.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the athlete chooses excessive box height,
- landing occurs in a deep squat because of insufficient jump height,
- the knees collapse inward,
- foot placement is unreliable,
- fatigue impairs coordination,
- the athlete steps or falls backward from the box.

### Closest Alternatives

Broad Jump for horizontal projection.

Knee Jump for concentric starting strength.

Depth Jump for advanced reactive strength.

---

## Depth Jump

### Select When

- advanced reactive strength is the objective,
- the athlete has demonstrated excellent landing mechanics,
- eccentric strength is sufficient,
- ground-contact time must be minimized,
- high neural intensity is acceptable,
- readiness is high and lower-limb tissues are well recovered.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the athlete collapses into a deep landing,
- ground contact becomes prolonged,
- the knees or ankles lose alignment,
- Achilles, patellar tendon or joint symptoms are present,
- lower-body fatigue is significant,
- foundational bilateral jumping has not been mastered.

### Closest Alternatives

Box Jump for lower landing impact and simpler vertical power.

Low Drop Landing for force-acceptance practice.

Repeated Pogos or low-amplitude hops for lower-level reactive work.

---

## Broad Jump

### Select When

- horizontal force production is required,
- acceleration qualities are targeted,
- whole-body projection is desired,
- bilateral landing and deceleration can be coached,
- a field-friendly maximal-power assessment is needed.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the athlete lands with the trunk collapsing forward,
- the knees collapse inward,
- balance is lost backward or sideways,
- landing distance is prioritized over control,
- lower-limb pain is present,
- the available surface is slippery or too short.

### Closest Alternatives

Box Jump for vertical projection.

Single-Leg Hop for unilateral horizontal power.

Lateral Bound for frontal-plane projection.

---

## Knee Jump

### Select When

- explosive hip extension from a static or near-static start is required,
- starting strength is the main target,
- contribution from a normal standing countermovement should be reduced,
- the athlete has sufficient hip, knee and trunk control,
- a novel but purposeful ballistic stimulus is appropriate.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the athlete cannot clear the feet safely,
- the toes or knees strike the floor aggressively,
- excessive lumbar extension replaces hip drive,
- the landing is unstable,
- kneeling causes discomfort,
- the movement is treated as a trick rather than a controlled power exercise.

### Closest Alternatives

Box Jump for standing vertical power.

Tall-Kneeling Jump to a raised surface for reduced difficulty.

Medicine-Ball Scoop Throw for a lower-impact hip-extension power option.

---

## Lateral Bound

### Select When

- lateral power is required,
- frontal-plane deceleration is a priority,
- hip and pelvic control must be trained,
- combat footwork or change-of-direction support is desired,
- unilateral side-to-side force transfer is appropriate.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the landing leg collapses inward,
- the athlete cannot stabilize before the next repetition,
- trunk rotation replaces lateral projection,
- the athlete lands on a rigid straight leg,
- ankle, knee or groin symptoms are present,
- bound distance exceeds landing control.

### Closest Alternatives

Single-Leg Hop for sagittal or multidirectional unilateral power.

Broad Jump for bilateral horizontal projection.

Skater Step for a lower-impact regression.

---

## Single-Leg Hop

### Select When

- unilateral propulsion is required,
- limb-specific elastic capacity is targeted,
- ankle stiffness and lower-leg reactivity are priorities,
- asymmetry must be identified or addressed,
- sport actions depend on one-leg take-off or landing.

### Avoid or Regress When

- landing alignment is inconsistent,
- the athlete cannot control pelvis and trunk position,
- Achilles, calf, patellar tendon or foot symptoms are present,
- repeated contact quality deteriorates,
- the athlete lacks foundational bilateral landing skill.

### Closest Alternatives

Broad Jump for bilateral horizontal power.

Lateral Bound for side-to-side unilateral projection.

Split Squat Jump for alternating unilateral power with a staggered stance.

---

## Split Squat Jump

### Select When

- alternating unilateral power is required,
- explosive stance switching is a priority,
- propulsion from a staggered base is desired,
- repeated vertical power must be integrated with coordination,
- combat-stance transfer is relevant.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the feet cross or land too narrowly,
- the knees collapse inward,
- the athlete loses pelvic alignment,
- landing noise and impact increase across repetitions,
- the rear knee repeatedly strikes the floor,
- conditioning fatigue replaces power quality.

### Closest Alternatives

Box Jump for bilateral vertical power.

Single-Leg Hop for one-limb propulsion without airborne limb exchange.

Reverse Lunge to Knee Drive for a lower-impact power regression.

---

# Difficulty Continuums

## Bilateral Power Continuum

Countermovement Jump

↓

Box Jump

↓

Broad Jump

↓

Depth Jump

The sequence is not purely linear because force vector and reactive demand differ.

CAS progresses only when the required landing and force-production qualities are present.

---

## Unilateral Power Continuum

Skater Step

↓

Controlled Lateral Bound

↓

Single-Leg Hop

↓

Repeated Multidirectional Single-Leg Hop

---

## Staggered-Stance Power Continuum

Reverse Lunge to Knee Drive

↓

Low-Amplitude Split Squat Jump

↓

Maximal Split Squat Jump

↓

Repeated Reactive Split Squat Jump

---

## Reactive-Strength Continuum

Pogo Jump

↓

Repeated Low Hops

↓

Low-Box Depth Jump

↓

Higher-Intensity Depth Jump

CAS does not increase drop height automatically.

The progression is based on contact quality, stiffness, jump output and the athlete’s ability to avoid collapse.

# Recovery-Cost Comparison

## Low to Moderate Recovery Cost

- Box Jump at low volume
- Knee Jump at low volume

These exercises can provide a strong neural stimulus without the highest total eccentric cost when performed with strict repetition limits.

---

## Moderate Recovery Cost

- Broad Jump
- Controlled Split Squat Jump
- Submaximal Lateral Bound

---

## Moderate to High Recovery Cost

- Single-Leg Hop
- Maximal Lateral Bound
- Repeated Split Squat Jump

---

## High to Very High Recovery Cost

Depth Jump

The Depth Jump should be treated as a high-intensity neural and tissue-loading exercise even when total repetition count is low.

# Readiness-Based Selection

## Low Readiness

Preferred options:

- low-volume Box Jump to a conservative height,
- submaximal Broad Jump only if landing quality is excellent,
- low-amplitude Lateral Bound,
- technical landing drills.

Avoid:

- Depth Jump,
- repeated maximal Single-Leg Hop,
- high-volume Split Squat Jump,
- maximal-distance bounds.

---

## Moderate Readiness

Preferred options:

- Box Jump,
- Broad Jump,
- Knee Jump,
- controlled Lateral Bound,
- low-volume Split Squat Jump.

Single-Leg Hops may be included when the athlete has demonstrated adequate tissue tolerance and landing control.

---

## High Readiness

Eligible options:

- all exercises,
- including Depth Jump and advanced repeated Single-Leg Hop variations,
- provided no hard constraint or recovery conflict is present.

High readiness does not override technical eligibility.

# Combat-Sport Transfer Comparison

## Highest General Lower-Body Power Transfer

- Box Jump
- Broad Jump

These exercises develop high-intent bilateral force production in simple patterns that are easy to dose and monitor.

---

## Highest Reactive Transfer

Depth Jump

This exercise supports rapid force reversal relevant to explosive re-acceleration, but only when the athlete is sufficiently prepared.

---

## Highest Stance-Switching Transfer

Split Squat Jump

The alternating staggered landing has clear transfer to stance changes, lunging actions, entries and rapid repositioning.

---

## Highest Lateral-Movement Transfer

Lateral Bound

This exercise supports lateral propulsion, evasive footwork, angle creation and frontal-plane braking.

---

## Highest Single-Leg Transfer

Single-Leg Hop

It develops limb-specific stiffness, propulsion and landing control relevant to kicking, stepping, shooting entries and recovery from unstable positions.

---

## Highest Starting-Strength Transfer

Knee Jump

It develops rapid hip extension from a reduced-preload position and may support explosive initiation from kneeling or compromised bases.

---

# Substitution Logic

## Box Jump Substitutions

Use Broad Jump when horizontal projection is preferred.

Use Countermovement Jump when no box is available.

Use Medicine-Ball Throw when lower-limb landing stress must be reduced.

---

## Depth Jump Substitutions

Use Low Drop Landing when force acceptance must be trained without immediate re-jump.

Use Pogo Jump when ankle stiffness and short ground contact are needed at lower intensity.

Use Box Jump when vertical power is required but reactive readiness is insufficient.

---

## Broad Jump Substitutions

Use Horizontal Single-Leg Hop for unilateral emphasis.

Use Box Jump for vertical emphasis.

Use Sled Sprint or resisted acceleration when horizontal force is required without jump landing.

---

## Knee Jump Substitutions

Use Tall-Kneeling Jump to Box for easier foot clearance.

Use Box Jump from a static start for standing concentric power.

Use Medicine-Ball Scoop Throw for explosive hip extension with lower landing demand.

---

## Lateral Bound Substitutions

Use Skater Step for a lower-impact regression.

Use Lateral Single-Leg Hop for a more elastic repeated-contact emphasis.

Use Shuffle-to-Stick for lateral deceleration without maximal airborne projection.

---

## Single-Leg Hop Substitutions

Use Split Squat Jump for alternating unilateral power.

Use Lateral Bound for frontal-plane propulsion.

Use Step-Up Jump for a more supported unilateral power option.

---

## Split Squat Jump Substitutions

Use Reverse Lunge to Knee Drive for reduced impact.

Use Single-Leg Hop for non-alternating unilateral power.

Use Box Jump for bilateral vertical power.

# Conflict Rules

CAS must not select high-intensity plyometrics when:

- the athlete reports acute lower-limb pain,
- landing mechanics are unsafe,
- the training surface is unsuitable,
- substantial lower-body fatigue is present,
- readiness is low,
- heavy eccentric lower-body work has already created excessive tissue stress,
- total weekly jump volume exceeds the athlete’s tolerance,
- or the required exercise cannot be performed with maximal intent and controlled landings.

Depth Jumps conflict strongly with:

- unresolved Achilles or patellar tendon symptoms,
- recent high-volume sprinting,
- high-fatigue squat or deadlift sessions,
- severe calf soreness,
- and poor reactive landing mechanics.

Single-Leg Hops and Lateral Bounds conflict strongly with:

- uncontrolled frontal-plane knee motion,
- unstable ankle mechanics,
- and meaningful interlimb asymmetry that worsens under speed.

Split Squat Jumps conflict strongly with:

- poor staggered-stance balance,
- crossed or excessively narrow foot placement,
- and fatigue-driven conditioning prescriptions.

# Engine Selection Hierarchy

The CAS Engine should apply the following hierarchy:

1. Check safety and hard constraints.
2. Identify the required force vector.
3. Identify whether the target is concentric power, slow stretch-shortening-cycle power or fast reactive strength.
4. Determine bilateral, unilateral or alternating-unilateral requirement.
5. Evaluate landing and deceleration capacity.
6. Evaluate tendon and eccentric-load tolerance.
7. Check technical eligibility.
8. Check readiness and recovery budget.
9. Select the lowest-complexity exercise that fully satisfies the objective.
10. Set volume according to quality, not fatigue accumulation.

---

# Minimum Decision Trace Requirements

When selecting a Plyometric exercise, the Decision Trace must identify:

- the required power quality,
- the dominant force vector,
- the required contact profile,
- whether bilateral or unilateral loading is needed,
- the athlete’s landing eligibility,
- the principal safety and recovery checks,
- why the selected exercise is preferable to the closest alternatives,
- and the condition that would trigger regression, substitution or termination.

Example:

> Split Squat Jump selected because the session requires alternating unilateral vertical power from a staggered stance. Box Jump was not selected because bilateral projection would not satisfy the stance-switching objective. Single-Leg Hop was not selected because the target is rapid interlimb exchange rather than isolated one-limb propulsion. Volume is restricted to high-quality repetitions because landing alignment is the governing termination criterion.

---

# Decision Summary

Box Jump is the default choice for controlled vertical power.

Depth Jump is reserved for advanced reactive strength.

Broad Jump is the default choice for bilateral horizontal projection.

Knee Jump emphasizes concentric starting strength from kneeling.

Lateral Bound is the primary frontal-plane power and deceleration exercise.

Single-Leg Hop is the primary unilateral propulsion and elastic-control exercise.

Split Squat Jump is the primary alternating staggered-stance power exercise.

> The best plyometric exercise is the one that produces the required force rapidly while preserving landing quality, tissue tolerance and the athlete’s ability to repeat high-intent efforts safely.
