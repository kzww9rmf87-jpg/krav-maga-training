# DEPTH JUMP

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Depth Jump is an advanced bilateral lower-body plyometric exercise used to develop reactive strength, stretch-shortening-cycle efficiency, rapid force production and the ability to tolerate and redirect high landing forces.

Within CAS, it is primarily selected as a high-intensity reactive power exercise.

The athlete steps from an elevated platform, lands briefly on the floor and immediately performs a maximal vertical jump.

The objective is not simply to jump after dropping from a box.

The objective is to absorb impact rapidly, maintain effective lower-limb stiffness and convert the landing into an explosive upward action with minimal ground-contact time.

---

# Philosophy

The Depth Jump is not a general jumping drill and is not appropriate for every athlete.

It is a high-intensity reactive exercise that places substantial demands on the nervous system, tendons, joints, landing mechanics and force-tolerance capacity.

CAS therefore treats the Depth Jump as an advanced exercise rather than a default plyometric option.

The athlete must already demonstrate competent landing mechanics, sufficient relative strength and the ability to perform lower-intensity jumps without technical deterioration.

The governing principle is:

> Absorb fast, stay organized and rebound explosively.

A higher drop does not automatically create a better training effect.

Excessive drop height often increases braking time, impact stress and technical collapse while reducing the quality of the reactive response.

The correct height is the lowest height that produces a powerful rebound with stable alignment and a short ground-contact time.

# Execution Standard

1. Use a stable platform appropriate to the athlete’s level.

2. Stand near the edge with the feet approximately hip-width apart.

3. Maintain an upright trunk and neutral head position.

4. Step forward from the platform rather than jumping upward or outward.

5. Allow the body to fall naturally under gravity.

6. Land on both feet simultaneously.

7. Contact the ground through the forefoot to midfoot while allowing the heels to settle naturally if required.

8. Keep the knees tracking in line with the feet.

9. Absorb the landing rapidly through the ankles, knees and hips.

10. Reverse direction immediately without a deliberate pause.

11. Jump vertically with maximal intent.

12. Use a coordinated arm swing when the programmed variation allows it.

13. Reach full triple extension during take-off.

14. Land the final jump under control.

15. Reset fully before the next repetition.

Each repetition begins from a stable position on the platform.

Repeated rapid stepping from the box without a full reset is not the standard CAS execution.

# Technical Failure Criteria

The repetition or set should be stopped when one or more of the following occur:

The athlete jumps from the platform instead of stepping off.

The athlete lands asymmetrically.

The knees collapse inward.

The feet rotate excessively or shift unpredictably.

The heels collapse violently.

The trunk folds forward excessively.

The athlete loses spinal control.

Ground contact becomes visibly prolonged.

The landing turns into a deep countermovement.

The athlete pauses before the rebound.

The jump travels excessively forward rather than vertically.

Jump height decreases meaningfully.

The athlete cannot control the final landing.

The athlete reports pain.

The platform moves or becomes unstable.

The athlete shows fear, hesitation or protective movement.

The set should end before technical failure becomes pronounced.

# CAS Selection Logic

The Depth Jump may be selected when:

The primary objective includes reactive strength or high-level lower-body power.

The athlete has demonstrated competent landing mechanics.

The athlete has completed appropriate lower-level plyometric progressions.

The athlete has sufficient relative strength and lower-limb control.

No relevant pain or medical restriction is present.

The weekly impact budget permits a high-intensity plyometric stimulus.

The athlete is sufficiently recovered.

The environment and equipment are safe.

The Depth Jump should not be selected when:

The athlete is a beginner in plyometric training.

The objective can be achieved with a lower-risk exercise.

The athlete is fatigued or sore.

The athlete has active tendon symptoms.

Landing mechanics are unstable.

The athlete cannot rebound rapidly.

The drop height cannot be controlled.

The exercise conflicts with high-impact combat, sprint or change-of-direction work.

Selection Priority

Low to Moderate for general populations

Moderate for trained combat athletes

High only when reactive strength is a clear priority and prerequisites are met

The exercise is optional rather than foundational.

CAS should prefer the least complex and least costly exercise that can produce the required adaptation.

# Evidence Classification

Evidence Level

Moderate to High

Evidence Supports

Development of reactive strength

Improvement of fast stretch-shortening-cycle performance

Improvement of jump performance in trained athletes

Development of eccentric-concentric transition capacity

Use within structured plyometric programs

Evidence Limitations

Outcomes depend strongly on training status, dose, drop height and technique.

High-intensity depth-jump methods are not universally superior to lower-intensity plyometrics.

Excessive volume or drop height may increase stress without improving adaptation.

Direct transfer to combat performance is indirect and must not be assumed from jump improvements alone.

CAS Interpretation

The exercise is evidence-supported for advanced reactive-strength development when appropriately dosed and technically supervised.

It should not be treated as mandatory or automatically superior to simpler jump variations.

# Decision Summary

The Depth Jump is an advanced reactive plyometric exercise that develops the ability to absorb high landing forces and rapidly redirect them into explosive vertical propulsion.

Its principal value lies in fast stretch-shortening-cycle training, reactive strength and high-rate force reversal.

Its principal limitations are high neurological cost, high tendon and joint stress, strong dependence on landing competence and poor suitability for fatigued or inexperienced athletes.

CAS should select it only when:

The athlete has completed the required progressions.

Reactive strength is a meaningful objective.

Landing mechanics are stable.

The athlete is pain-free and recovered.

The weekly impact budget permits the exercise.

A low-to-moderate drop height produces a rapid, powerful rebound.

The final decision rule is:

> Use the Depth Jump only when the athlete can turn impact into immediate power without losing alignment, speed or control.
