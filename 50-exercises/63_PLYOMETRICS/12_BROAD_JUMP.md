# BROAD JUMP

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Broad Jump is a bilateral lower-body plyometric exercise used to develop horizontal force production, explosive projection, triple-extension coordination and controlled landing mechanics.

Within CAS, it is primarily selected as a horizontal lower-body power exercise.

The athlete performs a countermovement from a standing position, projects the body forward as far as possible and lands under control on both feet.

The exercise develops the ability to generate a large horizontal impulse while maintaining trunk organization and lower-limb alignment.

---

# Philosophy

The Broad Jump is not simply a test of distance.

Its purpose is to produce powerful horizontal projection while preserving coordinated arm action, complete triple extension and a stable landing.

CAS therefore evaluates the exercise according to the quality of take-off, flight and landing rather than distance alone.

An additional few centimeters do not justify loss of posture, uncontrolled rotation or a collapsing landing.

The governing principle is:

> Project aggressively, land quietly and own the finish.

The best repetition is the longest jump the athlete can complete without sacrificing alignment, balance or landing control.

# Execution Standard

1. Stand with the feet approximately hip- to shoulder-width apart.

2. Position the toes forward or slightly outward according to the athlete's natural stance.

3. Begin tall with the trunk organized and body weight distributed through the whole foot.

4. Swing the arms backward while descending rapidly into a controlled countermovement.

5. Load the hips, knees and ankles without allowing the knees to collapse inward.

6. Reverse the countermovement aggressively.

7. Drive through the ground and extend the hips, knees and ankles forcefully.

8. Swing the arms forward to contribute to projection.

9. Project the body forward while maintaining enough vertical lift for safe flight and landing preparation.

10. Bring the feet forward during flight without excessive spinal flexion.

11. Contact the ground with both feet at approximately the same time.

12. Absorb the landing through the ankles, knees and hips.

13. Keep the knees aligned with the feet.

14. Stabilize without stepping, falling or touching the floor with the hands.

15. Finish balanced and under complete control before resetting.

Each repetition begins from a stable stationary position unless a specific repeated-jump variation is prescribed.

# Technical Failure Criteria

CAS considers the repetition technically failed when one or more of the following occur:

Incomplete hip, knee or ankle extension during take-off

Excessively slow or deep countermovement

Knee valgus during take-off or landing

Marked asymmetry between the legs

Premature forward reaching of the feet that interrupts full extension

Excessive lumbar flexion during flight

Landing primarily on the heels with loss of balance

Landing primarily on the toes without controlled absorption

Hands contacting the floor

Additional step required to regain balance

Backward fall after landing

Uncontrolled trunk rotation

Feet landing at clearly different times

Audibly heavy landing associated with poor force absorption

Visible reduction in distance combined with loss of explosive intent

Pain during take-off, flight or landing

A repetition is not validated solely because the athlete remains upright.

# Contraindications and Restrictions

Avoid or restrict when the athlete presents:

Acute ankle, knee, hip or lower-back pain

Recent lower-limb surgery

Unresolved Achilles tendon symptoms

Acute patellar tendon irritation

Inability to land bilaterally under control

Marked dynamic knee valgus

Significant balance impairment

Insufficient lower-body strength for safe deceleration

Medical restriction against jumping or impact loading

Temporary restrictions may also apply during periods of high lower-body fatigue or immediately after high-volume combat, sprint or strength sessions.

# Combat Transfer

Primary Combat Relevance

Horizontal force production contributes to rapid entry, explosive closing distance, forward pressure and forceful displacement of the opponent.

The exercise may support:

Explosive penetration steps

Takedown entries

Forward striking initiation

Rapid stance displacement

Clinch-driving actions

Acceleration from a static position

Secondary Combat Relevance

The landing phase reinforces the ability to regain stable posture after explosive movement.

Transfer Limitations

The Broad Jump does not reproduce combat stance, rotation, unilateral force application or technical striking mechanics directly.

It develops a general physical quality that must be integrated with sport-specific practice.

# CAS Selection Logic

CAS selects the Broad Jump when the session requires:

Horizontal lower-body power

Explosive triple extension

High-intent ballistic work with minimal equipment

A measurable horizontal jump task

General acceleration-force preparation

Landing control after forward projection

CAS deprioritizes or excludes the Broad Jump when:

The athlete lacks safe landing competency

Acute lower-limb pain or impact restriction is present

The session already contains excessive lower-body plyometric volume

The athlete is highly fatigued

Available space is insufficient

The primary adaptation requires fast reactive contacts rather than a single maximal projection

Selection Priority Increases When

Horizontal force is a current capability deficit

The athlete participates in a sport requiring explosive forward displacement

Equipment availability is limited

The athlete demonstrates competent vertical jumping but insufficient horizontal projection

Selection Priority Decreases When

Landing mechanics are unstable

Lower-body tissue tolerance is low

The athlete has recently completed high-impact work

The training objective is primarily aerobic, hypertrophic or restorative

# Decision Summary

The Broad Jump is a high-value horizontal power exercise for athletes who can jump and land safely.

CAS primarily uses it to develop explosive horizontal projection, triple extension, posterior-chain power and landing control.

It requires little equipment, is easy to measure and has strong relevance for acceleration and forward displacement.

Its value depends on maximal intent, complete extension and a controlled bilateral landing.

The exercise should remain low in volume and high in quality.

CAS selects it when horizontal power is required and substitutes it when impact tolerance, landing competency, available space or fatigue makes safe execution unlikely.
