# SPLIT SQUAT JUMP

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Split Squat Jump is a unilateral lower-body plyometric exercise used to develop explosive force production from a staggered stance, rapid switching between limbs and coordinated force absorption during alternating landings.

Within CAS, it is primarily selected when the athlete needs to improve the ability to produce vertical force from an asymmetrical base while maintaining trunk control, pelvic alignment and repeatable landing mechanics.

The exercise may be performed as repeated alternating jumps, as isolated single repetitions with a reset between efforts or as low-amplitude rhythmic contacts when the objective is elastic reactivity rather than maximal height.

For combat athletes, the Split Squat Jump supports explosive stance transitions, rapid level changes, aggressive entries, recovery from lunging actions and repeated propulsion from positions that resemble fighting and grappling stances.

---

# Philosophy

The Split Squat Jump is not simply a conditioning exercise performed until the legs burn.

Its value comes from producing high-quality force from a staggered stance, switching the limbs cleanly in the air and receiving the body in a stable position that remains ready for the next action.

CAS does not reward excessive jump height obtained through uncontrolled arching, narrow or crossed landings, knee collapse, heavy impacts, loss of trunk position or progressive shortening of the split stance.

The governing principle is:

> Explode from a stable split stance, switch cleanly and land ready to produce force again.

Repetition speed and total volume are increased only after the athlete can preserve alignment, landing quality and equal contribution from both limbs.

# Execution Standard

1. Begin in a stable split stance with the feet positioned on separate rails rather than on a single line.

2. Keep the torso tall or slightly inclined forward while maintaining a neutral spine and stacked ribcage.

3. Distribute the load primarily through the front foot while keeping the rear foot active for balance and assistance.

4. Lower into a controlled split-squat position without allowing the front knee to collapse inward.

5. Drive forcefully through the floor and extend the hip, knee and ankle to leave the ground.

6. Exchange the legs cleanly in the air without crossing the feet or rotating the pelvis.

7. Land softly in the opposite split stance with both feet contacting in a stable position.

8. Absorb force through the ankle, knee and hip while maintaining front-knee alignment over the foot.

9. Stabilize briefly or transition immediately into the next repetition according to the prescribed variation.

10. End the set before landing noise, jump height, stance width or alignment deteriorates.

# Key Coaching Cues

Stay on two rails.

Drive straight up.

Switch the legs, not the torso.

Land softly in a strong stance.

Front knee tracks over the foot.

Quality before speed.

# Technical Failure Criteria

CAS considers the repetition failed when one or more of the following occur:

The feet cross in the air or land on a single narrow line.

The front knee collapses medially.

The athlete loses balance or takes corrective steps.

The pelvis rotates excessively during the switch.

The torso arches, folds or twists to create artificial height.

The rear knee strikes or nearly strikes the ground unintentionally.

The landing is excessively loud or rigid.

The athlete lands with insufficient split-stance depth to control the force.

The feet progressively move closer together.

Jump height declines substantially across the set.

The switching pattern becomes asynchronous or hesitant.

Pain appears in the knee, ankle, Achilles tendon, hip or lower back.

# Progression Model

Stage 1

Master the static split squat and demonstrate pain-free unilateral control.

Stage 2

Perform low-amplitude assisted split squat jumps with a full reset.

Stage 3

Perform unassisted single repetitions with a stable opposite-leg landing.

Stage 4

Perform short alternating sets while preserving stance width and quiet landings.

Stage 5

Increase jump height or decrease ground-contact time according to the target adaptation.

Stage 6

Integrate reactive transitions, directional exits or combat-specific stance recovery.

Stage 7

Add minimal external load only when it improves the intended stimulus without reducing quality.

Progression Rule

Only one major variable should be increased at a time: height, contact speed, volume, complexity or load.

# Programming Applications

Maximum Power Development

3 to 5 sets of 3 to 5 repetitions per side

Use maximal intent and full recovery.

Technical Plyometric Development

3 to 4 sets of 4 to 6 repetitions per side

Use moderate amplitude and controlled landings.

Reactive Plyometric Development

3 to 5 sets of 6 to 10 total contacts

Use low to moderate amplitude and short ground contact while preserving alignment.

Power Endurance

3 to 4 sets of 10 to 20 total contacts

Use only with technically competent athletes and stop before landing quality declines.

Contrast Training

2 to 4 sets performed after heavy split squats, lunges or unilateral strength work

Use low repetition counts and long recovery.

Warm-Up Potentiation

2 to 3 sets of 3 repetitions per side

Use submaximal volume and high movement quality.

Combat Conditioning Circuit

Short controlled bouts may be used, but CAS avoids long fatigue-driven sets when the objective is power or landing quality.

# CAS Selection Logic

CAS may select the Split Squat Jump when:

The primary objective is lower-body power.

The athlete needs unilateral or staggered-stance force production.

The athlete demonstrates acceptable bilateral and unilateral landing control.

The planned session can accommodate moderate impact.

The athlete is not carrying excessive lower-body fatigue.

The exercise supports the selected combat capability without conflicting with recovery priorities.

CAS should deprioritize or reject the exercise when:

The athlete reports knee, ankle, Achilles or hip pain.

Readiness is low and landing control is likely to be impaired.

The athlete cannot maintain stance width or knee alignment.

The same session already contains high-intensity unilateral plyometric volume.

The athlete requires a lower-skill or lower-impact option.

The session objective is maximal strength, hypertrophy or recovery and the exercise adds unnecessary impact.

The athlete has no demonstrated prerequisite landing competence.

# Decision Summary

The Split Squat Jump is a high-value alternating unilateral plyometric exercise for athletes who already possess basic landing competence and require explosive force production from a staggered stance.

CAS selects it primarily for lower-body power, interlimb coordination, stance-switch ability and combat-relevant unilateral propulsion.

It should be programmed with low to moderate repetition counts, maximal technical intent and sufficient recovery when the objective is power.

The exercise must be regressed, substituted or removed when landing quality, stance width, knee alignment or pain-free execution cannot be maintained.

Its effectiveness depends not on accumulating fatigue, but on preserving fast propulsion, clean limb exchange and stable landings throughout every accepted repetition.
