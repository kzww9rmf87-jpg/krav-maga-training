# LATERAL BOUND

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Lateral Bound is a unilateral frontal-plane plyometric exercise used to develop lateral power, single-leg force acceptance and rapid control of the centre of mass.

Within CAS, it is primarily selected to improve the athlete’s ability to project the body sideways, land on one leg and stabilize immediately before the next action.

The athlete pushes explosively from one leg, travels laterally through the air and lands on the opposite leg with the hip, knee and ankle aligned.

The exercise is particularly useful for combat athletes because it trains force production and deceleration outside the sagittal plane while reinforcing control of the pelvis and lower limb during rapid changes of direction.

---

# Philosophy

The Lateral Bound is not a contest to travel as far as possible.

Its value lies in producing decisive lateral force while preserving a stable, quiet and repeatable landing.

CAS does not reward distance gained through uncontrolled trunk lean, excessive rotation, knee collapse or a recovery step.

The governing principle is:

> Push hard sideways, land on one leg and own the position.

Distance is increased only when the athlete can maintain frontal-plane alignment and immediate balance.

# Execution Standard

1. Stand on one leg with the support foot fully grounded.

2. Maintain a slight bend at the hip, knee and ankle.

3. Keep the pelvis level, trunk organized and eyes forward.

4. Allow the free leg and arms to move naturally to assist balance and propulsion.

5. Load the stance hip without allowing the knee to collapse inward.

6. Push the ground away forcefully and project the body laterally.

7. Travel toward the opposite side with sufficient height to reposition the limbs.

8. Prepare the opposite leg to receive the body.

9. Land on the midfoot of the opposite leg.

10. Allow the hip, knee and ankle to flex together to absorb force.

11. Keep the knee aligned over the foot.

12. Maintain a level pelvis and controlled trunk.

13. Hold the landing position for approximately one to two seconds unless a continuous variation is prescribed.

14. Reset deliberately before the next repetition.

Each repetition must finish with clear single-leg control.

# Technical Failure Criteria

A repetition is invalid when any of the following occurs:

The athlete takes off from both feet.

The push-off is hesitant or lacks clear lateral intent.

The support knee collapses inward during take-off.

The trunk rotates excessively to create momentum.

The athlete lands with the foot excessively turned in or out.

The landing knee collapses inward.

The pelvis drops markedly on the non-support side.

The foot rolls uncontrollably inward or outward.

The athlete lands rigidly without absorbing force.

The landing is excessively loud.

The athlete touches the free foot down immediately to regain balance.

A recovery step or hop is required.

The trunk falls significantly outside the base of support.

The athlete cannot hold the landing position.

Pain occurs during take-off or landing.

Distance or landing quality declines substantially.

# Substitution Logic

Substitute the Lateral Bound when the target is lateral power and unilateral landing control but the athlete cannot perform a higher-demand reactive drill safely.

Replace the Lateral Bound when:

The athlete cannot maintain single-leg alignment.

The athlete has ankle instability.

Lateral loading produces pain.

The available surface lacks traction.

Fatigue prevents stable landings.

Preferred substitutions include short lateral hops, lateral step-and-stick drills, skater steps or controlled change-of-direction drills.

---

The athlete lacks basic single-leg stability.

The athlete cannot control knee alignment.

The surface is unsafe.

The athlete is meaningfully fatigued.

# Decision Summary

The Lateral Bound is a high-value unilateral plyometric exercise for developing lateral power, frontal-plane control and single-leg landing capacity.

CAS selects it when the athlete needs to produce force sideways, absorb that force on the opposite leg and regain stable control rapidly.

It should be performed with low repetitions, maximal intent and strict landing standards.

Distance, speed and complexity must never be increased at the expense of knee alignment, pelvic control or balance.

The exercise is especially relevant to combat athletes who must create angles, move laterally and recover position after rapid displacement.
