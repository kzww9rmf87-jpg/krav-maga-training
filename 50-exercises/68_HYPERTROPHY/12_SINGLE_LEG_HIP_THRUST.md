# SINGLE-LEG HIP THRUST

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Single-Leg Hip Thrust is a unilateral hip-extension exercise performed from a supine position with the shoulders elevated or grounded.

Within CAS, it is the primary hip-extension exercise available to an athlete training without equipment.

---

# Philosophy

Hip extension produces the force behind a takedown, a sprint and a kick.

The barbell Hip Thrust trains it under heavy load, but requires a barbell, a bench and plates. The Single-Leg Hip Thrust trains the same pattern with body mass alone by loading one hip at a time.

CAS selects it when posterior-chain muscular development is requested and no external loading is available.

# Coaching Cues

Keep the ribs down; extend the hip, not the spine.

Drive through the heel of the working foot.

Keep the pelvis level throughout.

Pause briefly at full extension.

Lower under control.

---

# Common Errors

Extending the lumbar spine instead of the hip.

Pelvis dropping on the unsupported side.

Pushing through the toes.

Rushing the descent.

Incomplete hip extension at the top.

---

# Performance Indicators

Repetitions completed with a level pelvis.

Full hip extension reached on every repetition.

Symmetry between sides.

No lumbar compensation under fatigue.

---

# Scientific Evidence

Evidence Level

★★★

Hip-extension exercise is well supported for gluteal muscular development, and the horizontal loading vector is documented as producing high gluteal activation.

Evidence specific to the unilateral bodyweight variation is more limited than for the loaded bilateral Hip Thrust. CAS records this as limited evidence rather than extrapolating from the loaded version.

Transfer to grappling performance is stated as expert consensus.

---

# Explainability

Example

The Single-Leg Hip Thrust is selected because the athlete requested posterior-chain development and has no barbell available.

Loading one hip at a time produces a sufficient stimulus from body mass alone, at a fatigue cost low enough to sit beside combat practice.

# Success Criteria

The exercise is successful when:

---

# Final Principle

The Single-Leg Hip Thrust asks one hip to do the work of two.

That is enough to make body mass a training load,

and it costs the athlete almost nothing in systemic fatigue.
