# HYPERTROPHY EXERCISE COMPARISON

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document compares the Hypertrophy exercises defined in CAS and clarifies the conditions under which each should be selected.

Its purpose is not to rank exercises from best to worst.

Its purpose is to help the CAS Engine distinguish between exercises that may appear interchangeable but differ in movement pattern, equipment requirement, unilateral demand, interference with combat practice and recovery cost.

The comparison covers:

- Push-Up
- Split Squat
- Single-Leg Hip Thrust
- Goblet Squat
- Dumbbell Bench Press
- One-Arm Dumbbell Row
- Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

---

# Core Principle

> CAS selects the hypertrophy exercise that matches the required movement pattern with the equipment the athlete actually has, at the lowest interference with combat practice.

Hypertrophy is not treated as one undifferentiated quality.

The exercise must be chosen according to the dominant requirement:

- movement pattern,
- available equipment,
- unilateral demand,
- interference with technical work,
- recovery cost.

# Push-Up Compared With Dumbbell Bench Press

Both train the horizontal press. They are not interchangeable.

The Push-Up requires nothing and loads a fixed fraction of body mass. Once an athlete can perform well beyond twelve controlled repetitions, the stimulus falls away and no amount of additional volume restores it.

The Dumbbell Bench Press requires dumbbells and a bench, and its load can be raised indefinitely.

**Select the Push-Up** when no external loading is available, or when the session must sit beside technical work at low cost.

**Select the Dumbbell Bench Press** when dumbbells are available and a progressive stimulus is required.

Neither is a maximum-strength exercise. Both are documented as hypertrophy.

---

# Split Squat Compared With Goblet Squat

Both train the squat pattern.

The Split Squat raises the relative load by working one leg at a time, which is how body mass alone remains a stimulus. Its limit is balance and rear-leg fatigue, not the working leg.

The Goblet Squat restores true external loading and is bilateral, so the working muscles rather than balance set the limit. It requires one implement.

**Select the Split Squat** when no external loading is available, or when unilateral work is specifically wanted.

**Select the Goblet Squat** when a dumbbell or kettlebell is available and progressive loading matters more than the unilateral demand.

They are complementary rather than redundant, and a session may legitimately hold both.

---

# Single-Leg Hip Thrust Compared With Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

Both train hip extension. They load it very differently.

The Single-Leg Hip Thrust loads the hip at short muscle length, in a horizontal vector, with almost no spinal demand. It is the lowest-cost exercise in this family and the safest to place near combat practice.

The Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift loads the posterior chain through hamstring length, eccentrically, with a real spinal demand. It carries the longest recovery window in the family.

**Select the Single-Leg Hip Thrust** when no equipment is available, when spinal loading must be avoided, or when the session sits beside technical work.

**Select the Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift** when dumbbells are available and hamstring capacity through length is the objective — accepting a 48–72 hour recovery window.

Placing both in the same session is possible but rarely wise: they overlap heavily and their combined soreness is disproportionate.

---

# One-Arm Dumbbell Row: No Bodyweight Equivalent

This is the one pattern the family cannot serve without equipment, and the reason is physical rather than editorial.

A horizontal pull requires something to pull against. An inverted row needs a bar, a rack, rings or a sturdy support; a band row needs a band and an anchor. An athlete with genuinely no equipment cannot train the pattern.

CAS records this as an unsupported combination rather than substituting a vertical pull and calling it equivalent. A Pull-Up is not a horizontal pull, and a session that quietly swaps one for the other would misrepresent what it trains.

An athlete with a single dumbbell can train the pattern immediately, which is why the One-Arm Dumbbell Row is in this family and an inverted row is not.

# Interference Ranking

From lowest to highest interference with combat practice on the same or following day:

1. Single-Leg Hip Thrust
2. One-Arm Dumbbell Row
3. Push-Up
4. Dumbbell Bench Press
5. Goblet Squat
6. Split Squat
7. Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

The first three may precede technical work at reduced volume. The last four should not.

---

# Redundancy

Two exercises are redundant when they train the same pattern, at the same equipment level, with the same unilateral demand.

Within this family, no two exercises are redundant with each other.

Push-Up and Dumbbell Bench Press share a pattern but not an equipment level. Split Squat and Goblet Squat share a pattern but differ in unilateral demand and equipment. Single-Leg Hip Thrust and Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift share a pattern but load it at opposite muscle lengths.

The engine's redundancy rule compares primary adaptation, shared movement pattern and shared loaded body region. Split Squat and Goblet Squat will register as redundant under that rule, and that is the correct outcome for a single session: an athlete needs one squat, not two.

---

# Relationship To The Strength Family

Several exercises here resemble entries in the strength family. They are not duplicates, and the distinction is the adaptation rather than the movement.

| Hypertrophy family | Strength family | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Goblet Squat | Back Squat, Front Squat | Load ceiling and equipment |
| Dumbbell Bench Press | Bench Press | Load ceiling and equipment |
| Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift | Romanian Deadlift | Load ceiling and equipment |
| One-Arm Dumbbell Row | Chest-Supported Row, Barbell Row | Adaptation and equipment |
| Split Squat | Bulgarian Split Squat | Rear-foot elevation and skill |

A barbell exercise prescribed at 80–90% of a one-repetition maximum and a dumbbell exercise prescribed at RPE 8 are not the same prescription, do not require the same athlete data, and do not produce the same adaptation claim.

CAS must never silently substitute one for the other. A strength request answered with a hypertrophy exercise is a changed objective, and changing the objective is the athlete's decision.

---

# Final Principle

Seven exercises, four patterns, two equipment profiles.

The comparison exists so that the engine chooses between them on pattern, equipment and interference —

never on which one happens to score highest.
