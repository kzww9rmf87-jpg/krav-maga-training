# PUSH-UP

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Push-Up is a bilateral bodyweight horizontal pressing exercise designed to develop upper-body muscular development, pressing endurance and trunk stability.

Within CAS, it is the primary horizontal pressing exercise available to an athlete training without equipment.

---

# Philosophy

A combat athlete frequently trains without access to a barbell.

The Push-Up allows the horizontal pressing pattern to be trained anywhere, with the trunk braced against gravity rather than supported by a bench.

CAS selects the Push-Up when the requested adaptation is muscular development and no external loading is available.

The Push-Up is NOT a maximum-strength exercise for a trained athlete. Its resistance is fixed at a fraction of body mass and cannot be raised to the intensity a maximum-strength dose requires. CAS classifies it honestly rather than presenting an endurance stimulus as a strength stimulus.

# Coaching Cues

Brace the trunk before the first repetition.

Keep the ribs down and the pelvis level.

Lower the chest, not the head.

Keep the elbows at roughly forty-five degrees from the trunk.

Press the ground away.

---

# Common Errors

Hips sagging under fatigue.

Head leading the descent.

Elbows flaring to ninety degrees.

Partial range of motion.

Loss of scapular control at the bottom.

---

# Performance Indicators

Repetitions completed at a controlled tempo.

Trunk position maintained to the final repetition.

Full range of motion sustained under fatigue.

Symmetry between sides during single-arm progressions.

---

# Scientific Evidence

Evidence Level

★★★★

The Push-Up is a well-studied bodyweight pressing exercise with consistent evidence for upper-body muscular development and pressing endurance in beginner and intermediate populations.

Evidence for maximum-strength development in trained athletes is limited, because the resistance cannot be raised sufficiently. CAS therefore does not classify the Push-Up as a maximum-strength exercise.

Transfer to striking performance is indirect and depends on integration with sport-specific training. This is stated as expert consensus rather than as a demonstrated effect.

---

# Explainability

Example

The Push-Up is selected because the athlete requested muscular development and has no external loading available.

It trains the horizontal pressing pattern at an intensity the athlete can actually reach, and does so without the systemic fatigue that would interfere with combat practice.

# Success Criteria

The exercise is successful when:

Repetition capacity increases at a controlled tempo.

Trunk position is maintained under fatigue.

Upper-body muscular development progresses.

Recovery remains appropriate.

Combat practice on adjacent days is unaffected.

---

# Final Principle

The Push-Up earns its place not because it is difficult,

but because it is available.

It trains the horizontal press honestly,

at the intensity a bodyweight movement can actually deliver,

and CAS classifies it for what it is rather than for what would be convenient.
