# GOBLET SQUAT

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Goblet Squat is a bilateral squat performed with a single dumbbell or kettlebell held at the chest.

Within CAS, it is the primary loaded squat available to an athlete training without a barbell or rack.

---

# Philosophy

The squat pattern needs external load to remain a hypertrophy stimulus once body mass is no longer sufficient.

The Goblet Squat supplies that load with one implement and no rack. The front-loaded position also makes the movement self-limiting: an athlete who loses trunk position cannot complete the repetition, so the exercise regulates its own technique.

CAS selects it when lower-body muscular development is requested and a dumbbell or kettlebell is available.

# Coaching Cues

Hold the implement close to the chest.

Brace before descending.

Push the knees out as you descend.

Keep the whole foot in contact with the floor.

Stand up through the middle of the foot.

---

# Common Errors

Load drifting away from the chest.

Trunk collapsing forward.

Heels lifting.

Knees collapsing inward.

Cutting the depth short as the set progresses.

---

# Performance Indicators

Repetitions completed at a controlled tempo.

Depth maintained under fatigue.

# Scientific Evidence

Evidence Level

★★★★

Loaded squatting is among the most extensively researched resistance exercises for lower-body muscular development.

Evidence specific to the goblet position, as distinct from other squat variations, is more limited; CAS treats the front-loaded position as a technique and accessibility decision rather than a claim of different adaptation.

Transfer to grappling performance is stated as expert consensus.

---

# Explainability

Example

The Goblet Squat is selected because the athlete requested lower-body muscular development and has a dumbbell or kettlebell but no rack.

It restores external loading to the squat pattern with a single implement, and the front-loaded position keeps technique self-limiting as fatigue accumulates.

# Final Principle

The Goblet Squat gives the squat back its load

without a rack, a barbell or a spotter,

and it refuses the repetition when the trunk gives way —

which makes it the safest place for a squat pattern to be rebuilt.
