# ONE-ARM DUMBBELL ROW

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The One-Arm Dumbbell Row is a unilateral horizontal pulling exercise performed with the trunk supported.

Within CAS, it is the primary horizontal pull available to an athlete training with dumbbells.

---

# Philosophy

Pulling is under-trained in combat athletes relative to pressing, and horizontal pulling in particular is difficult to load without equipment.

The One-Arm Dumbbell Row supplies it with a single implement. Supporting the trunk removes the lower back as the limiting factor, so the working side reaches a hypertrophy stimulus without a systemic cost.

CAS selects it when upper-body pulling development is requested and a dumbbell is available.

# Coaching Cues

Set the trunk square and keep it there.

Start the movement with the shoulder blade, not the hand.

Pull the implement toward the hip, not the shoulder.

Keep the neck long.

Lower under control to full extension.

---

# Common Errors

Trunk rotating to help the pull.

Shrugging the shoulder toward the ear.

Pulling with the arm before the scapula moves.

Using momentum from the legs.

Cutting the range of motion short at the bottom.

---

# Performance Indicators

Repetitions completed with a square trunk.

# Scientific Evidence

Evidence Level

★★★★

Horizontal pulling is well supported for upper-back muscular development and for balancing the pressing volume typical of combat-athlete preparation.

Evidence specific to the one-arm supported variation, as distinct from other rowing variations, is more limited and is recorded as such.

Transfer to grappling performance is stated as expert consensus.

# Final Principle

Combat athletes press more than they pull.

The One-Arm Dumbbell Row corrects that with one implement,

one side at a time,

and almost no cost to the next day's training.
