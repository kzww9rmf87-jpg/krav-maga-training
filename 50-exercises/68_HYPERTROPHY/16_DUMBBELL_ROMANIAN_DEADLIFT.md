# DUMBBELL ROMANIAN DEADLIFT

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift is a bilateral hip-hinge exercise performed with dumbbells held in front of the thighs.

Within CAS, it is the primary loaded hinge available to an athlete without a barbell.

---

# Philosophy

The hinge trains the posterior chain through length, which is where hamstring injuries occur and where takedown force is produced.

The barbell Romanian Deadlift requires a barbell and plates. The dumbbell version trains the same pattern with implements an athlete can keep at home, and the lighter absolute load makes it more forgiving of the technique errors that make hinging risky.

CAS selects it when posterior-chain muscular development is requested and dumbbells are available.

# Coaching Cues

Set the upper back before the first repetition.

Push the hips back rather than bending the knees.

Keep the implements close to the legs.

Descend only as far as hamstring length allows without the spine rounding.

Finish by driving the hips forward, not by leaning back.

---

# Common Errors

Rounding the lower back at the bottom.

Turning the movement into a squat.

Letting the implements drift away from the legs.

Descending past the point where the spine can stay neutral.

Hyperextending the lumbar spine at the top.

---

# Performance Indicators

Repetitions completed with a neutral spine.

Range of motion limited by hamstring length rather than by spinal position.

Load progressed over successive sessions.

Hamstring tolerance to length improves.

---

# Scientific Evidence

Evidence Level

★★★★

Hip-hinge exercise with eccentric loading is well supported for posterior-chain muscular development and is documented in the hamstring-injury-prevention literature.

Evidence specific to the dumbbell variation, as distinct from the barbell variation, is more limited; CAS treats the implement as an accessibility decision rather than a claim of different adaptation.

Transfer to grappling performance is stated as expert consensus.

---

# Explainability

Example

The Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift is selected because the athlete requested posterior-chain development and has dumbbells but no barbell.

It loads the hinge through hamstring length, which is where the pattern both produces force and gets injured, at an absolute load that forgives the technique errors a heavier implement would punish.

# Success Criteria

The exercise is successful when:

Load or repetitions progress over successive sessions.

Spinal position stays neutral throughout.

Hamstring tolerance to length improves.

Recovery remains appropriate.

Delayed soreness is accounted for in the following days' combat practice.

---

# Final Principle

The hinge is where the posterior chain is built

and where the hamstring is most often lost.

The Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift trains it through length,

under a load light enough to forgive,

and heavy enough to matter.
