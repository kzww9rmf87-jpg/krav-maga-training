# HANG POWER CLEAN

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Hang Power Clean is a loaded whole-body power exercise used to develop explosive triple extension combined with the rapid receiving skill required to catch a rapidly accelerated bar in a stable front-rack position.

Within CAS, it is selected primarily to improve lower-body power, hip extension velocity, rate of force development, whole-body force transfer and the athlete's ability to decelerate and stabilize an external load immediately after maximal acceleration.

It is not treated as a simple pulling exercise or as a substitute for the Hang High Pull when the objective does not require a catch.

Its training value depends on an aggressive extension, a close bar path, a rapid and complete turnover of the bar, and a stable, controlled catch in the front rack.

---

# Philosophy

The Hang Power Clean teaches the athlete to accelerate an external load through coordinated extension of the ankles, knees and hips, then reorganize the body instantly to receive that same load in a position from which it can be controlled and used.

The governing principle is:

> Extend violently through the floor, then turn over and receive the bar as if catching a falling object—not as if lifting it a second time.

The exercise is valuable because it combines the propulsive demand of a triple-extension pull with the deceleration, timing and positional-stability demand of a true receiving skill, making it the most technically complete derivative in the Power category.

It loses its primary value when the athlete pulls the bar without full extension, receives the bar too high or too low, catches with unstable posture, muscles the bar into the rack with the arms, or continues repetitions after turnover quality deteriorates.

# Biomechanical Profile

The Hang Power Clean begins with the bar held above or near the knees in a balanced hang position.

The athlete loads the posterior chain through a controlled hip hinge while maintaining a stable trunk, neutral head position and full-foot pressure.

The movement is initiated by forcefully extending the hips and knees against the ground.

The ankles complete the extension as the athlete reaches a tall, powerful finish, with the trapezius driving the shoulders upward at the top of the pull.

The bar should travel vertically and remain close to the body throughout the extension.

Once full extension has been achieved, the athlete pulls the body down and under the bar, aggressively rotating the elbows around and forward to receive the bar across the front of the shoulders.

The catch is absorbed with a rapid, coordinated bend of the hips, knees and ankles, arriving in a quarter-squat or, for lighter loads and more advanced athletes, a near-standing front-rack position.

The athlete then stands to full extension to complete the repetition.

Effective execution requires:

- a stable and balanced hang position,
- controlled pre-tension before acceleration,
- aggressive hip and knee extension,
- coordinated triple extension with a tall finish,
- a rapid turnover of the elbows from under the bar to in front of it,
- an active pull of the body under the bar rather than a passive wait for the bar to arrive,
- a stable front-rack catch with the elbows high and the trunk upright,
- coordinated absorption through the hips, knees and ankles,
- and a controlled stand to complete the lift.

The Hang Power Clean differs from the Hang High Pull because the bar is received on the shoulders in a front-rack position rather than continued upward with the elbows.

It differs from the Push Press because the receiving phase occurs at the bottom of the pull rather than at the top of a press.

It differs from the Clean Pull because the athlete completes a full turnover and catch rather than continuing to accelerate the bar upward with straight arms.

It is more technically demanding than either the Hang High Pull or the Jump Shrug because it requires both maximal propulsive output and a precise, rapidly executed receiving skill.

# Velocity Profile

Intended Velocity

Maximal concentric intent during the pull, followed by maximal reactive speed during the turnover

Actual Velocity

High during the pull, briefly interrupted by the deceleration demand of the catch

Acceptable Velocity Loss

Low

The repetition should appear fast, coordinated and cleanly sequenced from extension into turnover into catch.

A substantial reduction in bar height, extension speed, turnover speed or catch stability indicates that the power and skill stimulus is deteriorating.

CAS should terminate or reduce the set when velocity loss, turnover delay or catch instability becomes visible or measurable beyond the programmed threshold.

# Loading Profile

Primary Loading Zone

Light to moderate external load

Typical Loading Orientation

Strength-speed to speed-strength, depending on the selected load and the athlete's receiving proficiency

Common Reference Range

Approximately 60 to 80 percent of an athlete's conventional power clean one-repetition maximum when such a reference is technically valid

Alternative Reference

A load that permits maximal acceleration, a rapid complete turnover and a stable, repeatable catch across all repetitions

The correct load is not defined by percentage alone.

CAS should prioritize execution velocity, turnover quality and catch stability over absolute load.

Loads that cause an incomplete extension, a slow or partial turnover, an unstable catch or a collapse in the front rack are too heavy for the intended power and skill objective.

Very light loads that provide no meaningful external resistance may be useful for technical learning but may not provide sufficient strength-speed stimulus.

# Execution Standard

## Starting Position

Stand with the feet approximately hip-width apart.

Hold the bar with a clean-width overhand or hook grip.

Keep the arms long and the shoulders positioned slightly in front of or directly above the bar.

Maintain a neutral head position and a braced trunk.

Distribute pressure through the whole foot.

## Hang Position

Hinge at the hips and allow the knees to bend slightly.

Lower the bar to a position above or near the knees according to the programmed variation.

Keep the bar close to the thighs.

Maintain a stable spine and balanced foot pressure.

Create tension through the posterior chain without pausing excessively.

## Extension

Drive forcefully into the floor.

Extend the knees and hips rapidly.

Finish tall through coordinated ankle, knee and hip extension.

Shrug the shoulders explosively as the final phase of extension.

Do not begin the turnover before the lower body has completed the main acceleration phase.

## Turnover

Immediately after full extension, pull the body down and under the bar.

Rotate the elbows aggressively around and forward.

Move the feet rapidly, if needed, into a stable receiving stance.

Keep the turnover fast and complete rather than partial or delayed.

## Catch

Receive the bar across the front of the shoulders with the elbows high and the trunk upright.

Absorb the impact with a coordinated bend of the hips, knees and ankles into a quarter-squat or, when appropriate, a near-standing front-rack position.

Maintain a braced trunk and stable wrist position throughout the catch.

Do not allow the elbows to drop or the torso to collapse forward.

## Finish

Stand to full hip and knee extension to complete the repetition.

Maintain control of the bar in the front rack before the next action.

## Return

Lower the bar under control back to the hang position.

Re-establish the hang position and posture before the next repetition.

Do not perform a rapid uncontrolled drop into the thighs or knees.

# Technical Failure Criteria

The repetition is considered technically failed when one or more of the following occur:

- the athlete begins the turnover before meaningful hip and knee extension,
- hip or knee extension is incomplete at the top of the pull,
- the bar drifts substantially away from the body during the pull,
- the turnover is slow, partial or incomplete,
- the bar is received before or after the correct catch position, forcing an unstable adjustment,
- the elbows drop during or after the catch,
- the torso collapses forward in the front rack,
- the athlete loses neutral spinal control during the catch,
- the catch produces a hard, uncontrolled collision with the shoulders,
- the athlete cannot stand cleanly out of the catch position,
- the athlete steps or stumbles excessively to control the catch,
- bar height, extension velocity or turnover speed falls substantially,
- grip failure alters the bar path,
- or pain occurs.

A technically successful repetition must preserve full extension, a rapid complete turnover and a stable, controlled catch.

Completion alone is not sufficient.

# Fatigue Profile

Systemic Fatigue

Moderate when volume and load are controlled

Neurological Fatigue

High per high-quality repetition

Metabolic Fatigue

Low to moderate when programmed for power

Technical Fatigue Sensitivity

Very high

The Hang Power Clean is highly sensitive to fatigue because turnover speed and catch stability degrade before complete muscular exhaustion.

Common fatigue-related changes include a slower turnover, a lower or later catch, forward torso lean in the rack, reduced extension velocity, wider or less stable foot placement and increased reliance on the arms to muscle the bar into position.

# Progression Model

## Stage 1 — Hinge Competency

The athlete demonstrates a stable hip hinge with neutral spinal control and whole-foot pressure.

## Stage 2 — Front-Rack Tolerance

The athlete demonstrates a comfortable, stable front-rack position under light load, including adequate wrist and shoulder mobility.

## Stage 3 — Extension Drill

The athlete learns to accelerate through the floor using jump shrugs or clean pulls with long arms.

## Stage 4 — Turnover Drill

The athlete practices the tall-catch or muscle-clean turnover with light or no load to isolate the receiving skill.

## Stage 5 — Light Hang Power Clean

The athlete performs low-load repetitions combining full extension with a controlled, complete turnover and catch.

## Stage 6 — Standard Hang Power Clean

The athlete uses moderate loads for repeated high-quality power-and-catch sets.

## Stage 7 — Strength-Speed Hang Power Clean

The athlete increases external load while preserving complete extension, a fast turnover and a stable catch.

## Stage 8 — Advanced Monitoring or Variation

The athlete uses velocity monitoring, cluster sets or progresses toward the Power Clean from the floor or related derivatives.

Progression should stop or regress when extension, turnover or catch quality deteriorates.

# Combat Transfer

The Hang Power Clean has moderate to high general transfer to combat sports because it develops rapid force production through the lower body and hips together with the ability to reorganize the body and stabilize under a sudden change in load or position.

Potential transfer qualities include:

- explosive hip extension,
- rapid lower-body force production,
- force transfer through a braced trunk,
- rapid whole-body reorganization under acceleration,
- deceleration and stabilization after a maximal effort,
- and positional control immediately following an explosive action.

The exercise does not reproduce the exact vectors, timing or technical patterns of takedowns, throws or striking actions.

Its transfer is therefore general rather than skill-specific, though the turnover-and-catch demand shares a structural similarity with the rapid transitions required in wrestling, judo and MMA.

CAS should use it to build physical and coordinative capacity that supports combat performance, not to replace technical practice.

# Substitution Logic

Substitute the Hang Power Clean when:

- the athlete cannot achieve a stable front-rack position,
- turnover mechanics remain unstable despite regression,
- wrist, elbow or shoulder discomfort occurs during the catch,
- grip fatigue prevents quality execution,
- equipment or space is unavailable,
- posterior-chain fatigue is excessive,
- or the required technical supervision is not available.

Preferred substitutions by objective:

For explosive pulling power without a catch

Hang High Pull

Clean Pull from Hang

For lower-body loaded power

Trap-Bar Jump

Loaded Jump Squat

For front-rack tolerance development

Front Squat

For unloaded explosive power

Broad Jump

Box Jump

The substitute should preserve as much of the intended adaptation as possible rather than merely resemble the exercise visually.

CAS should not substitute the Hang High Pull for the Hang Power Clean when receiving skill itself is the target adaptation.

---

# CAS Selection Logic

CAS may select the Hang Power Clean when:

- the athlete has adequate technical proficiency in both extension and turnover mechanics,
- explosive extension and receiving skill are both current objectives,
- front-rack tolerance is sufficient,
- coaching quality is available to supervise turnover and catch mechanics,
- sufficient equipment and space are available,
- current readiness supports high-velocity, high-precision execution,
- and the benefit of the catch justifies the added complexity relative to simpler derivatives.

CAS should deprioritize or reject the exercise when:

- the athlete is a barbell novice without supervision,
- hinge, extension or front-rack mechanics are unstable,
- acute low-back, hamstring, shoulder, elbow or wrist symptoms are present,
- fatigue prevents maximal intent or a controlled catch,
- the session already contains excessive posterior-chain or shoulder loading,
- the training objective is primarily conditioning,
- or a lower-skill option provides similar propulsive adaptation with less risk.

The engine must never select the Hang Power Clean merely because it appears more athletic or advanced.

Selection priority increases only when the receiving-skill component itself is a required adaptation, not merely because greater technical complexity is available.

# Evidence Classification

Evidence Level

Moderate

The Hang Power Clean belongs to the family of weightlifting derivatives commonly used to develop power, rate of force development and receiving skill.

Its exact superiority over other loaded power exercises is context-dependent and highly sensitive to the athlete's technical readiness.

The strongest rationale supports its use when:

- the athlete can execute both the propulsive and receiving phases competently,
- the load allows high acceleration and a stable catch,
- volume remains low,
- and the exercise fits the wider training plan.

CAS should avoid treating the movement as universally necessary or as inherently superior to non-catching derivatives.

It is one option among several for developing loaded whole-body power, distinguished specifically by its receiving-skill demand.

---

# Decision Summary

The Hang Power Clean is a high-skill loaded power exercise that combines explosive triple extension with a rapid, precisely timed turnover and catch in the front rack.

CAS should select it when the athlete has competent hinge mechanics, adequate front-rack mobility, demonstrated turnover competency and a clear need for a receiving-skill adaptation that simpler pulling derivatives do not provide.

It should be performed early in the session with low repetitions, maximal intent, controlled loading and sufficient recovery.

The exercise should be rejected or regressed when incomplete extension, an unstable turnover, a collapsed catch, fatigue or pain prevents high-quality execution.

Its governing rule is:

> Extend fully, turn over fast, catch tall and stop before turnover or catch quality deteriorates.
