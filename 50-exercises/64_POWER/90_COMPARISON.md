# POWER EXERCISE COMPARISON

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document compares the loaded Power exercises currently defined in CAS and clarifies the conditions under which each exercise should be selected.

Its purpose is not to rank exercises from best to worst.

Its purpose is to help the CAS Engine distinguish between exercises that may appear similar but create different force-expression profiles, technical demands, receiving requirements, upper-body contributions and recovery costs.

The comparison covers:

- Push Press
- Hang High Pull
- Hang Power Clean
- Jump Shrug

---

# Core Principle

> CAS selects the power exercise that allows the athlete to express maximal useful velocity while respecting technical competence, mobility, fatigue, equipment and the target force pathway.

Loaded power training is not treated as heavy strength work performed quickly.

The selected exercise must preserve:

- maximal acceleration intent,
- technically stable positions,
- limited velocity loss,
- appropriate loading,
- sufficient recovery,
- and a clear relationship to the desired adaptation.

When two exercises can train a similar quality, CAS prefers the least complex option that fully satisfies the objective.

# Primary Selection Distinctions

## Push Press

Select when the main objective is to transmit force from the legs through the trunk into an explosive overhead action.

It is the only exercise in this group whose final force expression is primarily an overhead press.

Its principal distinction is not merely vertical power, but vertical power expressed through the upper limbs.

---

## Hang High Pull

Select when the objective is aggressive triple extension followed by continued upward bar displacement, without the technical demands of receiving the bar.

It provides greater upper-body pulling continuation than the Jump Shrug while remaining less complex than the Hang Power Clean.

---

## Hang Power Clean

Select when the objective includes both explosive propulsion and rapid reception of the bar.

It offers the broadest coordination demand of the four exercises because the athlete must accelerate, redirect and stabilize the load.

It should not be selected merely because it appears more advanced.

---

## Jump Shrug

Select when the objective is rapid lower-body force production and triple-extension timing with the lowest receiving and turnover demands.

It is the default weightlifting derivative when CAS requires loaded vertical power but technical simplicity is a priority.

---

# Force-Pathway Comparison

## Lower-to-Upper Propulsive Transfer

Primary option:

Push Press

The athlete produces force through the legs, transfers it through a rigid trunk and expresses it through an overhead pressing action.

This makes the Push Press the strongest choice when the target adaptation includes upper-body propulsive power.

---

## Lower-Body Triple-Extension Transfer

Primary options:

- Jump Shrug
- Hang High Pull
- Hang Power Clean

All three begin from a hang position and rely on forceful extension of the hips, knees and ankles.

The main distinction is what occurs after peak extension:

- Jump Shrug: the athlete finishes tall with natural shoulder elevation.
- Hang High Pull: the elbows rise to continue the vertical bar path.
- Hang Power Clean: the athlete moves under the bar and receives it on the shoulders.

---

# Technical-Complexity Continuum

From lowest to highest general technical demand:

1. Jump Shrug
2. Push Press
3. Hang High Pull
4. Hang Power Clean

This order is not absolute.

An athlete with poor overhead mobility may find the Push Press less appropriate than a Hang High Pull.

An athlete with extensive weightlifting experience may perform the Hang Power Clean more reliably than another athlete performs the Hang High Pull.

CAS therefore evaluates technical competence individually rather than assigning complexity only from the exercise name.

---

# Receiving-Demand Comparison

## No Bar Reception

- Jump Shrug
- Hang High Pull

These are preferred when:

- front-rack mobility is insufficient,
- turnover skill is undeveloped,
- wrist or shoulder tolerance limits catching,
- fatigue makes receiving quality unreliable,
- or the objective does not require force absorption in a catch.

---

## Overhead Stabilization Without Re-Bend

Push Press

The athlete must stabilize the bar overhead but does not move under it into a receiving squat.

The main requirements are shoulder stability, trunk control and a stacked overhead position.

---

## Dynamic Front-Rack Reception

Hang Power Clean

The athlete must rapidly transition from extension into a partial-squat catch.

This creates additional demands for:

- front-rack mobility,
- turnover speed,
- force absorption,
- trunk posture,
- foot repositioning,
- and technical confidence.

---

# Upper-Body Contribution Comparison

## Highest Pressing Contribution

Push Press

The arms and shoulders continue bar acceleration after leg drive and stabilize the final overhead position.

---

## Highest Pulling Continuation

Hang High Pull

The upper body continues the bar path after lower-body extension through elbow elevation.

The arms must not initiate the movement prematurely.

---

## Highest Turnover Contribution

Hang Power Clean

The upper limbs redirect the elbows around and under the bar rather than continuing to pull it upward indefinitely.

---

## Lowest Active Arm Contribution

Jump Shrug

The arms remain long and primarily transmit force from the body to the bar.

Shoulder elevation occurs only after the lower body has completed the main acceleration.

---

# Exercise-by-Exercise Selection Profile

## Push Press

### Select When

- upper-body propulsive power is a primary objective,
- lower-to-upper force transfer is required,
- the athlete has adequate overhead mobility and stability,
- a ballistic press is more relevant than a pull,
- the athlete can maintain a vertical dip and stable trunk,
- and the load can be accelerated without grinding.

### Avoid or Regress When

- overhead pain is present,
- lumbar extension dominates the finish,
- the athlete cannot maintain front-rack control,
- the dip becomes excessively deep or unstable,
- the movement turns into a slow Strict Press,
- or fatigue causes repeated loss of overhead alignment.

### Closest Alternatives

- Push Jerk when receiving skill is appropriate and a higher load is needed,
- Landmine Push Press when overhead range is limited,
- Medicine-Ball Chest Throw when external loading should be reduced,
- Jump Shrug when the target shifts toward lower-body vertical power.

---

## Hang High Pull

### Select When

- vertical triple-extension power is required,
- the athlete can hinge and accelerate a bar safely,
- a stronger pulling continuation is desired,
- front-rack reception is unnecessary or contraindicated,
- the athlete can delay elbow flexion,
- and the bar path remains close and vertical.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the movement becomes an upright row,
- the athlete bends the arms before extension,
- the bar loops away from the body,
- lumbar hyperextension replaces hip extension,
- the athlete jumps forward repeatedly,
- or technical rhythm deteriorates under load.

### Closest Alternatives

- Jump Shrug for lower technical demand,
- Hang Power Clean when reception is specifically required,
- Clean Pull when heavier force-dominant loading is desired,
- Kettlebell Swing when equipment or technical constraints limit barbell use.

---

## Hang Power Clean

### Select When

- whole-body power and rapid force absorption are both objectives,
- the athlete has proven clean technique,
- front-rack mobility is sufficient,
- turnover and receiving skill are stable,
- the athlete can catch above a deep squat with balance,
- and the technical cost is justified by the session objective.

### Avoid or Regress When

- front-rack pain or mobility restriction is present,
- the athlete catches on straight legs,
- the elbows remain low,
- the bar crashes onto the shoulders,
- the athlete jumps forward or loops the bar,
- the pull becomes arm-dominant,
- or fatigue materially reduces catch quality.

### Closest Alternatives

- Hang High Pull when reception should be removed,
- Jump Shrug when technical simplicity is required,
- Clean Pull when heavier force production is prioritized,
- Medicine-Ball Scoop Throw when ballistic intent is required with less technical complexity.

---

## Jump Shrug

### Select When

- loaded vertical power is required,
- the athlete is learning triple extension,
- technical simplicity is important,
- front-rack mobility is insufficient,
- a high-velocity derivative is needed without turnover,
- or the athlete is not ready for the Hang High Pull or Hang Power Clean.

### Avoid or Regress When

- the athlete cannot land under control,
- the elbows bend early,
- the bar moves away from the body,
- the athlete repeatedly jumps forward,
- load selection suppresses take-off speed,
- grip fatigue limits performance,
- or the exercise becomes a slow shrug.

### Closest Alternatives

- Hang High Pull for greater bar displacement,
- Hang Power Clean for reception and force absorption,
- Loaded Jump Squat for lower-body propulsion without a pulling derivative,
- Kettlebell Swing for simpler hip-dominant power.

---

# Objective-Based Selection Matrix

| Primary Objective | Preferred Exercise | Secondary Option | Main Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper-body propulsive power | Push Press | Landmine Push Press | Expresses leg-generated force through an overhead press |
| Lower-body vertical power with minimal complexity | Jump Shrug | Loaded Jump Squat | Preserves triple extension without turnover or catch |
| Triple-extension power with active vertical pull | Hang High Pull | Jump Shrug | Adds pulling continuation after extension |
| Power plus dynamic force absorption | Hang Power Clean | Hang High Pull | Includes rapid pull-under and front-rack reception |
| Teaching Olympic-lift propulsion | Jump Shrug | Hang High Pull | Simplifies the movement while reinforcing extension timing |
| Avoiding front-rack stress | Jump Shrug or Hang High Pull | Kettlebell Swing | Removes the catch |
| Combat-relevant lower-to-upper force transfer | Push Press | Medicine-Ball Throw | Trains force transmission into the upper limbs |
| Highest coordination challenge | Hang Power Clean | Hang High Pull | Combines propulsion, turnover, reception and stabilization |

# Readiness-Based Selection

## Low Technical Readiness

Preferred options:

- Jump Shrug
- Push Press only when overhead mechanics are already competent

Avoid default selection of the Hang Power Clean.

The objective is to preserve power intent without allowing technical complexity to dominate the session.

---

## Moderate Technical Readiness

Preferred options:

- Push Press
- Jump Shrug
- Hang High Pull

The Hang High Pull may be introduced when the athlete can maintain long arms during extension and a close bar path.

---

## High Technical Readiness

Available options:

- Push Press
- Hang High Pull
- Hang Power Clean
- Jump Shrug

High readiness does not automatically justify the Hang Power Clean.

CAS still selects according to the adaptation target, recovery cost and movement constraints.

---

# Mobility-Based Selection

## Limited Front-Rack Mobility

Prefer:

- Jump Shrug
- Hang High Pull

Use the Push Press only if a safe rack and overhead position remain available.

Avoid the Hang Power Clean until the receiving position is adequate.

---

## Limited Overhead Mobility

Prefer:

- Jump Shrug
- Hang High Pull
- Hang Power Clean when front-rack mechanics are adequate

Avoid the Push Press if compensation is required to reach lockout.

---

## Limited Hip-Hinge Competence

Prefer:

- Push Press

Regress the pulling derivatives until the athlete can establish a stable hang position.

---

# Fatigue and Recovery Comparison

## Lowest Relative Technical Fatigue

Jump Shrug

The exercise has no turnover or catch, but power quality still falls rapidly when repeated under fatigue.

---

## Moderate Technical and Neurological Demand

- Push Press
- Hang High Pull

Both require precise sequencing and should end before bar speed or position deteriorates.

---

## Highest Technical Demand

Hang Power Clean

The reception phase makes fatigue-related errors more consequential.

CAS reduces volume or substitutes a pull-only derivative when readiness is insufficient.

---

# Recovery-Cost Comparison

Typical relative recovery cost when volume and loading are appropriate:

1. Jump Shrug — Low to Moderate
2. Push Press — Moderate
3. Hang High Pull — Moderate
4. Hang Power Clean — Moderate to High

Actual recovery cost changes with:

- load,
- number of repetitions,
- total contacts or catches,
- athlete proficiency,
- eccentric lowering strategy,
- grip demand,
- and proximity to technical failure.

A poorly executed light exercise may create more fatigue than a technically efficient heavier exercise.

---

# Programming Comparison

| Exercise | Typical Repetition Range | Typical Set Range | Rest Requirement | Primary Stop Rule |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Push Press | 2–5 | 3–6 | 2–4 min | Stop when bar speed or overhead position deteriorates |
| Hang High Pull | 2–4 | 3–6 | 2–4 min | Stop when extension timing or bar path deteriorates |
| Hang Power Clean | 1–3 | 3–6 | 2–5 min | Stop when turnover or catch quality deteriorates |
| Jump Shrug | 2–5 | 3–6 | 2–4 min | Stop when take-off speed, landing or arm position deteriorates |

These ranges are defaults rather than mandatory prescriptions.

CAS adjusts them according to athlete level, load, session objective and readiness.

# Combat-Sport Transfer Comparison

## Highest Upper-Limb Force-Transfer Relevance

Push Press

It reinforces the sequence of generating force from the floor and expressing it through the upper body.

This is relevant to striking actions at a general physical-preparation level, but it is not a technical striking drill.

---

## Highest General Hip-Extension Power Relevance

- Jump Shrug
- Hang High Pull
- Hang Power Clean

All three train rapid extension through the lower body and hips.

Their combat value is general rather than movement-specific.

---

## Highest Force-Absorption and Reorganization Demand

Hang Power Clean

The athlete must rapidly transition from propulsion into a stable receiving posture.

This may support general athletic reorganization but should not be confused with sport-specific defensive or stance mechanics.

---

## Highest Accessibility for Non-Weightlifters

Jump Shrug

It offers a strong loaded-power stimulus with the fewest technical barriers among the pulling derivatives.

# Substitution Logic

## Push Press Substitutions

Use Jump Shrug when:

- overhead pressing is not tolerated,
- the target shifts toward lower-body power,
- or shoulder fatigue must be reduced.

Use Landmine Push Press when:

- overhead range is limited,
- but lower-to-upper propulsive transfer remains desirable.

Use Medicine-Ball Throw when:

- ballistic upper-body intent is required with lower external load.

---

## Hang High Pull Substitutions

Use Jump Shrug when:

- elbow timing is poor,
- technical demand must be reduced,
- or the athlete begins pulling with the arms.

Use Hang Power Clean when:

- receiving skill is competent,
- and force absorption is part of the objective.

Use Clean Pull when:

- heavier force expression is prioritized over maximal velocity.

---

## Hang Power Clean Substitutions

Use Hang High Pull when:

- the athlete can produce propulsion but cannot receive the bar safely.

Use Jump Shrug when:

- both turnover and reception should be removed.

Use Front Squat plus Jump Shrug as separate exercises when:

- front-rack strength and explosive extension should be trained independently.

---

## Jump Shrug Substitutions

Use Hang High Pull when:

- the athlete is ready for greater bar displacement and active pulling continuation.

Use Loaded Jump Squat when:

- a pulling derivative is unnecessary,
- and the desired adaptation is primarily lower-body vertical propulsion.

Use Kettlebell Swing when:

- equipment, coaching or technical constraints limit barbell derivatives.

---

# Conflict Rules

CAS does not select a power exercise when:

- the athlete cannot perform it without pain,
- the required mobility is unavailable,
- the technical skill is below the minimum safe threshold,
- current fatigue prevents high-velocity execution,
- the exercise duplicates a more important high-intensity stressor,
- or the remaining session time is insufficient for adequate rest.

CAS avoids selecting multiple highly similar weightlifting derivatives in the same session unless a deliberate teaching progression is being used.

Examples of unnecessary redundancy include:

- Jump Shrug plus Hang High Pull plus Hang Power Clean as full-volume exercises,
- Hang High Pull plus Hang Power Clean when both target the same adaptation and fatigue budget is limited,
- or Push Press plus heavy overhead pressing when shoulder stress is already high.

When technical learning is the objective, CAS may sequence:

1. Jump Shrug
2. Hang High Pull
3. Hang Power Clean

The volume of each stage must remain low enough to preserve quality.

---

# Engine Selection Hierarchy

The CAS Engine should apply the following hierarchy:

1. Confirm that loaded power is appropriate for the session objective.
2. Identify whether the required expression is overhead propulsion or vertical pulling power.
3. Determine whether dynamic bar reception is necessary.
4. Check technical competence and movement familiarity.
5. Check front-rack, overhead and hip-hinge mobility.
6. Check current readiness and fatigue.
7. Select the least complex exercise that fully satisfies the objective.
8. Assign a load that permits maximal acceleration intent.
9. Limit repetitions before meaningful velocity loss.
10. Record the selection rationale in the Decision Trace.

---

# Minimum Decision Trace Requirements

When selecting one of these exercises, the Decision Trace should record:

- target power quality,
- selected force pathway,
- reason for selecting this exercise over the closest alternative,
- technical-readiness status,
- mobility constraints,
- reception requirement or exclusion,
- intended loading profile,
- repetition and rest logic,
- stop rule,
- and any relevant substitution trigger.

Example:

> Jump Shrug selected to train loaded vertical power and triple-extension velocity. Hang Power Clean excluded because front-rack reception is not yet technically reliable. Hang High Pull considered but rejected to minimize arm-action complexity. Prescribed for low repetitions with full rest and termination at visible velocity loss.

---

# Decision Summary

Select Push Press when the athlete must express lower-body-generated force through an explosive overhead press.

Select Hang High Pull when the athlete requires triple-extension power with continued vertical pulling but no receiving phase.

Select Hang Power Clean when both explosive propulsion and dynamic front-rack reception are justified by the objective and athlete competence.

Select Jump Shrug when loaded vertical power and triple-extension timing are required with the lowest technical and mobility barriers.

The most advanced exercise is not automatically the best exercise.

The best exercise is the one that produces the intended power adaptation with the highest movement quality, lowest unnecessary complexity and acceptable recovery cost.
